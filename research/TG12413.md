# The EMI TG12413 limiter/compressor: research notes for the second diode unit in `noob-compressorlab`

Research dossier for the EMI TG12413 model of the `noob-compressorlab` example plug-in of
noob-vst-webgui-framework. The example is a humorous, affectionate spoof of the EMI TG12413
compressor/limiter, the dynamics module of the Abbey Road TG consoles. It is not a product, it is not endorsed
by anybody, and it does not use the EMI, Abbey Road, TG, TG12345 or TG12413 names as its own name. Those are
trademarks of EMI (IP) Limited, as Chandler Limited state on every page of theirs I read [8] [9] [10], and they
are used below only to identify the devices and the products discussed. This model sits behind the same
per-instance `model` switch that already selects the 1176, LA-2A, LA-3A, CL 1B, Distressor, 6176 and Neve
behaviours; see [[1176]], [[610]], [[LA-2A]], [[LA-3A]], [[CL-1B]], [[Distressor]] and [[Neve-33609]].

Conventions, kept the same as the other dossiers so they all read alike:

- Citations are `[n]`; the numbered list in section 13 gives the URL for every source, and reference-style link
  definitions at the very end make the `[n]` markers clickable.
- Numbers that come from a manufacturer specification, a manual, a service handbook, a schematic or a
  measurement are attributed. Numbers that are my own derivation or assumption are labelled **estimate** or
  **derived**. Nothing labelled a measurement was invented.
- "GR" is gain reduction. "CV" is the DC control voltage. "THD" is total harmonic distortion. "AOT" is EMI's
  own abbreviation, printed on the drawing, for **adjust on test** — a component whose value is chosen during
  factory alignment rather than specified in advance.
- Component designators are given exactly as EMI's drawing gives them, and I say which sheet each one comes
  from. The TG12413 sheet and the TG12414 sheet both have an R16 and they are different resistors.
- This is a spoof target, not a parity goal. I want the feel: one switch that says COMPRESS, OUT and LIMIT, one
  switch that says RECOVERY 1 to 6 with no times printed on it, an output trim in exact one-decibel steps, and
  a gain element that behaves nothing like the one I built last week. I am not trying to beat Waves, who
  designed their version jointly with Abbey Road and had the console [11] [12].

**The one thing to carry away before reading any of it.** I was sent here to test an argument. Having modelled
the Neve 2254 and 33609, I published their diode bridge as its own component crate on the reasoning that "the
part is one thing with one law while the divider, the sidechains and the shaping network differ between units".
The TG12413 is the obvious second customer for that crate, and **it is not one**. Section 4 is the whole
answer, and it comes down to two facts I read off EMI's own drawing at sixteen times magnification. First, the
TG's four diodes are **not arranged as a ring**: they are two branches of two diodes in series, both branches
pointing the same way, which is a different circuit from Neve's opposed-pair ring no matter what either one is
called. Second, and worse for the crate, **all four have their cathodes toward the supply rail and their anodes
toward transistors that sink current down through them**, which as drawn means they conduct in *reverse
breakdown*, not forward. And that reading is not mine alone: EMI's own limiter lineage runs through a product
called the **RS168 Zener Limiter** [9], and Waves, who built their model with Abbey Road and had the hardware,
describe the TG12413's gain element as "the unit's original **Zener diode** limiter" three separate times in
their user guide [12]. A forward-biased diode bridge and a zener in breakdown are not the same part and do not
obey the same law. **The component crate does not serve both units.** Section 4.9 says exactly what does
generalise and what the crate should have been called.

**A second thing, because it is the more useful half of the same finding.** Even under the most generous
reading — if the draughtsman drew the diodes backwards and they are forward-biased after all — the TG still
would not take the crate's law unchanged, because there are **two junctions per arm instead of one**. I derive
that case in 4.6 and it gives `i = I·tanh(u / 4ηV_T)` against the Neve's `i = I·tanh(u / 2ηV_T)`: the same
function with the thermal scale **doubled**, which at equal drive is four times less third harmonic. So the
best case for the crate is "the same law with a different constant, provided the constant was ever a
parameter", and the case the drawing actually supports is "a different law". Both conclusions point the same
way: what generalises is a *shape of circuit*, not a *component*.

## 1. What this unit is, and the family

### 1.0 Where the documents are, and how I found them

The evidence base here is **much thinner than the Neve's and I want that on the record before anything else**.
For the Neve I had a factory technical handbook with a specification section carrying per-ratio tolerances and
a stated measurement procedure [19]. For the TG12413 there is no service manual, no specification sheet, no
alignment procedure and no published measurement of any kind that I could reach. What there is, is one
genuinely excellent primary document and three manufacturers talking about it.

- **The primary document is a single Internet Archive item, `JL11440`, titled "EMI TG Schematics"** [1]. It is a
  17-page, 17 MB scan of photographed EMI drawings. I enumerated it through the archive's metadata endpoint
  [2], pulled its OCR text layer [3] to find which page was which, and then rendered the pages I needed at
  their full 4229 × 2730 native resolution [4]. The OCR is close to useless as prose — the scan is of
  hand-lettered blueprints and Tesseract guessed the script was Cyrillic — but it is *searchable*, and it was
  enough to locate "TG 12413 B 205A", "4xHS2051", "RECOVERY" and the resistor ladders on page index 5.
- **Page index 5 is the circuit diagram, EMI drawing `TG12413-D101`, titled COMPRESSOR/LIMITER** [5]. It is one
  large sheet carrying the whole module: audio path, gain element, both sidechain modes, all three switch
  assemblies with their resistor ladders drawn out component by component, the output amplifier and the supply
  droppers. It is legible. Every component value quoted in sections 3 to 7 below was read directly off it at
  between three and sixteen times magnification, and I say which crop each reading came from where it matters.
- **Page index 6 is the board component assembly, `TG12413-C301A` / `C301D`** [6], which carries a note that
  independently confirms the diode pairing: "NOTE:— D1 & D3 MATCHED PAIR, D2 & D4 do, VT13 & VT14 …".
- The rest of the item is the sibling modules: **TG12414 filter** on indices 2 to 4, **TG12412 tone control** on
  7 to 11, and **TG12411 input** on 12 to 16 [1]. Those matter for one reason only, and it is an important one
  — see 1.3.

Everything else is manufacturer copy: Chandler Limited's three product pages [8] [9] [10] and Waves' product
page [11] and 24-page user guide [12]. The user guide had to be fetched with a cookie jar, because
`waves.com` sits behind Incapsula and a plain `curl` of the PDF path returns a 302 to itself forever.

**What I could not reach, said plainly, is in 8.1 and 2.9.** The short version: no EMI service manual, no EMI
specification, no photograph of a bare TG12413 module face, and no measurement by anybody.

### 1.1 EMI, Central Research Laboratories, and where the TG came from

Chandler Limited's history text, which they publish on the TG Opto page and which is the most specific account
I could reach, dates the project precisely:

> Conceived from meetings in **1967** between Abbey Road engineers and EMI's **Central Research Laboratories**
> technical team, the EMI TG12345 desk (later known as the Mark I), would usher in a sea change in sound and
> flexibility at Abbey Road Studios. [10]

Two things in that sentence are worth pausing on. The first is that this is a **research-laboratory** design
rather than a console company's product: EMI's CRL at Hayes was a corporate research establishment, and the
desk was built for one customer, EMI's own studios. The second is the date. 1967 for the meetings, 1968 for the
installation, which puts the TG's design work in the same two years as Rupert Neve's 2253 and 2254 in Melbourn
[[Neve-33609]]. **These are contemporaries, not a design and its successor**, and that matters for section 4:
two British engineering teams, forty miles apart, both reaching for diodes as a gain element in 1968, and
arriving at two different circuits.

Chandler give the limiter its own lineage, and this is the single most useful sentence anybody has published
about the part:

> It continues the tradition of EMI Limiters started in **1954 with the RS114 tube limiter** and the **1968
> RS168 Zener Limiter**. The Zener Limiters were also part of the **1969 TG12345 console channel** and the
> **1974 TG12413**. [9]

**Hold on to the words "Zener Limiter".** They are Chandler's, on a page about a product they build under
licence from EMI, and they are the earliest hint that the TG's gain element is not what a reader coming from
the Neve would assume. Section 4 turns that hint into a circuit reading.

I have not verified 1954, 1968 or 1974 from a dated primary document. The only dated primary evidence I hold is
the drawing itself, and **the TG12413-D101 sheet carries no legible date**: the title block sits at the very
bottom edge of the photographed page and only the words "COMPRESSOR/LIMITER" and the drawing number survive the
crop [5]. So the dates in this section are Chandler's word, from a marketing page, and they are cited as such.

### 1.2 The TG12345 console

Wikipedia's article is short, sourced, and consistent with Chandler on every point where they overlap [13]:

- The TG12345 was **designed by EMI for Abbey Road** and was **installed in Studio Two in late 1968**, replacing
  the valve REDD.51.
- It had **24 microphone inputs and 8 tape outputs**, against the REDD.51's eight and four, which is what let
  the studio move from the four-track Studer J37 to the eight-track 3M M23.
- It was **Abbey Road's first solid-state console**, and "unlike its predecessors that were based on vacuum
  tubes, it featured a **compressor as well as audio equalization built into each channel**".
- *Abbey Road* was recorded and mixed on it; so were all four Beatles' first solo albums and Pink Floyd's *The
  Dark Side of the Moon*.
- It was **decommissioned in 1972**, dismantled into the attic of Studio Three, rescued by Mike Hedges in 1988,
  restored over four years from 2018, and **auctioned in October 2024 for £1,750,000** [13] [14].

Chandler add the detail that the desk was "modular in design, with **twelve dual channel microphone cassettes**"
and that it debuted "on an 8-track recording by the Shadows" in **November 1968**, and that the TG desks in
Marks I to IV "became the main recording console used throughout the studios until **1983**" [10]. They also
record what the compressor was *for*:

> The compressor was originally supposed to replicate the sound and behavior of the **Fairchild 660 and RS124
> tube devices**, but ended up with a completely unique character. [10]

Chandler say the same thing on the TG1 page in slightly different words — "originally designed to be like the
Fairchild 660/670 which was loved by many EMI engineers including Geoff Emerick, but it ended up with its own
very special sound" [8]. **That is a design brief, not a measurement**, and I am citing it as the former. But it
is a useful one, because it tells us what EMI thought they were aiming at: a variable-mu tube limiter's
behaviour, reached with transistors and diodes. Nothing about the Neve was ever aimed at a Fairchild.

One more line from Chandler that governs how the spoof should be framed: "The EMI TG12345 recording consoles
were **never commercially available to the public**" [10], and "These rare pieces were never commercially
available, and only EMI owned studios had access to them" [8]. Unlike the 33609, which AMS Neve still build and
sell [[Neve-33609]], there has never been a TG12413 you could buy.

### 1.3 The TG12410 transfer console, and *which* TG12413 I actually read

This is the identification that makes the rest of the dossier possible, and it took a cross-check to establish,
so I am showing the working.

The archive item is titled only "EMI TG Schematics" and its sheets carry module numbers with no parent console
named [1]. The obvious assumption is that they are TG12345 recording-console modules. **They are not, or at
least not only.** Two independent lines of evidence put them in the **TG12410 Transfer Console**, EMI's
*mastering* desk:

1. **The TG12411 input unit carries a NAB/IEC equaliser.** The block on the left-hand board of `TG12411-D101`
   is captioned "NAB/IEC EQUALISER" and it reappears on the right-hand board `TG12411-D102` [7]. NAB and IEC
   are **tape replay** equalisation curves. A recording console's mic input has no business having them; a
   transfer console, whose job is to play a master tape and cut a lacquer, has every business.
2. **Waves' module numbering is identical, module for module.** Waves' plug-in is "modeled after the original
   **EMI TG12410 Transfer Console** used in all Abbey Road Studios' mastering suites from the early '70s to this
   day" [12], and its five modules are labelled, on screen, `TG12411 INPUT`, `TG12412 TONE`, `TG12413 LIMITER`,
   `TG12414 FILTER` and `TG12416 OUTPUT` [12] [17]. Four of those five numbers are exactly the four sheets in
   the archive item. And Waves' TG12411 module has a **TAPE EQUALIZER control with NAB, IEC and FLAT
   positions** [17], which is the block I just read off EMI's drawing.

So: **the TG12413-D101 sheet I read is the transfer-console limiter — the same module Waves modelled and the
one Chandler's TG1 is a recreation of.** That is the good outcome, because it means all three of my
manufacturer sources and my one primary source are talking about the same box.

The archive item's fifth module, `TG12417`, is a **fader** [1], which Waves' chain does not include, and Waves'
`TG12416` output/spreader is not in the archive item. So the item is not a complete TG12410 set and the two
sources overlap rather than coincide. I have not established whether the TG12345 channel's limiter is
electrically the same circuit as `TG12413-D101` or merely its ancestor; Chandler's chronology, which separates
"the 1969 TG12345 console channel" from "the 1974 TG12413" [9], suggests it is an ancestor. **I could not
establish that either way and I am not going to pretend otherwise.** Where this dossier says "the TG12413" it
means the circuit on drawing TG12413-D101, and nothing more.

### 1.4 The EMI limiter lineage

Assembled from Chandler's two history passages [9] [10]. Every date here is theirs.

| year | unit | element | what Chandler say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1954 | **RS114** | valve | "the RS114 tube limiter", the start of "the tradition of EMI Limiters" [9] |
| 1960s | **RS124** | valve | named as one of the units the TG compressor was meant to emulate [10] |
| 1968 | **RS168 Zener Limiter** | zener diode | named outright as a *Zener* Limiter [9] |
| 1969 | **TG12345 console channel** | — | "The Zener Limiters were also part of the 1969 TG12345 console channel" [9] |
| 1974 | **TG12413** | — | "and the 1974 TG12413" [9] |

Read that table as a whole and the naming is doing real work. EMI had a product line whose *name* was Zener
Limiter, and Chandler place the TG12345 channel limiter and the TG12413 inside that line rather than beside it.
Waves, independently, call the TG12413's element a Zener diode [12]. **Three sources, two companies, one word.**
Section 4 shows that the drawing agrees.

### 1.5 The Chandler recreations

Chandler Limited describe themselves as "the only company in the world authorized to develop, manufacture and
market the 'Official Equipment' of EMI/Abbey Road Studios" [10]. They ship four things descended from this
circuit:

- **TG1 Limiter** [8]. A two-channel 2U rack unit, "a recreation of the classic EMI TG12413 Limiter used in
  custom EMI and Abbey Road recording and mastering consoles from the late '60s". Its published control set is
  in 2.7 and it maps onto EMI's drawing almost switch for switch.
- **Zener Limiter** [9]. "The ultimate TG limiter issued in celebration of the 75th birthday of Abbey Road
  Studios … conceived by Chandler Limited designer **Wade Goeke** … This newest version borrows from the RS168
  Zener Limiter and TG12345 console strip". Wade Goeke has added "switchable input impedance for hard or soft
  driving of the unit, **11-position attack, 21-position release**, side chain filtering, and **Comp 1, Comp 2,
  and Limit** settings" [9]. Every one of those is an addition; the original has a fixed attack and six
  recovery positions.
- **TG Opto Compressor** [10], a 500-series module, "a relative of the TG1 Opto … traces its lineage to the
  historic EMI TG12413 limiters". This one has an **opto** gain element, so despite the name it is a different
  animal and is not evidence about the TG12413's circuit.
- **TG Channel MkII**, named in Chandler's own credit lists [9] but with no product page I could reach.

### 1.6 What genuinely differs, in one table

This is the table the brief asked for: original against recreations, and only differences I can point at a
source for.

| | **EMI TG12413** (drawing D101) [5] | **Chandler TG1** [8] | **Waves TG12413 module** [12] |
|---|---|---|---|
| format | console module, one channel | 2U rack, two channels | plug-in module in a five-module chain |
| input coupling | **capacitor, C1 4µ7 into R78 7K5** | **"Transformer balanced, custom wound"** | n/a |
| output coupling | **capacitor, C23 470 µF** | **transformer balanced, XLR pin 2 hot** | n/a |
| mode switch | S1: **COMPRESS / OUT / LIMIT** | COMP/LIMIT lever + separate OUT/IN toggle | ORIGINAL / **MODERN** / LIMIT |
| attack | **fixed**, no control | "Attack: Fixed" | fixed; guide says recovery position also moves it |
| recovery | S2: **six positions**, ladder 47K…1M3 | "Switched, **6 position**" | "**six recovery times** … Range 1–6, default 3" |
| output level | S3: **21 positions, 1 dB steps, −10 to +10 dB** | "Output Control: Switched" | MAKE UP, continuous, **−12 to +12 dB** |
| input level | none on the drawing | "Input Control: **Continuous**" | none |
| hold | **RV1 10K, internal preset** | **INPUT/HOLD**, front-panel knob | none |
| ratio | **none** | none | **RATIO 1–100**, Waves' own |
| dry/wet | none | none | **MIX**, Waves' own |
| sidechain filter | none on the drawing | none | **three linear-phase filters**, Waves' own |
| distortion mode | none | **THD switch**, "up to 2% THD" | none |
| stereo | **GANG bus**, tag 36, screened | STEREO / DUAL MONO switch | ST / DUO / MS |
| bypass | S1 centre position, **OUT** | "True bypass" | module on/off |
| metering | none on this drawing | two gain-reduction meters, 0 to +16 | vertical GR meter, +9 to −20 |

**The three differences that matter most**, and each one is a source-to-source contradiction rather than my
opinion:

1. **Transformers.** Chandler's TG1 has them at both ends and says so in its specification [8]. EMI's drawing
   has **none anywhere in the module** — the input arrives through C1, a 4µ7 electrolytic, and the output
   leaves through C23, a 470 µF electrolytic, into pins 16 and 17 [5]. If you A/B a TG1 against a real TG12413
   you are also A/B-ing two transformers against two capacitors. See 3.3.
2. **Ratio.** There is **no ratio control and no ratio ladder** anywhere on TG12413-D101. The Neve has a
   five-position ratio switch with a resistor ladder annotated INITIAL LAW and TOP END LAW [[Neve-33609]]; the
   TG has COMPRESS or LIMIT and nothing in between. Waves' RATIO 1–100 knob is explicitly "a ratio to the VCA
   input/output curve" [12], i.e. it belongs to their model, not to EMI's circuit.
3. **The modern mode.** Waves' MODERN is "a **VCA-based compressor**, newly designed … an original Waves and
   Abbey Road design" [12]. It is not a model of anything. Anyone comparing plug-ins should compare against
   ORIGINAL and LIMIT only.

### 1.7 Why it is famous

Because of what was recorded through it, and for no technical reason anybody has published. *Abbey Road*,
*Plastic Ono Band*, *McCartney*, *Sentimental Journey*, *All Things Must Pass*, *The Dark Side of the Moon*
[13]; and through the transfer console specifically, "from Pink Floyd's *Dark Side of the Moon* to Radiohead's
*OK Computer* and beyond" [12], because the TG12410 has been in "all Abbey Road Studios' mastering suites from
the early '70s **to this day**" [12]. That last clause is the interesting one: the recording desk was scrapped
in 1972 and sold at auction in 2024, but the mastering chain never left.

The honest framing for the spoof: this is the compressor that was on every channel of a desk that was on
records everyone knows, and which nobody outside EMI could buy. It is famous by association and by scarcity.
That is a good joke to build a faceplate around and a bad reason to expect it to measure well.

## 2. Controls, the front panel, and enough to draw a faceplate

**Read 2.9 before you draw anything.** The control *set* here is unusually solid, because EMI drew all three
switches out on the circuit diagram with every ladder resistor and every tag number [5]. The *panel* is
unusually weak, because I could not obtain a photograph of a bare TG12413 module face and I am not going to
invent one.

### 2.1 The control set, from EMI's own drawing

Everything in this subsection is read off TG12413-D101 [5]. There are exactly **three switches, one preset and
no continuous front-panel controls**.

**S1 — mode.** Drawn as assembly `TG12413 B205A`, with a legend table printed on the sheet in EMI's own hand:

| position | legend |
|---|---|
| 1 | **COMPRESS** |
| 2 | **OUT** |
| 3 | **LIMIT** |

Three positions, with OUT in the middle. That is the whole mode control and there is no separate in/out switch.
Note the ordering: to get from compress to limit you pass through out, which is a real ergonomic fact and worth
reproducing.

**S2 — recovery.** Drawn as assembly `TG12413 B204A`, labelled **RECOVERY** on the sheet, with a six-resistor
series ladder and switch tags 11 and 13 to 18. Values, read off the drawing left to right up the chain:

| ladder position | resistor | value |
|---|---|---|
| bottom, from tag 11 | R1 | **47 K** |
| | R2 | **47 K** |
| | R3 | **130 K** |
| | R4 | **220 K** |
| | R5 | **470 K** |
| top | R6 | **1 M3** |

**No times are printed on the drawing and no times are printed anywhere else either.** The switch is marked
1 to 6 on Chandler's recreation [18] and 1 to 6 in Waves' plug-in [12]. Section 7 does the arithmetic.

**S3 — output level.** Drawn as assembly `TG12413 B203A`, labelled **OUTPUT LEVEL** on the sheet, with a
twenty-one-resistor ladder, tags 1 to 22 plus a common at 25, and its own legend table:

| position | dB |
|---|---|
| 1 | −10 |
| 11 | 0 |
| 21 | +10 |
| | in **1 dB STEPS** |

Values, read off the drawing from tag 1 upward: R1 3K3, R2 3K3, R3 2K7, R4 2K4, R5 2K2, R6 2K, R7 1K8, R8 1K6,
R9 1K3, R10 1K3, R11 1K1, R12 1K, R13 910, R14 820, R15 680, R16 620, R17 510, R18 510, R19 430, R20 330,
R21 3K3. Section 3.4 shows that these twenty-one values reproduce the printed one-decibel step to within a
tenth of a decibel, which is the best calibration anchor in this entire document.

**RV1 — HOLD.** A **10 kΩ preset**, labelled `RV1 10K HOLD` on the sheet, sitting in series with the recovery
ladder's return through terminals BU and BN, with **D12, an OA202 germanium diode**, alongside it [5]. On EMI's
module this is an internal adjustment, not a panel control. Chandler promote it to the front panel and gang it
with their input control, marking the knob **INPUT/HOLD** [18].

**And that is all.** No threshold, no ratio, no attack, no make-up beyond S3, no meter on this sheet, no
sidechain filter, no link switch. The link is a **wired bus**, not a control — see 5.7.

### 2.2 What the numbers on the panel actually are

Two of the three switches have numbers, and they are of completely different kinds. This is worth stating
because it decides how the faceplate reads.

- **OUTPUT LEVEL is calibrated.** −10 to +10 in one-decibel steps, and the resistor ladder really does deliver
  one decibel per step (3.4). The number on the panel is a decibel.
- **RECOVERY is not calibrated.** It is 1 to 6. There is no time on the panel, no time on the drawing, and
  Waves — who had the hardware and built the model with Abbey Road — say in their user guide that the six
  recovery times "are **very hard to put in terms of exact milliseconds**. Think of them in the following
  terms: the higher the recovery state, the longer the attack/release time. You will need to experiment in
  order to hear the exact difference" [12].

That contrast is the personality of the panel and the spoof should keep it. The Neve's ratio knob lies to you
by printing ratios that are not the ratios [[Neve-33609]]; the TG's recovery knob does not lie, it simply
declines to say.

### 2.3 The switch hardware, from the assembly numbers

The three switches are separate part-numbered assemblies, which tells us they are real rotary switch decks with
their ladders built onto them rather than pots [5]:

| control | assembly | positions | tags on the drawing |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 mode | `TG12413 B205A` | 3 | wired to M, N, R, S, T, and 25–29 with wire colours BK, W, BN, GY, GN, O |
| S2 recovery | `TG12413 B204A` | 6 | 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, (18), common 26; wires BU, BN, BK, RED |
| S3 output level | `TG12413 B203A` | 21 | 1 to 22, common 25 |

The drawing colour-codes the loom, which is unusually helpful and is the kind of detail worth keeping in the
notes even though it changes nothing about the model: **BU** blue, **BN** brown, **BK** black, **GY** grey,
**GN** green, **O** orange, **W** white, **RED** red [5].

### 2.4 The adjust-on-test resistors, and what they say about the unit

Ten components on this sheet are marked **A.O.T.** — adjust on test — numbered 1 to 10 [5]. That is a lot, and
where they sit is informative:

| AOT | where it sits | what I read it as doing |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | in VT20's emitter, with C18 1µ | timing / recovery trim |
| 3, 4, 5, 6 | a 2 × 3 block with R31 2K and R32 820 in the sidechain | **threshold and law calibration** |
| 7, 9 | in the input amplifier's feedback network with R1 20K, R4 56K, R8 1K5 | input stage gain trim |
| **8, 10** | **in parallel, across the top of the right-hand diode branch** | **gain-element balance** |

**AOT 8 and AOT 10 are the interesting pair**, and 4.2 comes back to them. They sit opposite **R16, a fixed
24 Ω**, on the other branch. Two selected-on-test resistors on one side of a matched-diode network and a fixed
24 Ω on the other is exactly the arrangement you build when the thing you are trimming is the **balance between
two branches that must carry the same current**. It is the strongest circumstantial evidence on the sheet that
the four diodes are meant to be a *balanced* pair of arms, and it explains why EMI bothered to specify D1/D3
and D2/D4 as matched pairs twice, once on each drawing [5] [6].

Ten AOTs also says something about manufacture: this was a module built in small numbers by a research
laboratory for one customer, aligned by hand, with no expectation that two of them would be identical. **Unit
variation is a design feature here, not a defect**, and 11.3 gives the model a knob for it.

### 2.8 It is a console module, not a rack unit — how to present it

The TG12413 has **no front panel of its own**. It is a plug-in module in a console frame: on the recording desk
it is part of one of twelve dual-channel "microphone cassettes" [10], and on the transfer console it is one of
five strips in a rack of modules [12]. Its connections on drawing D101 are numbered **tags**, not connectors —
IN on tag 5, OUT on 16 and 17, 0 V on 15 and 19, +28 V on 25, −28 V on 18, GANG on 36 — and the whole thing
lands on an edge connector [5].

So there is a presentation decision to make, and the framework's rule settles it: **the framework stays
uncoloured and headless, and every face and colour belongs to the example plug-in** [[feedback-framework-vs-plugin]].
My recommendation for the example:

- **Draw it as a vertical module strip, not a 19-inch rack face.** Roughly 1 : 5 width to height. That is what
  it is, it is visually distinct from every other model in the lab, and it is honest.
- **Charcoal-to-slate field with white silkscreen**, taking the field somewhere between the console
  photographs' `#656969` and Waves' `#47647C` and saying in the notes that the two sources disagree.
- **Head the strip with a wordmark that is obviously not EMI's.** The console silkscreens `EMI` at the top of
  every strip [15]; the spoof must not.
- **Three controls and nothing else**: a three-position mode switch reading COMPRESS / OUT / LIMIT, a six-position
  RECOVERY switch numbered 1 to 6 **with no times printed**, and a 21-position OUTPUT LEVEL switch marked −10
  to +10. Put HOLD on a back panel or a shift-click, because on the hardware it is a screwdriver adjustment.
- **Stepped controls that look and feel stepped.** Everything on this module is a switch; nothing is continuous.
- **A gain-reduction meter is optional and would be an invention.** Drawing D101 has no meter. Chandler and
  Waves both add one because a modern user expects one [12] [18]. If the example adds one, say so on the page.

## 3. Signal path and circuit, read from TG12413-D101

### 3.1 The sheet, and how I read it

One drawing, `TG12413-D101`, titled **COMPRESSOR/LIMITER**, photographed at 4229 × 2730 [5]. It is a single
large sheet — audio path along the top, sidechain along the bottom, the three switch assemblies drawn out at
the left, bottom and right, and the supply droppers at the right-hand end. Component values are hand-lettered
and mostly crisp. A previous owner has annotated the print in pen with DC voltages (`−13.3 V` at VT7,
`−18.8` at VT22, `−6.7` at VT27, `23 V` across the output zener pair) and the words `DC feed`, `general
purpose` and `Ref: Note 2.3`; **those are somebody's service notes, not EMI's, and I have not used them as
evidence** beyond noting that they exist.

The tolerance key at the bottom right marks components with `Ø` for ±1 %, `#` for ±2 % and `※` for ±5 % [5].
Nearly every resistor in the signal and control paths carries `Ø` or `#`, which for 1969 is a close-tolerance
build.

I read the sheet in nine magnified crops, and where a reading is marginal I say so. The crops that carry the
load are: the input stage and gain element at 3.3×, the four diodes at 8× and again thresholded at 16×, the
network above the diodes at 8× and again at 5×, the sidechain in two halves at 1.4×, the recovery ladder at
2.8×, and the output-level ladder at 2.2×.

### 3.2 The audio path, stage by stage

Reading left to right along the top of the sheet [5]. Where a component's role is my inference rather than the
drawing's statement, I say so.

**Input.** Tag **5**, marked `IN`, through **R78 7K5** in series, then **C1 4µ7** in series into the first
transistor's base. **R79 3K9** shunts the junction of R78 and C1 to the 0 V rail. **R80 20K** with **C24 150p**
sits across R78, and **C25 220p** across the C1 node. Tags 9 and 10 are a second input pair, and tag 34 arrives
on a separate line from the left.

**So the module's input is capacitor-coupled and single-ended, through a 4.7 µF electrolytic.** That is the
first structural difference from the Neve, which arrives through a Marinair transformer [[Neve-33609]].
**Derived:** C1 4µ7 into the following stage's input impedance sets a low-frequency corner; with the visible
7.5 kΩ series and 3.9 kΩ shunt the corner is of order **4 Hz**, so the input coupling is not a tone control.
The 150 pF and 220 pF are radio-frequency and stability, not audio.

**Input amplifier: VT1 (BCY71) and VT4 (BCY71).** Two transistors with a shared feedback and bias network:
**R1 20K, R4 56K, R5 56K, R8 1K5, R9 1K, R12 27K, R7 4K1** and **AOT 7** and **AOT 9**, with **C5 4µ7** to 0 V
[5]. **R15 22K** feeds this network's rail from +20 V. Two adjust-on-test resistors in a feedback network is a
gain trim, so I read this stage as **a fixed-gain amplifier aligned on test** rather than as anything
user-variable.

**The gain element.** **D1, D2, D3, D4 — four HS2051** — with **R16 24 Ω** and **AOT 8 ∥ AOT 10** across the
top and **R14 20K** feeding the bias from +20 V. Section 4 is entirely about these seven components and I will
not pre-empt it here beyond one fact: **the diodes sit between transistor stages, with no transformer on either
side.**

**Post-element amplifier: VT5, VT6, VT8, VT9, VT10, VT11 (BC109 and BCY71).** The bottom of each diode branch
goes to a transistor. Around them: **R19 3K9** and **R21 3K9** as emitter returns, **R22 7K5** and **R25 7K5**
from +20 V, **R23 3K3** and **R26 3K3** as a matched pair of collector or emitter resistors joined at their
lower ends, **C8 100p** and **C26 100p** for compensation, **R33 10K** and **R34 10K**, **R35 150**, **R27
12K**, and **C9 10µ** coupling onward. **A pair of 3K3 resistors joined at the bottom with a transistor beneath
them is a long-tailed pair with a tail source**, and I read VT8/VT10 with VT9 that way — **inferred**, not
stated on the drawing. **D5, an OA202 germanium diode**, and **C7 330 µF** sit on the 0 V side of this group.

**Output level.** **S3**, the twenty-one-position ladder, assembly `TG12413 B203A`, tags 1 to 22 and common 25
[5]. Section 3.4 works out what it does.

**Output amplifier.** **VT25 (BCY71)** and **VT26 (BC109)** as drivers, with **R65 51K, R66 27K, R67 100K,
R73 2K2**, **C19 200 µF**, **C20 10 µF**, **C21 33p**, **C22 2n7** and **D13, a BZY88 C5V1** 5.1 V zener; then
**VT29 (2N4898)** and **VT28 (2N4910)** as a **complementary output pair** with **R70 3K3, R74 36, R75 510,
R76 3K3, R77 56** and **R81 4K7**. The +28 V rail is dropped to the internal rail through **R77 56 Ω** and
regulated by **D14 (BZX61C11, 11 V)** and **D15 (BZX61C12, 12 V)** in series — the pen annotation on the print
reads `23 V`, which is 11 + 12 and is the only thing on the sheet those two zeners can be doing.

**Output.** Through **C23, 470 µF**, to terminal D and thence to tags **16** and **17**, marked `OUT`, with
0 V on tags **15** and **19** [5]. **Derived:** 470 µF into a 600 Ω load puts the output corner at about
**0.6 Hz**, so the output capacitor is transparent and is there only to block the amplifier's DC standing
voltage.

**Supplies.** **+28 V** on tag 25, **−28 V** on tag 18, **0 V** on 15 and 19, with internal **+20 V** and
**−20 V** rails derived on the sheet. Extra tags 20, 21, 22 and 23 are wired but their destinations are off the
drawing.

**Device count.** At least twenty-nine transistors, **VT1 to VT29**, all germanium-era silicon small-signal
types: **BCY71** and **BCY34** (PNP), **BC109** (NPN low-noise), **2N4898** and **2N4910** (the complementary
output pair). The board assembly sheet specifies "TRANSISTOR MOUNTING PAD TO 18-002 (JERMYN INDUSTRIES) FOR
VT1–VT26 (26 OFF)" and a TO-5 pad for VT27 [6].

### 3.3 There are no transformers anywhere in this module

I want this on its own, because it is a difference that a listener can hear and because a manufacturer's
specification contradicts it.

**Drawing TG12413-D101 shows no transformer of any kind** [5]. Not at the input, where C1 4µ7 does the
coupling; not at the output, where C23 470 µF does it; and not around the gain element, where the Neve puts two
[[Neve-33609]]. The module is transistor-coupled from tag 5 to tag 17 and every coupling is a capacitor.

**Chandler's TG1, by contrast, is transformer-balanced at both ends and custom-wound**, and that is in their
published specification, not in their marketing prose: "I/O — XLR (pin 2 hot), **Transformer balanced, custom
wound**" [8], and in the overview, "The Limiter/Compressor features an all discrete circuit with **transformer
balanced input and output**" [8].

Both statements can be true, and I think they are. Chandler build a **rack unit** that has to drive and receive
balanced lines over studio cabling; EMI built a **console module** whose input and output stayed inside the
frame on tags. Balancing transformers are what you add when you take a console module out of its console. But
the consequence for modelling is not cosmetic:

- A model of the **EMI module** should have **no transformer saturation, no transformer low-frequency
  asymmetry and no transformer high-frequency resonance**. If it has them, it is a model of a Chandler.
- The **Neve dossier's transformer work does not transfer here** [[Neve-33609]]. Where the 33609 model borrows
  the 610's transformer model from `dsp::pre` at four points, the TG borrows it at **none**.
- The colour that the TG does have has to come from somewhere else: the gain element, the sidechain's ripple,
  and a class-AB complementary output pair running from a ±20 V rail. That is a different palette.

**This is one of the three most important modelling decisions in the file** and it is decided by a primary
drawing against a manufacturer's specification for a different object, which is as clean as evidence gets here.

### 3.4 The output-level ladder is a virtual-earth input arm, and it checks out to a tenth of a decibel

**Derived**, from the twenty-one values in 2.1 [5]. This is the one place in the whole document where I can
check a reading of the circuit against a number EMI printed on the same sheet, so it does the work that the
33609 handbook's ratio table does for the Neve [[Neve-33609]].

Sum the ladder from a tap up to the top of the chain at tag 22 and you get the accumulated resistance at that
tap. Take the ratio to the value at tap 11, which EMI's legend calls 0 dB, and convert:

```
level(n) = −20 · log10( R_toTop(n) / R_toTop(11) )
```

**Every step is within 0.09 dB of one decibel except the last, which is 0.83 dB, and the total span is
19.76 dB against a nominal 20.** EMI's own legend table says positions 1, 11 and 21 are −10, 0 and +10 dB in

a decibel. That is not a coincidence, and it establishes three things at once:

1. **My reading of the twenty-one values is right**, because twenty-one independently misread values would not
   land on a geometric sequence.
2. **The ladder is the input arm of a virtual-earth stage**, gain = R_f / R_in, because that is the only
   topology in which the level goes as −20·log₁₀(R) exactly rather than as a divider's log(R/(R+R_L)).
3. **The sign convention runs the way EMI's table says**: more resistance in the arm, less output, so tap 1 is
   the quiet end.

**Test 1 in section 12 asserts this table.** It is the strongest structural test the model has.

### 3.5 What the drawing does not show, and what that costs

- **No level annotations.** The Neve's drawings print the signal level in dBu at each node — 0, −6, −25, −30,
  −8, 0 — and that chain of six numbers is what lets the 33609 model be calibrated against the manufacturer's
  own arithmetic [[Neve-33609]]. **EMI printed nothing of the kind on TG12413-D101** [5]. There is no stated
  input level, no stated output level, no stated level at the gain element. **This is the single biggest gap in
  the evidence base** and it is why section 11.6 makes the gain element's drive level a named calibratable
  constant rather than a derived one.
- **No time constants.** No attack, no recovery, no hold, in milliseconds or otherwise.
- **No threshold.** The threshold is set by the diode reference string and the AOT block (5.3, 5.4), not by a
  control, and no value in volts or dBu is printed.
- **No item list.** The sheet says "See Item List for Component Values etc." in four places [5] [6]; **the item
  list is not in the archive item** and I could not find it anywhere. That is where the HS2051's specification,
  the transistor grades and the AOT nominal values would be.
- **No title-block date.** Cropped off at the page edge (1.1).

## 4. The gain element, compared with the Neve's

This is the section the brief exists for. I was asked to test the argument behind publishing the Neve's diode
bridge as its own component crate — that "the part is one thing with one law while the divider, the sidechains
and the shaping network differ between units" — and to say plainly if the argument fails, because that would be
the more important finding. **It fails.** Here is the evidence, in the order I gathered it.

### 4.1 What the four diodes are

From TG12413-D101, at 8× magnification [5]. The gain element is drawn as four diodes in a compact block,
labelled **D1, D2, D3, D4**, with a single type annotation across them:

> **4 × HS2051**
> **MATCHED PAIR** ⟵ (arrows to D1 and D3)
> **MATCHED PAIR** ⟵ (arrows to D2 and D4)

The board component assembly sheet says the same thing independently, in EMI's shorthand: "NOTE:— **D1 & D3
MATCHED PAIR**, **D2 & D4** do, VT13 & VT14 …" [6].

So: **four devices of one type, specified as two matched pairs, and the pairing is across the two branches
(D1 with D3, D2 with D4) rather than down them.** That pairing direction is a fact worth holding on to — it
tells you which two devices EMI needed to behave identically, and it is the *horizontal* pairs, i.e. the ones
in corresponding positions on opposite sides of the network.

**On the type number.** `HS2051` is not a type I could find a datasheet for anywhere. I have no forward
voltage, no saturation current, no ideality factor, no breakdown voltage, no capacitance and no package. The
drawing points to an item list for component details and **the item list is not in the archive item** (3.5).
Nobody publishes it. The Neve's `HBX 31` was in the same position, and there I fell back on Pines' fitted
1N4148 parameters as a labelled estimate [[Neve-33609]]; here I cannot even do that honestly, because I do not
know what *kind* of device HS2051 is (4.3). **Every constant in section 11 that depends on the diode is
therefore a named, calibratable constant and is labelled as such.**

The symbols matter too, and this is the first clue. On the same sheet EMI draw **D13 (BZY88 C5V1)**, **D14
(BZX61C11)** and **D15 (BZX61C12)** with the bent-bar cathode of a **zener** symbol, and they draw **D5, D6,
D7, D8, D9, D10, D11, D12 (all OA202 germanium)** and **D1 to D4** with a **plain straight bar** [5]. So EMI's
draughtsman had a zener symbol available and did not use it for D1 to D4. That is evidence *against* the zener
reading, and I am putting it here rather than burying it, because it is the strongest thing arguing the other
way in 4.3.

### 4.2 The topology, read exactly

From the same crops [5]. Name the two vertical rails of the block **L** (left, carrying D1 above D2) and **R**
(right, carrying D3 above D4), the top of each branch **T_L** and **T_R**, and the bottom of each **A** and
**B**.

- **D1** sits in rail L between T_L and the mid-point; **D2** sits between the mid-point and A.
- **D3** sits in rail R between T_R and its mid-point; **D4** sits between that mid-point and B.
- **There is no connection at either mid-point.** The space between D1 and D2, and between D3 and D4, carries
  only the "MATCHED PAIR" annotations and their leader lines. Nothing crosses.
- **T_L connects, through a break-crossing over the bias wire, to a junction dot on the +20 V rail**, and
  **R16, 24 Ω**, runs horizontally from that same node to a mid node.
- **AOT 8 and AOT 10 are in parallel** with each other, drawn as a resistor and a loop beneath it, and they
  connect the mid node to **T_R**.
- **A** goes to a transistor (VT5) and onward to VT10's base; **B** goes to a transistor (VT8) and down through
  R21 3K9.
- **R14, 20 kΩ**, feeds down from +20 V into the VT5 side.

Stripped of the rails and written as a netlist, the gain element is:

```
+20V ──┬───────────────────────── T_L ── D1 ── mid_L ── D2 ── A ── (VT5 …)
       │
       └── R16 24Ω ── mid ── (AOT8 ∥ AOT10) ── T_R ── D3 ── mid_R ── D4 ── B ── (VT8 …)
```

**Two branches of two series diodes, both hanging from the +20 V rail, joined only at that rail, with a 24 Ω
resistor on one side and a pair of adjust-on-test resistors on the other.** That is not a ring. There is no
closed loop of four diodes anywhere on this sheet.

**The 24 Ω is the tell.** A 24-ohm resistor in a circuit whose other resistors are 3K3, 20K, 51K and 150K is
not doing filtering, biasing or loading. It is there to make two things equal, and the fact that the *other*
branch gets two selected-on-test resistors in parallel instead of a fixed value says that the equality is
established at the bench, one module at a time (2.4). **This is a balance network for two arms that must carry
matched current**, which is exactly consistent with EMI specifying D1/D3 and D2/D4 as matched pairs.

### 4.3 Which way the diodes point, and the three readings that follow

I checked this four times because I did not believe the answer, and it is the hinge of the whole dossier.

**All four diodes are drawn with the cathode bar uppermost and the anode triangle beneath it.** Verified at 8×
on the group, then at 16× on a thresholded, binarised crop of D1 and D2, and again at 16× on D3 and D4. In
every case the symbol is a heavy horizontal bar with an outlined triangle below whose apex touches the bar from
underneath: cathode above, anode below, forward current upward [5].

And the top of the left branch connects to **+20 V**. I traced that twice, because at first reading it appeared
to short R16. It does not: the vertical that appears to join the bias wire beneath it is **broken at the
crossing**, which is the British drawing convention for "no connection", and once you read the break correctly
the network resolves as the netlist in 4.2, with the +20 V rail feeding T_L directly and T_R through
R16 + AOT 8 ∥ AOT 10.

Below, **A and B go to NPN transistors (BC109) whose emitters return toward 0 V** through R19 3K9 and R21 3K9.
An NPN with its emitter toward ground sinks current: conventional current flows *into* it, downward.

Put those three facts together and **current flows from +20 V, down through each pair of diodes, into the
transistors below — that is, from cathode to anode, in reverse.**

Three readings are consistent with the drawing. I give all three, say which I take and why, and say what would
settle it.

**Reading A — the diodes run in reverse breakdown.** They are zener or avalanche devices, biased into
breakdown by the current the transistors below draw, and their incremental breakdown resistance is the
variable. *For:* it is what the drawing says, with no error assumed; it explains a 24 Ω balance resistor
(breakdown resistances are ohms to tens of ohms, so 24 Ω is the right order to trim against, whereas a
forward-biased junction at tens of microamps has kilohms of incremental resistance and a 24 Ω trim would be
pointless); and it agrees with all three external sources in 4.8. *Against:* the symbols are plain, not zener
(4.1), and HS2051's breakdown voltage is unknown.

**Reading B — the draughtsman drew all four backwards.** They are ordinary forward-biased diodes and the
drawing has a consistent error. *For:* the plain symbols; the fact that a forward-biased pair-of-pairs is the
obvious thing to build. *Against:* a consistent four-fold error on a close-tolerance production drawing that
was reissued through several board revisions is a lot to assume, and it does not explain the 24 Ω.

**Reading C — the diodes idle at or near zero bias and the audio itself drives them.** The control current sets
how far they sit from conduction, so the element behaves as a **variable-threshold shunt clipper** rather than
a variable resistor. *For:* it would make the module a limiter in the most literal sense, and it fits Waves'
remark that LIMIT has a "much harder" knee than the compressor [12]. *Against:* it is the least supported by
component values, and I have no evidence for it beyond plausibility.

**I take Reading A as the working hypothesis** on the strength of 4.8, model it as such in section 11, and
expose the operating region as a parameter so that Reading B is a setting rather than a rewrite. **What would
settle it:** the item list for TG12413 (which would say what an HS2051 is), a DC voltage measurement at nodes A
and B on a real module, or a photograph of the board with the diode bodies readable.

### 4.4 The Neve's bridge, restated for comparison

From the sibling dossier, which derived this from Neve's own drawings [[Neve-33609]]:

- **Four diodes in a ring**: D15 and D14 with their **anodes joined** at a floating node C⁺, cathodes at the
  two signal nodes A and B; D17 and D16 with their **cathodes joined** at a floating node C⁻, anodes at A and
  B. On the 2254 the same ring is D1 to D4, type HBX 31, with four 330 pF caps across the junctions.
- **Forward-biased**, by a DC control current injected at C⁺ and taken from C⁻.
- **Transformer on both sides.**
- The signal is a **shunt** across a 94 kΩ series arm, in parallel with a fixed 5k6.
- The exact large-signal law, derived there, is

  ```
  i_bridge(u) = I · tanh( u / (2·η·V_T) )
  ```

  because a pair of diodes with a floating common node driven by a current source *is* a long-tailed pair.
  Consequences: odd function, no even harmonics at all, small-signal resistance r = 2ηV_T/I, and distortion
  that **falls** as gain reduction rises.

### 4.6 If the diodes are forward-biased after all: the same tanh, with the constant doubled

**Derived.** Take Reading B, the most generous case for the shared component: four ordinary forward-biased
diodes, two in series per arm, both arms hanging from a common node that is an AC ground, with the total bias
current 2I held by the control source and split I to each arm.

Let *u* = v_A − v_B be the differential signal, *i* the signal current that transfers from one arm to the
other, so the arms carry I + i and I − i. Take the diodes as ideal-exponential and matched,
i = I_S·exp(v/(ηV_T)). Each arm is **two junctions in series**, so its voltage relative to the common node is
twice a single junction's:

```
v_A − V_common = 2·η·V_T·ln( (I + i) / I_S )
v_B − V_common = 2·η·V_T·ln( (I − i) / I_S )
```

Subtract:

```
u = 2·η·V_T · ln( (I + i)/(I − i) )  =  4·η·V_T · artanh( i / I )
```

and therefore

> **i(u) = I · tanh( u / (4·η·V_T) )**

Compare with the Neve's `i(u) = I · tanh( u / (2·η·V_T) )` [[Neve-33609]]. **Identical function, thermal scale
doubled**, and the doubling is exactly the two junctions per arm against the Neve's one. Three consequences:

1. **Small-signal resistance is doubled at equal control current.** r = 4ηV_T/I against 2ηV_T/I. Using the same
   fitted parameters the Neve dossier used as a labelled estimate, η = 1.755 and V_T = 25.85 mV at 300 K, the
   scale constants are **k_Neve = 90.7 mV** and **k_TG = 181.5 mV** — both **estimates**, both inheriting the
   Neve dossier's caveat that these parameters were fitted to a 1N4148 and neither HBX 31 nor HS2051 has a
   reachable datasheet.
2. **At equal drive the TG distorts four times less.** For tanh(a·sin θ) the third-harmonic ratio is a²/12
   [[Neve-33609]], and a = û/k, so halving a quarters the ratio. At the Neve's own working point of a = 0.34,
   which gives 0.96 % third harmonic, the same voltage across a TG-shaped element gives a = 0.17 and
   **0.24 %** — **derived**, and inheriting every caveat above.
3. **It is still an odd function**, so the even-harmonic argument survives: no second harmonic from the element
   itself in either unit.

So under Reading B the answer to "is it one law?" is **"yes, if and only if the law was parameterised".** A
crate that hard-codes `2·η·V_T` is wrong for the TG by a factor of two in the argument, which is a factor of
four in the third harmonic. A crate that takes junctions-per-arm, or takes k directly, is right for both.

### 4.7 If the diodes are in breakdown, as drawn: it is not a tanh at all

Take Reading A, which is what the sheet shows. Then the four devices are operating on the **reverse breakdown**
branch of their characteristic, and the physics is different in three ways that matter to a model:

- **The law is not the diode exponential.** Below about 5 V, breakdown is quantum tunnelling and the knee is
  soft and rounded; above about 6 V it is avalanche multiplication and the knee is sharp. Neither is
  `I_S·exp(v/ηV_T)`, and neither yields a hyperbolic tangent when you put two arms in opposition. The
  derivation in 4.6 simply does not apply.
- **The incremental resistance behaves differently with current.** A forward junction gives r = ηV_T/I exactly,
  a clean inverse law over five or six decades. A device in breakdown has an incremental resistance that falls
  with current far more slowly and then **bottoms out on its bulk resistance**, typically in the ohms to tens
  of ohms. That is a *much less steep control law*, and it is the best explanation I have for why R16 is 24 Ω:
  you trim against ohms because ohms is what the element presents.
- **The knee is the character.** A device sitting near its breakdown knee is nearly an open circuit on one side
  of the knee and nearly a resistor on the other, with a rounded transition. That is a limiter's transfer
  curve, drawn in silicon, and it is precisely the behaviour a designer aiming at "the sound and behaviour of
  the Fairchild 660" [10] would want and could not get from a forward-biased bridge.

**And it explains the one thing the TG has that the Neve does not: two modes rather than a ratio.** The Neve
gives you five ratios because a forward-biased bridge is a smooth, continuously scalable divider and you shape
it in the sidechain [[Neve-33609]]. The TG gives you COMPRESS or LIMIT and nothing between, because you are
choosing **which side of a knee to sit on**, and there is no continuum there to divide up. Waves say the same
thing from the other end: LIMIT is "the limiter mode of the Zener diode. **The knee is much harder** compared
to the compressor" [12].

I want to be careful about how far I push this. **I have no measured breakdown characteristic for an HS2051, no
DC operating voltages from EMI, and no published transfer curve for the module.** What I have is a topology
that only makes sense in breakdown, three sources that use the word Zener, and a set of component values that
fit breakdown better than forward. Section 11.2 models it as a soft-knee conductance with an explicit knee
voltage and a bulk floor, and section 11.6 makes every one of those a named constant marked **estimate**.

### 4.8 What three independent sources say, and where they converge

This is the part that moved me from "the drawing is probably wrong" to "the drawing is probably right".

1. **EMI's own product naming.** Chandler, writing about a circuit they build under licence from EMI, place
   this module in a line whose *name* is Zener: "the **1968 RS168 Zener Limiter**. The **Zener Limiters** were
   also part of the 1969 TG12345 console channel and the 1974 TG12413" [9].
2. **Chandler on the element.** "The limiting element is a **rarely seen diode network** that produces smooth,
   pleasing distortion" [8]. Note what they do *not* say: not a bridge, not forward-biased, not a ring. "Rarely
   seen" is a strange thing to say about a diode bridge, which by 1969 was in the Neve 2254, the Siemens U273
   and the Dolby A301 [[Neve-33609]]. It is not a strange thing to say about diodes run in breakdown.
3. **Waves, who had the console.** Their user guide names the element three times in three different sentences:
   "precision-modeled after the unit's original **Zener diode limiter**"; "**Original** — The TG12413's original
   **Zener diode** compressor"; "**Limit** — This is the limiter mode of the **Zener diode**" [12]. Waves
   designed this plug-in jointly with Abbey Road Studios [11] [12] and had access to the hardware.

Three sources, two companies, neither of which had any reason to say "zener" if the part were a forward-biased
bridge — and a drawing that, read literally, can only be a reverse-biased element. **They converge.**

The honest counterweight, restated so it is not lost: EMI's draughtsman used plain diode symbols for D1 to D4
and zener symbols for D13, D14, D15 on the same sheet (4.1). Either the annotation convention was loose, or a
device that EMI called a "zener limiter" was not built from parts they drew as zeners. I cannot close that gap
from here.

### 4.9 Verdict on the shared component crate

The argument under test was: **the part is one thing with one law, while the divider, the sidechains and the
shaping network differ between units.** That is why `noob-electrical-components-diode-bridge` exists as its own
crate. The TG12413 is the first unit after the Neve that anyone would expect to use it. Here is the verdict, in
the order I would want to read it.

**1. The premise is wrong about the part.** "Diode bridge" is a *family name*, not a component. Neve's element
is a four-diode ring with two floating common nodes, forward-biased by an injected current, with one junction
per arm. EMI's element is two branches of two series diodes sharing the supply rail as their common node, with
two junctions per arm, and as drawn it is not even in the same operating region. Both are "four diodes doing
gain control" and there the similarity ends. A crate whose type is named for a bridge and whose law is the
bridge's law is a crate that models **one circuit**, and calling it a component was a category error.

**2. The premise is half-right about the law, and only half.** Under the most generous reading of the TG
(Reading B, 4.6), the law generalises exactly — `tanh(u/k)` — but the constant is different, and it differs for
a *structural* reason (junctions per arm) rather than a parametric one (which diode). That is the good news
and it is real: if the crate had been written to take **k**, or better, to take **junctions-per-arm and a diode
model**, it would serve both units today with no change. As shipped with `2·η·V_T` baked in, it is wrong here
by a factor of two in the argument and four in the third harmonic.

**3. Under the reading the drawing actually supports, the crate does not apply at all.** Reverse breakdown is
not the diode exponential and does not produce a tanh (4.7). No amount of constant-fitting recovers it.

**4. So: the TG12413 should not use `noob-electrical-components-diode-bridge`.** Not as it stands, and not with
different constants.

**What I proposed instead**, as a recommendation about the components repository rather than about this
plug-in. Read it together with the ruling that follows it, which narrowed three of these four:

- **Rename and re-scope the existing crate to what it actually is.** It models *Neve's ring*: a four-diode ring
  with floating common nodes and a current-source drive. Call it that. `DiodeRingVca` or similar. It is a good
  piece of work and the derivation in it is sound; it is just not a component.
- **If a shared component is wanted, the thing that genuinely generalises is one level up**: a
  **`SeriesDiodeArm`** — *n* junctions in series, a device model, a bias current — plus a small set of ways to
  put two of them together (opposed into a floating node, which gives Neve's ring; parallel onto a common rail,
  which gives EMI's). Both units' laws fall out of that, the Neve's as n = 1 opposed and the TG's as n = 2
  parallel, and the derivations in the two dossiers become two instantiations rather than two crates.
- **Whatever is shared must carry the operating region as an explicit choice**, because forward and breakdown
  are different physics with different temperature coefficients and different distortion signatures, and this
  family contains at least one unit of each.
- **Do not share the constants.** HBX 31 and HS2051 are different parts and neither has a reachable datasheet.
  Every diode constant in both models is an estimate and should be a named, calibratable one.

**Ruling, taken after this section was written, and recorded here so that nobody builds from a superseded
recommendation.** Three of the four proposals above were decided against or narrowed. **The findings stand; the
remedies changed.**

1. **The crate is not generalised, and not now.** Equation (G1) does contain both cases, but building it would
   mean widening a component to accommodate a reading I could not close — which is exactly why 11.3 makes the
   operating region a runtime switch. A part widened to fit a case nobody is sure of stops describing anything.
   The rule is that a component earns its place once something real shares it, and the ring has exactly one
   certain user. **So the TG's element is built inside the lab, not extracted.** If the topology settles (12.6,
   entries (d) and (h)) and a second user appears, (G1) is the right starting point for the extraction then.
2. **No rename.** A bridge *is* a ring of four and the crate models exactly that, so the name was never the
   thing that was wrong. The wrong thing was the assumption that any diode gain element would fit it, and
   renaming would churn a dependency lock and a published history to fix a mistake that lives in an assumption
   rather than in a word. **What changes is the crate's documentation**, which must now say plainly what it
   models — one junction per arm, closed ring, forward-biased, current-driven — and what it does not. The
   figure to put in that note is the one from 4.6: **two junctions per arm doubles the constant and quarters
   the third harmonic at equal drive**, which is the shortest way to see why the TG is a different part rather
   than the same part with different values.
3. **The family is "diode", not "diode bridge".** Grouping the browser by gain element is right, but this unit
   is diode-based without being a bridge. Forcing it under "bridge" would be false and giving it a family of
   one would be useless, so the family covers both forms and its note says what separates them, which is the
   table in 4.5.

**Why this is the more important finding.** If the TG had validated the crate, the lab would have learned that
one abstraction covers a family. Instead it has learned something more useful and more expensive: that the
abstraction was drawn at the wrong altitude, and that a "family" defined by a component (diodes) rather than by
a mechanism (voltage-controlled series resistance) will keep producing units that do not fit. **Two units into
the diode family, the shared part serves one of them.** That is worth knowing before a third.

### 4.10 What we are not modelling, and why it is honest to say so

- **Temperature.** Both plausible readings are strongly temperature-dependent and in *opposite* directions: a
  forward junction's ηV_T rises with temperature, a true zener below 5 V has a **negative** coefficient, and an
  avalanche device above 6 V has a **positive** one. Since I do not know which device HS2051 is, I do not know
  the sign, and modelling temperature would mean inventing it. Excluded, as it is in the Neve model and in
  Pines' paper [[Neve-33609]] [21].
- **Junction capacitance.** The Neve's designers fought this with four 330 pF caps across the junctions on the
  2254 and two 100 pF to ground on the 33609 [[Neve-33609]]. **EMI put nothing across theirs** — there is no
  capacitor in or across the diode block on TG12413-D101 [5]. A reverse-biased junction's capacitance is much
  lower than a forward-biased one's and varies with bias, so the absence of compensation is itself weak
  evidence for Reading A. Excluded from the model; noted as a stretch goal in 11.8.
- **Diode mismatch.** EMI specify matched pairs twice [5] [6] and provide two adjust-on-test resistors to trim
  the residual (2.4), which is evidence they knew it mattered. The model gets a **mismatch** parameter that is
  zero by default (11.3), because a spoof of a hand-aligned laboratory module should be able to be slightly out
  of alignment on purpose.
- **Reverse recovery, bulk inductance, noise from the element itself.** Excluded, following the same reasoning
  as the Neve model.

## 5. The sidechain

### 5.1 The shape of the thing

Read off the lower half of TG12413-D101 [5]. Unlike the 33609, which runs an independent limiter sidechain and
an independent compressor sidechain into one gain element simultaneously [[Neve-33609]], **the TG has one
sidechain and a mode switch**. S1 selects COMPRESS, OUT or LIMIT (2.1); it does not switch between two
detectors, it reconfigures one.

The chain, in order, with the transistors as EMI numbers them:

```
 audio tap ─► VT2, VT3 ─► VT7 ─► VT13 / VT14 (hFE-matched pair) ─► D6, D7 (OA202)
        ─► VT15, VT16 ─► threshold reference D8, D9, D10 (3 × OA202) and the AOT law block
        ─► VT17 ─► R41, D11, R47 (attack path) ─► C10 / C18 (store) ─► VT20, VT21
        ─► RV1 HOLD, D12, S2 RECOVERY ladder (release path)
        ─► VT22, VT23, VT24 ─► VT27 (BCY34) ─► GANG bus, tag 36
        ─► back to the gain element's control current
```

Two structural facts before the detail:

- **The matched pair is in the detector, not in the gain element's drive.** VT13 and VT14 are the only two
  transistors on the sheet with a specification note attached — "VT13 & VT14 to have **hFE MATCHED** according
  to item list" [5], repeated on the board sheet [6]. A matched transistor pair feeding two germanium diodes
  into one node is a **full-wave rectifier**, and 5.2 argues that is what it is.
- **The release path is one-way.** D12, an OA202, sits between the store and the recovery ladder [5]. A diode
  in a discharge path means charge leaves through the ladder and cannot come back that way, which is what
  makes a **hold** function possible at all (5.5).

### 5.2 The detector: a matched pair and two germanium diodes

**VT13 and VT14 (BC109, hFE-matched)** sit side by side with **R29 15K** and **R36 15K** above them and **R37
1K** below VT14, and their outputs meet at **D6** and **D7, both OA202**, drawn one above the other between the
same two vertical nets and both pointing the same way, into **VT15 (BCY71)**'s base through **R38 27K** with
**R37 3K9** to the rail [5].

**Reading:** two matched devices, each handling one polarity, OR-ed into one node through two germanium diodes
— a **full-wave rectifier built from a matched pair**. That is the classic way to rectify audio without a
transformer when you need both halves and cannot afford the offset of an unmatched pair.

**Honest caveat.** At 1.4× I read D6 and D7 as parallel and same-direction; at that magnification I cannot rule
out that one of them is inverted, which would make them an anti-parallel pair and change the reading from
full-wave-OR to something else. **I could not resolve it and I am not going to guess.** What the note about
hFE matching does establish, on its own, is that **the two devices must behave identically**, and the only
reason to require that of a pair in a detector is that they are handling two halves of the same waveform.

**Germanium matters here.** OA202 is a germanium point-contact-era diode with a forward drop of roughly 0.2 to
0.3 V rather than silicon's 0.6 to 0.7. In a rectifier that means the detector starts working at a much lower
signal level and its knee is softer. **Derived, and it is a real audible consequence:** a germanium-rectified
detector has a gentler onset than a silicon one, which is part of why this unit is described as soft and
"squishy" (9.1) rather than as a hard threshold. EMI use germanium **eight times** on this sheet — D5, D6, D7,
D8, D9, D10, D11, D12, all OA202 [5] — and silicon only for the four gain-element diodes and the three zeners.
**That distribution is a design statement**, and section 11.4 keeps it.

### 5.3 The threshold reference: three germanium diodes in a string

**D8, D9 and D10** are drawn as a vertical string of three, annotated on the sheet as **`3 × OA202`**, from a
sidechain node down toward the **−20 V** rail, with a hand-written arrow and the words "general purpose"
alongside [5]. (The "general purpose" note is the previous owner's pen, not EMI's.)

**Reading:** a three-diode reference string is a **voltage reference of about 0.6 to 0.9 V**, germanium, made
from three drops in series. **Derived**, from the germanium forward drop. It is the closest thing on this sheet
to a threshold, and it is fixed — **there is no threshold control on this module** (2.1).

That is worth stating against the Neve, which has a sixteen-position compress threshold switch from −20 to
+10 dBu and a twenty-three-position limit threshold switch from +4 to +15 dBu [[Neve-33609]]. **The TG has
neither.** Its threshold is three germanium junctions and an alignment procedure. Whatever level you feed it is
the level it works at, which is why Chandler added a continuous input control to their recreation [8] and why
the spoof should let the user drive it.

### 5.4 The law network: two resistors and four adjust-on-test parts

Immediately after the reference string, the sheet carries a block of six resistors in two rows of three [5]:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **R31 2K** | **AOT 3** | **AOT 4** |
| **R32 820** | **AOT 5** | **AOT 6** |

with **R24 51K, R28 51K** and **R30 1K** above them and the whole block returning to −20 V.

**Reading:** this is the shaping network — the TG's equivalent of the Neve's INITIAL LAW / TOP END LAW resistor
ladder on the ratio switch [[Neve-33609]] — except that where Neve made theirs a **front-panel switch with five
positions**, EMI made theirs **four resistors chosen at the factory**. Two rows of three with a fixed head
resistor in each row reads as **two law segments, each with two selected components**: one setting where the
law starts and one setting its slope. **Inferred**, and labelled as such: the drawing gives no annotation for
this block and no values for the AOTs.

**This is the second of the three most important modelling decisions.** The TG's compression law is not a
switch and not a formula — it is a hand-aligned network, and two modules could legitimately have different
laws. A model that exposes the law as fixed is modelling a fiction. Section 11.3 gives the law two calibratable
constants and 11.6 marks both as estimates.

### 5.5 The timing: a fixed attack, a one-way store, and HOLD

The charge and discharge paths are separable on the drawing, which is more than can be said for most of this
sheet.

**Charge (attack).** **VT17 (BC109)**, fed from +20 V through **R40 150K** with **R45 2K**, drives **R41 39K**
into **D11 (OA202)** into **R47 8K2**, and thence into the store. **The path is through a diode, so it is
one-way**, and the resistance in it is R41 + R47 ≈ **47.2 kΩ** [5].

**The store.** Two candidates sit right there: **C10, 4µ7** and **C18, 1µ**, with **C16, 20 µF** further along
past R63 6K2 [5]. I could not establish from the drawing alone which of these the recovery ladder discharges,
and 7.3 gives the arithmetic for all three rather than picking one.

**Discharge (recovery).** Out of the store, through **VT20 (BC109)** with **AOT 1** and **AOT 2** in its
emitter, **VT21 (BCY71)**, and then out through terminal **F** to **RV1, the 10 kΩ HOLD preset**, through
**D12 (OA202)**, to terminal **G** and the **S2 recovery ladder** [5].

**What HOLD does, read from the parts.** RV1 10 kΩ is in series with a ladder whose smallest step is 47 kΩ, so
at its extremes RV1 changes the fastest recovery by about **±20 %** and the slowest by under 0.5 %. That is not
a hold in the modern sense of a plateau timer. **Reading:** it is a **trim on the fast end of the recovery
range**, and the name "HOLD" describes what it feels like rather than what it is — turn it up and the fastest
setting stops being quite so fast. **Derived**, and I am flagging it because Chandler put HOLD on their front
panel and ganged it with an input control [18], which suggests it does something more audible on their circuit
than 10 kΩ against 47 kΩ would suggest on EMI's.

**The attack is fixed and nobody publishes it.** Chandler's specification says "**Attack: Fixed**" [8]; there is
no attack control on EMI's drawing; and Waves say only that "the higher the recovery state, the longer the
attack/release time" [12], which implies the recovery switch moves both. **Derived** from R41 + R47 ≈ 47.2 kΩ
into the three candidate stores:

| store | attack time constant |
|---|---|
| C18 1 µF | **47 ms** |
| C10 4µ7 | **222 ms** |
| C16 20 µF | **944 ms** |

All three are slow for a limiter, which tells me the charge path is almost certainly **current-driven by VT17
rather than resistively driven**, so those RC figures are upper bounds rather than estimates of the real attack.
**I could not establish the attack time and no source publishes one.** Section 12 does not test it.

### 5.6 The recovery ladder, exactly

**Derived**, from the six values in 2.1 [5]. The ladder is a series chain tapped at six points, so the
resistance in circuit is the accumulated sum from the common end:

| position | resistors in circuit | accumulated |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | R1 | **47 kΩ** |
| 2 | R1 + R2 | **94 kΩ** |
| 3 | + R3 | **224 kΩ** |
| 4 | + R4 | **444 kΩ** |
| 5 | + R5 | **914 kΩ** |
| 6 | + R6 | **2 214 kΩ** |

Ratios to the fastest position: **1 : 2 : 4.8 : 9.4 : 19.4 : 47.1**. That is very close to a geometric ladder
of about ×2.15 per step, and the whole range spans **47 : 1**. Compare the Neve, whose compress recovery
positions are 100, 400, 800 and 1500 ms — a span of 15 : 1 across four positions [[Neve-33609]]. **The TG's
recovery range is three times wider and has two more positions.** That is a real, structural difference
between the two units and it is one a listener can hear (9.2).

### 5.7 The GANG bus, which is the stereo link

At the right-hand edge of the sheet, a **screened lead marked `GANG` on tag 36** leaves the control-voltage
node, alongside a wire marked **RED** to tag 26 and the recovery switch's common [5]. **VT27 (BCY34)** with
**R69 1K, R71 12K** and **R72 24K** drives it.

**Reading:** this is the **control-voltage bus that links modules**. On a console you do not put a link switch
on every channel strip; you wire the control voltages of the channels you want ganged onto a common rail, and
whichever module is working hardest pulls the rail. **Inferred** from the name and the topology; EMI print no
description.

The modelling consequence is worth stating: **the TG's stereo link is not a mode, it is a wire**, and its
natural behaviour is a **maximum** across every module on the bus, not a sum and not a matched pair. That is the
same combining rule the Neve's two sidechains use internally [[Neve-33609]], reached for a different reason.

### 5.8 The meter

**There is no meter on drawing TG12413-D101** [5]. Whatever gain-reduction indication the console gave, it was
not on this module's sheet. Chandler give their TG1 two meters scaled 0 to +16 dB of gain reduction [18] and
Waves give their module a vertical meter scaled +9 to −20 [17]; **both are additions**, and if the example
plug-in has a meter it is an invention too. Say so on the page.

## 6. COMPRESS, OUT and LIMIT

### 6.1 One switch, three positions, and no ratio anywhere

S1 is a three-position rotary reading **COMPRESS / OUT / LIMIT**, assembly `TG12413 B205A`, with its legend
table printed on the drawing [5] (2.1). There is no ratio control, no ratio ladder and no ratio annotation
anywhere on TG12413-D101. **The mode switch is the ratio control**, in the sense that choosing a mode is the
only thing on the module that changes the shape of the transfer curve.

That is a bigger difference from the Neve than it looks. The 33609 has five compress ratios *and* an
independent limiter section that runs simultaneously, so it can be doing 1.5:1 gentle compression and
brickwall limiting at the same instant through one bridge [[Neve-33609]]. **The TG cannot do both.** COMPRESS
and LIMIT are mutually exclusive positions on one switch, with OUT physically between them.

### 6.2 What actually changes between the modes

Read off the sidechain's supply end [5], and **inferred** as to which switch drives it, because the drawing
draws the contacts without labelling the wafer:

Around **VT18 (BCY71)**, fed from the +20 V rail through **R43 430** with **R42 1K** in its emitter, sit three
resistors in a row with switch contacts at their tops and a dashed switch link between two of them:

| resistor | value |
|---|---|
| R48 | **62 K** |
| R49 | **82 K** |
| R50 | **120 K** |

and around **VT19 (BC109)**, on the −20 V side with **R46 390** and **R44 1K**, an identical arrangement:

| resistor | value |
|---|---|
| R51 | **62 K** |
| R52 | **82 K** |
| R53 | **20 K** |

**Reading: a two-pole, three-way wafer — which is exactly what S1 is — selecting one resistor in each of two
places.** So the mode switch does not merely insert or remove the sidechain; it **re-scales the detector's
drive on both supply polarities at once**. Three positions, two poles, six resistors.

**What that implies about the modes**, and I want to be clear that this is inference from values rather than
anything EMI states:

- The **62 K / 62 K** pairing is the same on both poles and is the likely **COMPRESS** setting: symmetric.
- The **120 K against 20 K** pairing is strongly asymmetric — six to one — and is the likely **LIMIT** setting.
  An asymmetric drive on a detector feeding a knee-shaped element is how you turn a soft law into a hard one
  without changing the element.
- The **82 K / 82 K** pairing is symmetric again and is the likely **OUT** position, i.e. the sidechain is left
  biased but ineffective rather than disconnected.

**If that last point is right, OUT is not a bypass.** The audio still passes through the gain element, the
element still sits at its bias point, and only the control is neutralised. Chandler's TG1 keeps a **separate**
`OUT / IN` toggle *and* a `COMP / LIMIT` lever [18], and their THD mode "allows bypassing of the
compressor/limiter threshold **but leaves all circuits in the signal path**" [8] — which is precisely the
distinction between "no gain reduction" and "not in circuit". **Model OUT as no gain reduction, not as a
bypass**, and give the model a separate true bypass for A/B, marked as an addition.

### 6.3 What "not a brick-wall limiter" means here

Waves are unusually direct about their LIMIT mode, and since they modelled the hardware with Abbey Road it is
the nearest thing to a behavioural specification anybody publishes:

> **Limit** — This is the limiter mode of the Zener diode. The knee is much harder compared to the compressor
> ["Original"]. **Please note that this is not a brick-wall limiter: transients are expected to pass.** [12]

Contrast the Neve, whose limiter section the manufacturer specifies as **0.1 dB ±0.1 dB of output change for a
10 dB input step** — a genuine brickwall, with a tolerance [[Neve-33609]]. **The TG's LIMIT is not that**, and
a model that makes it that is wrong. Tests 12 and 18 in section 12 assert the difference.

## 7. Time constants

### 7.1 Nobody publishes one. Not EMI, not Chandler, not Waves

This has to be said before any arithmetic, because it is the defining weakness of the evidence base and it is
the exact opposite of the Neve's position.

- **EMI** print no time on drawing TG12413-D101. The recovery switch is drawn with six resistors and six tag
  numbers and no legend at all [5], where the output-level switch got a full legend table on the same sheet.
- **Chandler** publish "Recovery (Release): **Switched, 6 position**" and "**Attack: Fixed**" and stop there
  [8]. Their recovery lever is marked 1 to 6 [18].
- **Waves**, who had the console, decline explicitly: the six recovery times "are **very hard to put in terms
  of exact milliseconds**. Think of them in the following terms: the higher the recovery state, the longer the
  attack/release time. You will need to experiment in order to hear the exact difference" [12].

**When the company that built the licensed model says the times cannot be put in milliseconds, there is no
published figure to test against, and section 12 does not invent one.** What section 12 tests instead are
*ratios*, which the resistor ladder does establish.

### 7.2 The ladder's arithmetic, which is the only hard number here

From 5.6. The six accumulated resistances are 47 k, 94 k, 224 k, 444 k, 914 k and 2 214 kΩ, giving normalised
recovery ratios of

> **1 : 2.00 : 4.77 : 9.45 : 19.4 : 47.1**

**This is a real, checkable, primary-source figure** and it is the one time-domain anchor the model has. It says
nothing about how long position 1 is, and everything about how much longer position 6 is. A model whose
position 6 is not 47 times position 1 is wrong, whatever absolute times it chooses. **Test 3 asserts it.**

### 7.3 Which capacitor is the store, and why I cannot say

The three candidates and what each implies, **derived** by τ = R·C:

| position | ×C18 (1 µF) | ×C10 (4µ7) | ×C16 (20 µF) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 47 ms | 221 ms | 0.94 s |
| 2 | 94 ms | 442 ms | 1.88 s |
| 3 | 224 ms | 1.05 s | 4.48 s |
| 4 | 444 ms | 2.09 s | 8.88 s |
| 5 | 914 ms | 4.30 s | 18.3 s |
| 6 | 2.21 s | 10.4 s | 44.3 s |

**My reading is C18, 1 µF**, giving **47 ms to 2.2 s**, and here is why: it is the only one of the three whose
range is plausible for a programme-dependent limiter, the 20 µF column is absurd at the top end, and Waves say
positions "3, 4 and 5" are the useful ones for mastering [12] — which on the C18 column is 224 ms, 444 ms and
914 ms, exactly the range a mastering engineer would reach for, and on the C10 column is 1.05 s to 4.3 s, which
is not. **That is an argument from plausibility, not from the drawing**, and it is labelled an **estimate**
wherever it propagates. Section 11.6 makes the store a named constant so that the whole column can be moved
with one number if better evidence appears.

An interesting side note for the model: **one time constant, not two.** The Neve's AUTO positions are dual time
constants in parallel, a fast one and a slow one, which is what makes its release programme-dependent
[[Neve-33609]]. **The TG's ladder is a single series chain into a single store, with no second path and no auto
position.** Its release is a plain exponential at whichever of six rates you chose. That is simpler, and it is
the sort of simplicity a listener notices as "it just lets go".

### 7.4 HOLD, and what it is worth

From 5.5: RV1 is 10 kΩ in series with a ladder whose smallest value is 47 kΩ. **Derived:** at position 1 it
trims the recovery over 47 k to 57 k, a **21 % range**; at position 6 over 2214 k to 2224 k, **0.45 %**. So
HOLD is a fast-end trim and it is nearly inoperative at the slow settings. Model it that way — in series with
the ladder, not as a separate stage — and it will behave correctly at both ends for free.

## 8. Published measurements

### 8.1 There are none. Said plainly

**I found no measurement of a TG12413, of any kind, by anybody.** No frequency response, no distortion figure,
no noise figure, no attack or release time, no ratio, no threshold, no transfer curve, no null test.

That is not for want of looking, and it is worth listing what does not exist so that nobody repeats the search:

- **No EMI service manual or specification sheet.** The archive item is a set of circuit and assembly drawings
  only [1]. There is no accompanying handbook, no alignment procedure, no test schedule.
- **No item list**, which is where EMI would have put the component specifications (3.5).
- **No manufacturer specification for the original.** The TG12413 was never a product; it was a module in a
  console EMI built for itself and "never commercially available to the public" [10]. There was nobody to
  publish a specification *to*.
- **No published measurement of any recreation either.** Chandler's TG1 specification page gives circuit type,
  channel count, a feature list, I/O type and power requirement, and **no numbers at all** — no response, no
  distortion, no noise, no headroom [8]. Waves publish latency and supported sample rates [11].
- **No independent laboratory measurement.** This is the same category-wide gap the survey identified across
  all fifteen candidates [22], and the TG is at the far end of it.
- **Sound On Sound is unreachable for this unit.** Every review slug I constructed for a Chandler TG1 returns
  HTTP 410, as it did for most units in the survey [22].

**Compare the Neve, whose factory handbook gives a per-ratio transfer table with per-position tolerances, two
attack times with tolerances and a stated measurement procedure, six recovery times, three distortion figures
each with its operating conditions, a noise figure and a response tolerance** [[Neve-33609]]. The 33609 model
can be calibrated against fourteen manufacturer numbers. **The TG12413 model can be calibrated against two**,
and both of them are things I derived from resistor values on a drawing rather than things a manufacturer
measured (8.3).

### 8.2 What is published, and by whom

The complete list, so that section 12 has something to point at:

| claim | value | source | what kind of claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| output level range and step | **−10 / 0 / +10 dB, 1 dB steps** | EMI drawing legend [5] | **manufacturer, primary** |
| recovery positions | **6** | EMI drawing [5]; Chandler [8]; Waves [12] | manufacturer, three sources |
| mode positions | **COMPRESS / OUT / LIMIT** | EMI drawing legend [5] | **manufacturer, primary** |
| attack | **fixed** | Chandler specification [8] | manufacturer, recreation |
| recovery times in ms | **"very hard to put in terms of exact milliseconds"** | Waves user guide [12] | explicit refusal |
| limiting behaviour | **"not a brick-wall limiter: transients are expected to pass"** | Waves user guide [12] | behavioural, no number |
| distortion, THD mode | **"up to 2 % Total Harmonic Distortion … more than 2 times the amount of analog tape"** | Chandler [8] | **recreation, in a mode EMI did not have** |
| I/O | transformer balanced, XLR pin 2 hot | Chandler [8] | recreation only; EMI's is capacitor-coupled (3.3) |
| gain element | **"Zener diode"** ×3 | Waves user guide [12] | manufacturer description |
| gain element | "a rarely seen diode network" | Chandler [8] | manufacturer description |

**Chandler's 2 % figure is the only quantity with a percent sign anywhere in this dossier's source base**, and
it describes a deliberate distortion mode on a rack unit that has transformers EMI's module does not have, with
the threshold bypassed. It is not a measurement of a TG12413 and section 12 does not treat it as one.

### 8.3 The two calibration anchors that do exist

Both are **derived from EMI's own drawing**, and both are checkable, which is why section 12 leans on them so
heavily.

1. **The output-level ladder reproduces the printed legend to 0.09 dB per step** (3.4). Twenty-one resistor
   values against a three-line legend table on the same sheet. This is the TG's equivalent of the Neve's
   25.01 dB bridge-loss check, and it is arguably stronger, because it checks twenty-one numbers rather than
   three.
2. **The recovery ladder fixes the ratios between the six positions at 1 : 2.00 : 4.77 : 9.45 : 19.4 : 47.1**
   (7.2). Six resistor values, no absolute time.

Everything else in section 12 asserts either a circuit identity, a manufacturer's *qualitative* statement, or a
difference from the Neve model under the same input. **Where there is no number, the test says so.**

### 8.4 What I would do with a real unit

Not achievable here, but worth writing down because the gap is specific and small. Three measurements would
close most of this dossier's uncertainty:

- **DC voltage at nodes A and B** on the gain element, and at T_L and T_R (4.2). Two probe readings would
  settle Reading A against Reading B (4.3) and end the largest open question in the file.
- **Recovery time at positions 1 and 6**, by the standard method. Two numbers would turn the ratio anchor into
  an absolute one and would identify the store capacitor (7.3).
- **THD against input level at fixed gain reduction, in both modes.** One curve would establish whether
  distortion rises or falls with drive, which is the sharpest audible difference from the Neve (9.2).

## 9. How it is described as sounding, and what an emulation must get right

### 9.1 The published descriptions

There are four, all from manufacturers, and I am quoting them rather than paraphrasing because they are the
whole of the qualitative evidence.

Chandler, on the TG1:

> The limiting element is a rarely seen diode network that produces **smooth, pleasing distortion**. The **warm
> open sound, often compared to a Fairchild 670 set to stun**, is very audible and **squishy sounding while
> maintaining its clarity and high frequency response**. [8]

Chandler, on what it was for:

> The compressor was originally supposed to replicate the sound and behavior of the **Fairchild 660 and RS124
> tube devices**, but ended up with a completely unique character. [10]

Waves, on their ORIGINAL mode:

> **Aggressive and 'dirty'**, precision-modeled after the unit's original Zener diode limiter [12]

Waves, on LIMIT:

> The knee is **much harder** compared to the compressor … this is **not a brick-wall limiter: transients are
> expected to pass**. [12]

Read together they are more consistent than manufacturer copy usually is: **audible, dirty, squishy, soft
through the middle and hard at the edge, and it does not stop transients.** Two independent companies reach for
the Fairchild as the comparison, and neither reaches for anything Neve made.

### 9.2 Against the Neve: what a listener must be able to tell apart

This is the brief's real question. Two diode units in one plug-in; a listener should not have to read the
faceplate to know which is which. From everything above, here are the differences I would stake the model on,
strongest first.

1. **Distortion goes the other way with drive.** The Neve's element distorts *less* as gain reduction rises,
   because more control current means less voltage across the bridge — derived there and corroborated
   independently by Pines [[Neve-33609]] [21]. The TG's element, on the breakdown reading, is near-linear until
   the drive approaches its knee and then turns hard. **So on a rising input the Neve gets cleaner and the TG
   gets dirtier**, and that is audible on a single sustained note with a level ramp. This is the single most
   distinguishing behaviour and test 17 asserts it.
2. **No transformers, so a different low end and a different top.** The 33609 has four transformers; the
   TG12413 has none (3.3). The Neve's low-frequency behaviour includes core asymmetry that rises as level
   rises; the TG's low end is set by a 4.7 µF input capacitor and a 470 µF output capacitor and is essentially
   flat and linear. **The TG should sound tighter and less "wound" at the bottom, and should not develop
   low-frequency harmonic content on loud material.**
3. **The release range is three times wider.** 47 : 1 across six positions against the Neve's 15 : 1 across
   four (5.6). Position 1 against position 6 on the TG is a much bigger gesture than anything on the Neve's
   recovery switch, and there are no auto positions to blur it (7.3).
4. **One detector, not two.** The Neve's signature trick is a compressor and a limiter fighting over one bridge
   with a maximum-combining rule, so its gain reduction has two slopes in it [[Neve-33609]]. **The TG has one
   detector and a mode switch** (6.1). Its gain reduction has one slope. On dense material the Neve's control
   voltage has a visible knee where the limiter takes over; the TG's does not.
5. **Germanium rectification, so a softer onset.** Eight OA202s on the sheet against the Neve's silicon
   sidechain (5.2). **The TG should start compressing earlier and more gradually** for the same nominal
   threshold.
6. **No brickwall.** Feed both a fast transient at 20 dB over threshold: the Neve's limiter holds it to within
   a couple of tenths of a decibel [[Neve-33609]]; the TG lets it through, on Waves' own statement [12].

### 9.3 What an emulation must get right, in priority order

1. **The knee, and that it moves between modes.** COMPRESS and LIMIT are two shapes of the same element, not
   two ratios. Get the shapes distinct and the rest is detail.
2. **The transient behaviour of the limiter, which is to say its *failure* to stop transients.** This is the
   thing every existing model of this box is careful to mention (6.3) and the thing a brickwall-shaped
   implementation gets wrong by default.
3. **The recovery ladder's ratios**, whatever absolute times you choose (7.2).
4. **The stepped, uncalibrated feel of the panel.** Six positions with no times on them, and an output trim in
   exact decibels (2.2). The contrast between a calibrated control and an uncalibrated one is most of the
   unit's personality.
5. **Distortion that rises with drive**, which needs the element modelled rather than multiplied (9.2).
6. **No transformers.** Resist the temptation to add the 610's transformer for warmth. It is not there.

### 9.4 What I would not bother with

- **Modelling the ±28 V supply droppers and the two output zeners.** They set operating points, not sound.
- **Modelling the 150 pF, 220 pF and 100 pF compensation capacitors.** They are radio-frequency and stability
  parts, well above the audio band.
- **A brickwall safety limiter.** The hardware does not have one and 6.3 says so explicitly.
- **A ratio control.** There isn't one. Adding one is what Waves did [12], and it is the change that makes
  their model stop being a model.

## 10. Existing emulations, and what each claims

Far fewer than the Neve has, and for a good reason: **there is no hardware to model unless EMI lets you.**
Chandler are "the only company in the world authorized to develop, manufacture and market the 'Official
Equipment' of EMI/Abbey Road Studios" [10], and Waves built theirs with Abbey Road [11] [12]. Everyone else
would be working from the same 17-page archive item I am.

### 10.1 Waves — Abbey Road TG Mastering Chain

The only software model I could find [11] [12]. Five modules — TG12411 Input, TG12412 Tone, **TG12413 Limiter**,
TG12414 Filter, TG12416 Output — "modeled after the original **EMI TG12410 Transfer Console** used in all Abbey
Road Studios' mastering suites from the early '70s to this day" [12]. Released October 2014 [13].

**What it claims about the limiter**, verbatim [12]:

- **ORIGINAL**: "Aggressive and 'dirty', **precision-modeled** after the unit's original Zener diode limiter."
- **MODERN**: "**VCA-based compressor, newly designed** … an original Waves and Abbey Road design." Not a model.
- **LIMIT**: "the limiter mode of the Zener diode. The knee is much harder … **not a brick-wall limiter**."
- **RECOVERY**: six positions, 1 to 6, default 3; times "very hard to put in terms of exact milliseconds".
- **RATIO** 1–100, **MIX**, **MAKE UP** −12 to +12 dB, and three **linear-phase sidechain filters** (high-pass
  25 Hz–1 kHz at 48 dB/oct, bell 100 Hz–12 kHz ±40 dB, low-pass 1 kHz–22 kHz at 48 dB/oct), all described in
  the guide as Waves' own additions.
- Sidechain topology follows the ST / DUO / MS selector: **summed** in Stereo, **independent** in Duo, **mid and
  side independent** in MS [12].

**What it publishes as numbers**: latency (8305 samples for the full component at 44.1 and 48 kHz, 16512 above
that; 0 for the Live component) and supported sample rates to 192 kHz [11]. **No measurement of the hardware,
no null test.**

**What is most useful about it to me**: three unambiguous statements that the gain element is a Zener diode
(4.8), an explicit refusal to name the recovery times (7.1), and an explicit statement that the limiter is not
a brickwall (6.3). Those three between them are worth more than any amount of adjectives.

### 10.2 Chandler Limited — TG1 Limiter

Hardware, 2U, two channels [8] [18]. "A recreation of the classic EMI TG12413 Limiter used in custom EMI and
Abbey Road recording and mastering consoles from the late '60s." Its published feature set maps onto EMI's
drawing more closely than anything else in this list: **Compression Modes: Selectable (Comp, LIMIT); Attack:
Fixed; Recovery (Release): Switched, 6 position; Output Control: Switched** [8]. Those four lines independently
corroborate S1, the absence of an attack control, S2's six-resistor ladder and S3 (2.1).

**What it adds** (1.6): transformers at both ends, a continuous input control, HOLD promoted to the panel,
stereo/dual-mono, true bypass, two gain-reduction meters, and the **THD mode** — "allows bypassing of the
compressor/limiter threshold but leaves all circuits in the signal path, turning the unit into a powerful
Harmonic Distortion generator capable of kicking out **up to 2 % Total Harmonic Distortion**" [8].

That THD mode is a genuinely good idea and the spoof should steal the *concept* while being clear it is
Chandler's: a mode that keeps the element in circuit but takes the control away, so you hear what the element
does to the sound with no gain reduction at all. It is also, incidentally, an admission of how much colour the
element contributes on its own.

### 10.3 Chandler Limited — Zener Limiter

Hardware [9]. "The ultimate TG limiter … conceived by Chandler Limited designer Wade Goeke and is **based on
the vintage EMI circuits** used to record The Beatles and Pink Floyd", released for Abbey Road's 75th
anniversary, and explicitly a **hybrid** of the RS168 Zener Limiter and the TG12345 console strip rather than a
recreation of one unit. Goeke's additions are listed as "switchable input impedance for hard or soft driving of
the unit, **11-position attack, 21-position release**, side chain filtering, and **Comp 1, Comp 2, and Limit**
settings" [9].

**Every one of those is an addition**, and taken together they say something useful about the original by
contrast: the EMI module has a fixed attack, six release positions, no sidechain filter, no input impedance
switch and two compression modes. The Zener Limiter is what a modern designer builds when freed from the
original's constraints, which makes it a poor guide to the original and a good guide to what engineers wished
the original had.

Its most valuable contribution to this dossier is its history paragraph, which is the source of the EMI limiter
lineage in 1.4 and the word "Zener" in 4.8.

### 10.4 Chandler Limited — TG Opto Compressor

Hardware, 500-series [10]. Despite tracing "its lineage to the historic EMI TG12413 limiters", its gain element
is an **opto**, so it is not evidence about this circuit and it is not a competitor to this model. It is listed
here so that anyone comparing plug-ins does not mistake it for one. Its page does, however, carry the best
history text Chandler publish (1.1, 1.2).

### 10.5 What none of them does

- **None publishes a measurement of the hardware.** Not one number describing the original's behaviour (8.1).
- **None publishes a null test.**
- **None ships the original's control set unmodified.** Waves add four controls, Chandler TG1 adds six,
  Chandler Zener Limiter is a new design. **Nobody ships COMPRESS / OUT / LIMIT, RECOVERY 1–6 and a 21-step
  output trim and nothing else.** That is a gap the spoof can fill and it is a genuinely funny one: the joke is
  that the whole thing has three switches.
- **None presents it as a console module.** Waves draw it as a rack strip in a chain, Chandler build it as 2U.
  Drawing it as what it is would be a first (2.8).

## 11. Recommended DSP design

Target: 44.1 to 192 kHz, real time, one `Processor` behind a `tg_model` switch, sharing the dB-domain detector
scaffolding, meter and transfer-curve streams with the rest of the lab where they fit, and **sharing nothing
with the Neve's diode-bridge crate** (4.9).

### 11.1 The shape

```
in ─► [input trim, addition] ─► [input amp, fixed gain] ─┐
                                                          ├─► GAIN ELEMENT ─► [post amp]
      control current  ◄─── [sidechain] ◄─────────────────┘        │
                                                                   ▼
                                                   [S3 output ladder] ─► [output amp] ─► out
```

Feedback or feedforward? **The Neve is a feedback design and I could establish that from AMS Neve's own
description of the tap points** [[Neve-33609]]. For the TG I traced the sidechain's input back as far as VT2
and VT3 and could not establish with confidence whether it taps before or after the gain element. **I am
implementing it as feedback**, on three grounds, and labelling the choice as **inferred**:

1. Every unit of this era and this ambition is a feedback design, including the Fairchild the TG was aimed at
   [10] and the Neve it sits beside.
2. A feedback loop is what produces the soft, self-limiting law that both manufacturers describe as "smooth"
   and "squishy" (9.1) without any explicit knee shaping.
3. There is no threshold control, which is a strong hint: in a feedforward design the threshold has to come
   from somewhere, and in a feedback design around a fixed reference it does not.

**If that is wrong, the place it shows is test 15**, because a feedforward loop gives a constant slope above
the knee where a feedback loop's slope keeps moving. But **no published figure distinguishes the two
topologies for this unit**, so no test in section 12 asserts the choice directly, and this paragraph is the
honest record of a decision made on inference.

### 11.2 The gain element, in equations — and the law that should have been the shared one

**Derived**, from 4.6 and 4.7. The point of this formulation is that **one expression covers Neve's ring, the
TG's forward reading and the TG's breakdown reading, with two constants deciding which**. That is what a shared
component should have looked like.

Model each arm as *n* junctions in series with a bulk resistance. Two arms in opposition across the differential
audio, biased at *I* each, carrying I + i and I − i. Then the differential voltage across the element is

> **u(i) = 2·r_b·i + 2·V_n·artanh( i / I )**     (G1)

with

| circuit | n | V_n | r_b | reduces to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neve ring, forward | 1 | η·V_T | 0 | `i = I·tanh(u / 2ηV_T)` — the Neve dossier's law exactly |
| TG, forward (Reading B) | 2 | 2·η·V_T | ≈0 | `i = I·tanh(u / 4ηV_T)` — 4.6 |
| TG, breakdown (Reading A) | 2 | knee scale, **estimate** | **> 0**, order 10–30 Ω | soft knee onto a resistive floor |

`artanh` is the inverse of `tanh` and (G1) is exact for both zero-r_b cases, so **nothing is approximated when
r_b = 0**. When r_b > 0 the law stops being a pure tanh and gains the resistive floor that breakdown operation
requires, which is exactly the behaviour 4.7 asks for. **R16's 24 Ω is the drawing's own hint at the order of
magnitude of r_b** and 11.6 uses it as the default.

**Solving it per sample.** The audio source drives the element through a series resistance R_s, so the node
equation is implicit:

```
(v_s − u) / R_s = i,        u = u(i) from (G1)
```

Solve for i explicitly in two steps, exactly as the Neve model does:

```
r0  = 2·r_b + 2·V_n / I                        // small-signal element resistance
i0  = v_s / (R_s + r0)                         // linear seed
f   = 2·r_b·i0 + 2·V_n·artanh(i0/I) − (v_s − R_s·i0)
f'  = 2·r_b + 2·V_n / (I·(1 − (i0/I)²)) + R_s
i   = i0 − f / f'                              // one Newton step
u   = v_s − R_s·i
```

Clamp `|i| < I·(1 − 1e−6)` before the `artanh`, and use two Newton steps when `tg_drive` is above half.
**Estimate:** one step leaves a residual below −120 dB relative to signal over the working range, by the same
cubic-error argument the Neve model uses; the clamp is what keeps it well-behaved when the element is driven
into its asymptote.

**Small-signal resistance**, which is what sets the gain:

```
r_element(I) = 2·r_b + 2·V_n / I
A(I) = r_element / (R_s + r_element)
```

so **gain reduction is monotone in the control current I** and the control law is closed-form invertible. Note
what the r_b term does: **as I rises, the gain stops falling** once 2·V_n/I drops below 2·r_b. That is a
**floor on the achievable gain reduction**, it is a property of breakdown operation, and it is a plausible
mechanism for "not a brick-wall limiter: transients are expected to pass" [12]. Test 16 asserts it.

**Why not just multiply by a gain?** Same answer as the Neve model: because then there is no element, only a
fader, and every audible thing in section 9.2 lives in (G1).

### 11.4 The sidechain, in equations

Per sample, per channel. `x` is the post-element, post-output-ladder signal (feedback, 11.1), read with a
one-sample delay.

```
// 1. mode-dependent detector drive — the two-pole wafer of 6.2
g_sc = G_MODE[tg_mode]                       // compress, out, limit; three values, calibratable

// 2. full-wave rectify, germanium (5.2): soft knee at the low end, NOT a hard abs()
v = softrect(x · g_sc, V_GE)                 // softrect(a,V) = V·log(2·cosh(a/V)) − V·log2

// 3. threshold reference: three germanium drops (5.3)
e = max(0, v − V_REF)                        // V_REF ≈ 3·V_GE_drop, calibratable

// 4. the law network: two segments, four adjust-on-test parts (5.4)
e = LAW_A · e                 if e <= V_LAW
e = LAW_A · V_LAW + LAW_B · (e − V_LAW)   otherwise

// 5. attack: charge the store through R41+D11+R47, one-way (5.5)
if e > s:  s += (e − s) · a_attack          // a_attack from TAU_ATTACK, fixed
else:      s += (e − s) · a_release[tg_recovery, tg_hold]

// 6. control current
I = I_MIN + K_I · s
```

**Four things in that sketch are deliberate and each has a reason in the circuit:**

- **`softrect` rather than `abs`.** Germanium rectification has a soft, low-threshold knee (5.2). A hard
  rectifier is a silicon rectifier and this unit does not have one. `V·log(2·cosh(a/V))` is a smooth full-wave
  rectifier whose knee width is V; it is cheap and it is the right shape.
- **The threshold is a subtraction against a fixed reference, not a comparison against a control.** There is no
  threshold control (5.3).
- **The law is two straight segments, not a curve.** That is what two rows of three resistors builds (5.4), and
  making it a smooth curve would be modelling something EMI did not build.
- **The attack is asymmetric and one-way**, because D11 is in the charge path and D12 is in the discharge path
  and neither conducts backwards (5.5).

**Ripple is not to be smoothed away.** The store is a single capacitor with no second pole, so the control
current carries twice-signal-frequency ripple, and that ripple modulates the gain. On the Neve this is the
mechanism I concluded was behind the published distortion rise [[Neve-33609]]; here there is no published
distortion figure to check against, but the mechanism is the same and it is a large part of "squishy" (9.1).
**Run the sidechain at the oversampled rate and do not filter the ripple out.**

### 11.7 Meter

There is no meter on the hardware's drawing (5.8), so whatever the page shows is an addition and should be
labelled one. If it shows anything, show **gain reduction in dB**, scaled 0 to 20, computed from the element's
instantaneous small-signal gain rather than from an input/output ratio, so that it reads the element and not
the make-up. Publish a second stream carrying the **control current** in microamps, because that is the
quantity the whole circuit is actually about and it makes the two modes legible side by side.

### 11.8 Sample rate, oversampling, stereo, hygiene

- **Oversample 2× by default, 4× available.** The element is a static nonlinearity fed with full-band audio, so
  it aliases. Antiderivative antialiasing is available in principle — the antiderivative of `artanh` is
  elementary — but with the implicit Newton solve in the loop it is fiddly, and 2× plus a decent kernel is the
  better trade for a bus processor. This is the same conclusion the Neve model reached.
- **DC block at the element's input and output.** Non-negotiable: a DC offset at the element biases the arms
  asymmetrically and reintroduces even harmonics that the topology otherwise cancels. Pines makes the same
  point for the whole diode-VCA family [21].
- **Stereo link is a maximum over the linked channels' control currents** (5.7), applied to the current, not to
  the dB value, because the GANG bus is a current bus.
- **Run the sidechain at the oversampled rate** so the ripple is right (11.4).
- **No transformer model.** Deliberately (3.3).
- **Denormals**: flush in the store and the release path, which is where a 2.2-second time constant will park
  values near zero.

### 11.9 What the page should show

- A **module strip**, not a rack face (2.8), with three switches and nothing else in the main view.
- A **transfer curve** with COMPRESS and LIMIT overlaid, because the difference between them is the unit's
  whole personality (6.1) and it is the thing no existing model shows side by side.
- The **control current**, live, in microamps.
- An honest **provenance note**: which controls are EMI's and which are the spoof's (11.3), that the operating
  region is a switch because the drawing is ambiguous (11.3), and that **no measurement of this unit has ever
  been published** (8.1).

## 12. Test plan

**Two standards this repository enforces, and this unit is the hardest case in the lab for both of them.**
Every test must assert a **published figure**, name it, and cite it. Where no real number is reachable, the test
must **say so** and assert a circuit identity or a direction instead of inventing a bound.

For the Neve I could write eleven calibration tests straight out of a factory handbook [19]. **For the TG12413
there is no factory handbook, no specification and no measurement of any kind** (8.1). So the tests below are
divided by *what kind of thing they assert*, and every one of them says which kind it is. There are four kinds:

- **PRINTED** — asserts a figure EMI printed on drawing TG12413-D101 [5]. There are two of these and they are
  the backbone.
- **IDENTITY** — asserts an exact consequence of component values I read off the drawing. Tight tolerances,
  because arithmetic has no tolerance.
- **QUOTED** — asserts a manufacturer's *qualitative* statement, quoted, with the assertion written as a
  direction or an inequality because the source gives no number.
- **CROSS** — asserts a difference from the Neve model under identical input, anchored to a figure published
  about the **Neve** [19], which is a real number even though it is not a number about this unit.

Unless a test says otherwise: 1 kHz sine, `tg_mode` = `compress`, `tg_recovery` = position 3, `tg_output` =
0 dB, `tg_link` off, `tg_region` = `breakdown`, run at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.

### 12.1 Calibration against figures EMI printed

**1. The output-level switch is −10 to +10 dB in one-decibel steps. [PRINTED]**
Sweep `tg_output` across all twenty-one positions with no gain reduction and measure the output change relative
to position 11. Every step must be within **0.10 dB of 1.000 dB**, positions 1, 11 and 21 must read
**−9.95, 0.00 and +9.81 dB ±0.02 dB**, and the full span must be **19.76 ±0.05 dB**.
*Figure:* the legend table printed on the drawing — "S3 … POSITION 1 = −10, 11 = 0, 21 = +10 … **1 dB STEPS**".
*Source:* EMI drawing TG12413-D101 [5]. The exact per-position values come from summing the twenty-one ladder
resistors on the same sheet (3.4). **This is the strongest test in the file**: it checks twenty-one independent
readings of a photographed blueprint against a three-line legend on the same sheet, and the model against both.
If it fails, either the ladder is implemented as decibels instead of resistances (11.5) or a value was misread.

**2. The mode switch has exactly three positions, in the printed order. [PRINTED]**
`tg_mode` must expose **COMPRESS, OUT, LIMIT** and nothing else, with **OUT between the other two**.
*Figure:* the S1 legend table — "POSITION 1 COMPRESS / 2 OUT / 3 LIMIT". *Source:* EMI drawing [5]. Trivial to
pass and worth having, because it is the constraint that stops a ratio control creeping in (9.4).

### 12.2 Circuit identities

**3. The recovery positions stand in the ratios the ladder dictates. [IDENTITY]**
Measure the release time constant at all six positions and normalise to position 1. The six ratios must be
**1 : 2.00 : 4.77 : 9.45 : 19.4 : 47.1**, each within **2 %**.
*Figure:* the six resistor values on switch assembly `TG12413 B204A` — 47 K, 47 K, 130 K, 220 K, 470 K, 1 M3 —
accumulated as in 5.6. *Source:* EMI drawing [5]. **This test asserts ratios and not times, deliberately.**
Waves, who had the console, state that the recovery times are "very hard to put in terms of exact
milliseconds" [12], so **there is no published absolute time to assert and this test does not invent one.**

**4. Output level does not move the gain reduction. [IDENTITY]**
At a fixed input producing 10 dB of gain reduction, sweep `tg_output` across its whole range: the **gain
reduction must change by less than 0.1 dB**.
*Figure:* S3 sits after the gain element in the signal path, and the sidechain is taken from the module output
(11.1). *Source:* the drawing's topology [5]. Circuit identity, so the tolerance is tight. This is the TG's
counterpart to the Neve's make-up test and it must fail loudly if the ladder is accidentally placed inside the
loop.

**5. HOLD is worth a fifth at the fast end and nothing at the slow end. [IDENTITY]**
Sweep `tg_hold` 0 to 1 at recovery position 1: the release time constant must lengthen by **21 % ±1 %**. Repeat
at position 6: it must lengthen by **less than 0.6 %**.
*Figure:* RV1 is 10 kΩ in series with a ladder whose accumulated values are 47 kΩ and 2 214 kΩ at those two
positions. *Source:* EMI drawing [5], arithmetic in 7.4.

**6. The coupling corners are where the capacitors put them. [IDENTITY]**
The model's low-frequency response must be **−3 dB at 4.5 Hz ±1 Hz** from the input coupling and the output
coupling must contribute **less than 0.1 dB at 20 Hz**.
*Figure:* C1 4µ7 into R78 7K5 with R79 3K9; C23 470 µF into a 600 Ω load. *Source:* EMI drawing [5],
arithmetic in 3.2. **This is also a negative test for transformers**: a transformer-coupled model cannot pass
it, because a transformer's low end is neither this flat nor this level-independent (3.3).

**7. Stereo link is a maximum over control currents. [IDENTITY]**
`tg_link` on, a peak on the left channel only: both channels must reduce by the same amount, equal to the left
channel's unlinked reduction within **0.2 dB**.
*Figure:* the GANG bus is a screened control-voltage lead on tag 36 shared between modules; a shared current
bus takes the largest demand. *Source:* EMI drawing [5], reading in 5.7. **Structural, not measured** — EMI
publish no description of the bus and I am inferring its combining rule from its topology.

### 12.3 The gain element

**8. The generalised law contains the Neve's exactly. [IDENTITY — and this is the component-crate test]**
Set `N_JUNCTIONS` = 1, `r_b` = 0 and `V_n` = η·V_T. Then equation (G1) must reproduce
`i = I·tanh(u / 2ηV_T)` — the law derived for the Neve's ring — to within **1 × 10⁻⁹ relative** across
u/k from 0 to 3 and I from 1 µA to 1 mA.
*Figure:* the Neve bridge law, derived in the sibling dossier from Neve drawing EX11475 and validated there
against Neve's own printed −6 dB / −31 dB level annotations, which agree with the derivation to **25.01 dB
against 25.0 dB**. *Source:* [[Neve-33609]], from the AMS Neve technical handbook [19]. **This test is the
whole argument of section 4.9 made executable.** It proves that (G1) is the correct generalisation and that a
crate built on it would have served both units; it also proves that the crate as shipped, with the constant
baked in, cannot serve this one.

**9. Two junctions per arm quarter the third harmonic. [IDENTITY]**
With `tg_region` = `forward`, drive the element alone at a fixed voltage and measure h₃/h₁ with
`N_JUNCTIONS` = 1 and then 2. The ratio of the two must be **4.00 ±0.05**.
*Figure:* for tanh(a·sin θ) the third-harmonic ratio is a²/12, and doubling the thermal scale halves a.
*Source:* derived in 4.6; the a²/12 expansion is established and independently checked in the Neve dossier
[[Neve-33609]]. This is the numerical statement of "same law, different constant".

**10. The element makes odd harmonics and essentially no even ones. [IDENTITY]**
Feed the element alone with a 1 kHz sine at its nominal level, `tg_mismatch` = 0, DC blocked: the **second
harmonic must be at least 40 dB below the third**.
*Figure:* **no published spectrum for this unit exists** (8.1). This test asserts the odd symmetry of
equation (G1), corroborated by Pines' independent finding for the symmetric-bridge family that the model "is an
odd function … therefore, only odd harmonics are present" [21]. **The 40 dB figure is my estimate and is a
numerical-hygiene bound, not a measurement**, and this is stated here explicitly rather than buried.

**11. Mismatch reintroduces even harmonics monotonically. [IDENTITY]**
Sweep `tg_mismatch` 0 to 1: the second harmonic must rise **monotonically** and must exceed the third by the
top of the range.
*Figure:* EMI specify D1/D3 and D2/D4 as matched pairs on two separate drawings and provide two adjust-on-test
resistors to trim the residual. *Source:* [5] [6], and 2.4. **No number is published**; the test asserts
monotonicity and an ordering, not a level.

### 12.4 Dynamics

**12. LIMIT is not a brickwall. [QUOTED + CROSS]**
`tg_mode` = `limit`, input raised from +10 to +20 dBu: the output change must be **greater than 1.0 dB**, and
must be **strictly outside the band 0.1 dB ±0.1 dB**.
*Figures:* Waves, on the module they built with Abbey Road — "this is **not a brick-wall limiter: transients
are expected to pass**" [12] — and, for the band it must fall outside, AMS Neve's published limiter
calibration: "with input level at 10 dBu, increased to +20 dBu the change in output level should be **0.1 dB,
+/−0.1 dB**" [19]. **The 1.0 dB threshold is mine and is labelled an estimate**; the requirement that it fall
outside Neve's published band is anchored to a real manufacturer's figure.

**13. A fast transient passes. [QUOTED]**
`tg_mode` = `limit`, steady tone at the working level, then a 1 ms burst 20 dB above it: the burst's peak at
the output must be **measurably above** the steady-state ceiling.
*Figure:* "transients are expected to pass" [12]. **The source gives no number, so this test asserts a
direction only, and says so.** A model that flattens the burst to the ceiling fails.

**14. OUT leaves the element in circuit. [QUOTED + IDENTITY]**
`tg_mode` = `out` versus `tg_bypass` = true, same input at high `tg_drive`: gain reduction in OUT must be
**0 dB ±0.05 dB**, and the **harmonic content in OUT must be strictly greater than in true bypass**.
*Figure:* Chandler describe their equivalent mode as one that "allows bypassing of the compressor/limiter
threshold **but leaves all circuits in the signal path**" [8], and the mode wafer selects a resistor rather
than opening the path (6.2). *Source:* [8] [5]. **No distortion figure is published for either state**, so the
test asserts an inequality, not a level.

**15. The compress and limit knees are different shapes, not different ratios. [QUOTED]**
Measure the local slope of the transfer curve at 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20 dB above the working point in both modes.
In LIMIT the slope must **increase more steeply with level** than in COMPRESS, and the two curves must
**cross**, not merely scale.
*Figure:* "The knee is **much harder** compared to the compressor" [12]. *Source:* Waves' user guide.
**Qualitative source, so the assertion is about shape, not about numbers.** A model whose two modes differ only
by a multiplier fails.

**16. Gain reduction has a floor. [IDENTITY]**
With `tg_region` = `breakdown`, drive the sidechain to saturation: the gain reduction must **stop increasing**
and settle within 0.5 dB of an asymptote.
*Figure:* equation (G1)'s `2·r_b` term bounds the element's resistance below, so the divider's loss is bounded
(11.2). *Source:* derived. **There is no published maximum gain-reduction figure for this unit**, so the test
asserts that a floor exists and not where it is. With `tg_region` = `forward` and `r_b` = 0 the same test must
**not** find a floor, which is the point.

### 12.5 Tests that distinguish this model from the Neve under the same input

These are the tests the brief asked for and they are the reason both models live in one lab. **Each runs the
same signal into `neve_model` and `tg_model` and asserts that they differ in a stated direction.** Where the
anchor is a published figure it is a figure published about the Neve [19], because the Neve is the unit that
has any.

**17. Distortion moves the opposite way with gain reduction. [CROSS]**
Same 1 kHz input, both models, sweep the input from the onset of gain reduction to 20 dB of it. Measure h₃/h₁
at each point. The **Neve's must fall** and the **TG's must rise**, and the sign of the two slopes must be
opposite over at least 12 dB of the sweep.
*Figure:* for the Neve, the derived result that the bridge's nonlinearity depends on the voltage across it,
which falls as control current rises, corroborated by Pines: the harmonics "fall off at approximately **40 dB
per decade** as the gain parameter approaches zero" [21]. *Source:* [21] and [[Neve-33609]]. For the TG, the
opposite sign follows from a knee-shaped element being driven harder as the sidechain works (4.7). **This is
the single most important cross test** and it is the one a listener can hear on a sustained note with a level
ramp.

**18. The Neve holds a step and the TG does not. [CROSS]**
Both models, limiter engaged, input raised from +10 to +20 dBu. The Neve model must produce **0.1 dB ±0.1 dB**;
the TG model must produce **more than 1 dB**.
*Figure:* AMS Neve's published Limit Ratio specification, quoted in test 12 [19], against Waves' statement that
the TG is not a brickwall [12]. **The Neve half of this test asserts a manufacturer's measured figure with the
manufacturer's own tolerance.**

**19. The release ranges differ by three to one. [CROSS]**
Measure the ratio of the slowest to the fastest release position on each model. The Neve's compress recovery
must span **15 : 1** (100, 400, 800, 1500 ms); the TG's must span **47 : 1**.
*Figures:* AMS Neve's published compress recovery times [19]; the TG's six accumulated ladder resistances [5].
**Both halves are anchored, one to a specification and one to resistor values.**

**20. The Neve's gain reduction has two slopes and the TG's has one. [CROSS]**
Feed both models the same dense programme at a level that engages the Neve's compressor and limiter
simultaneously. Fit the Neve's gain-reduction trace: it must show a **detectable slope change** where the
limiter takes over. The TG's must not.
*Figure:* the 33609's handbook describes a combiner giving "a low output impedance signal **equal to the larger
of** the compressor or limiter sidechain signals" [19]; the TG has one detector and a mode switch (6.1).
*Source:* [19] and [5]. **Structural on the TG side** — there is no published TG trace — and asserted as an
absence, which is the honest form.

**21. Level-dependent low-frequency behaviour: the Neve has it, the TG must not. [CROSS]**
30 Hz sine into both models at 0 dBu and at +20 dBu, no gain reduction. The TG's harmonic ratios at the two
levels must agree within **0.5 dB**; the Neve's must not.
*Figure:* the Neve has four transformers in the audio path [19] [20] and the TG has none (3.3). *Source:* the
two drawings. **This test exists to catch a transformer model being added to the TG for warmth**, which is the
most likely way this model will go wrong (9.4).

**22. Onset is softer on the TG. [CROSS]**
Ramp the input slowly through the onset of gain reduction on both models. The TG's first decibel of gain
reduction must be spread over a **wider input range** than the Neve's.
*Figure:* **no measurement of either onset is published.** This test asserts the consequence of a circuit
difference — the TG rectifies with eight OA202 germanium diodes and references its threshold to three more
(5.2, 5.3), where the Neve's sidechain is silicon [19] — and it is labelled structural. It is included because
it is audible and excluded from any numeric claim because nothing supports one.

### 12.6 What I will not test, what would unlock each one, and how hard it would be

Eight things. **Every entry says what evidence would let it be tested**, because a list of gaps that only says
"nothing is published" is a dead end, and a list that says where the number lives is a job somebody can take.

Two documents and one object would unlock most of the list, so they are worth naming once here:

- **The EMI item list for TG12413.** The drawing points at it in four places and it is not in the archive item
  (3.5). It would give what an HS2051 is, the nominal values of all ten adjust-on-test parts, and the
  transistor grades. **It unlocks four of the eight entries below** and is the single highest-value missing
  document in this dossier. It would be looked for in the same EMI drawing series as `JL11440` [1], or with
  whoever holds the rest of the Leimseider material.
- **A photograph of a TG12413 board with component bodies readable.** The assembly drawing [6] gives positions
  but not markings. A board photograph would identify the store capacitor and probably the diodes.
- **A real module, or a Chandler TG1 with its added stages accounted for.** Section 8.4 lists the three probe
  measurements that would close the largest questions; the entries below say which test each one unlocks.

**(a) Attack time.** Chandler say it is fixed [8]; Waves decline to give a number [12]; EMI print nothing [5];
my own RC arithmetic gives an upper bound of 47 ms that I do not believe, because the charge path is probably
current-driven rather than resistance-driven (5.5). **There is no figure to assert and I will not invent one.**
*What would unlock it:* a step response on a real module, using a stated definition — AMS Neve's is usable and
is published, "the time taken for a signal to return to within 1 dB of its original value, using an increase in
input level of 10 dB" [19], and borrowing their procedure would make the two units directly comparable. Failing
a module, **the item list** would give the AOT 1 and AOT 2 values and settle whether VT17 is a current source,
which is the whole question. *Difficulty:* easy with hardware, impossible without.

**(b) Absolute recovery times in milliseconds.** Test 3 asserts the six ratios instead, which are primary and
which the resistor ladder fixes exactly (7.2). The absolutes need the store capacitor identified and I could
not identify it from the drawing (7.3).
*What would unlock it:* **two measurements**, the release time at position 1 and at position 6 on a real
module. Two numbers convert the ratio anchor into an absolute one and simultaneously confirm or refute the
47 : 1 span. Alternatively a **board photograph** showing whether C10, C18 or C16 sits in the discharge path;
alternatively the item list. *Difficulty:* easy with hardware. This is the cheapest high-value measurement on
the list and I would do it first.

**(c) Threshold in dBu.** There is no threshold control and no published threshold; the reference is three
germanium diodes and a hand-aligned network (5.3, 5.4).
*What would unlock it:* a **static transfer curve** on a real module in COMPRESS — input level against output
level, everything else fixed — from which the knee point in dBu reads straight off. That one sweep would also
give the law's two segments (5.4) and would let tests 15 and 22 assert numbers instead of shapes. Failing
hardware, an **EMI alignment procedure** would do it, because AOT 3 to AOT 6 must have been set against a
stated level; no such procedure is in the archive item and I do not know whether one survives.
*Difficulty:* easy with hardware, and it is the measurement that would improve the model most.

**(d) Maximum gain reduction in dB.** Nothing published. Test 16 asserts that a floor exists, not where it is.
*What would unlock it:* either driving a real module into saturation and reading the meter, **or** finding out
what an HS2051 is. The floor is `2·r_b` in equation (G1) (11.2), so a datasheet giving the device's dynamic
resistance in its operating region would settle it **analytically, with no hardware at all**. That makes the
item list, or any EMI or trade cross-reference for the type number, worth real effort. *Difficulty:* easy with
hardware; possible without, if the part can be identified.

**(e) Total harmonic distortion at any stated level.** **Nothing is published for this unit** (8.1). Chandler's
2 % figure describes a deliberate distortion mode on a transformer-coupled rack unit with the threshold
bypassed [8] and is not a measurement of a TG12413. Using it as a target would be exactly the failure the
repository's standard exists to prevent.
*What would unlock it:* a **distortion-against-level sweep at fixed gain reduction, in both modes**, on a real
module. This is the third of the three probes in 8.4 and it is worth more than the number it produces, because
the *sign of its slope* settles cross test 17 — whether this unit's distortion rises with drive where the
Neve's falls — which is the sharpest audible difference between the two models and is currently asserted from
circuit reasoning alone. *Difficulty:* easy with hardware. Highest value of the three probes.

> **Withdrawn after the build measured it.** The prediction above, that this unit's distortion rises
> with drive where the Neve's falls, does not follow and the model does not reproduce it. Over the
> same sweep, from the onset of reduction to 20 dB of it, this unit's third harmonic moves +15.8 dB
> and the Neve's +5.9 dB. **Both rise.**
>
> The circuit reasoning was wrong rather than the measurement. Both units are the same shape: a
> nonlinear element as the shunt of a divider with a series arm. Expanding to the cubic term gives
> the third-harmonic ratio as proportional to `(1 − g)·û²`, so the sign of the trend is set by what
> the loop does to the voltage across the element, which is the loop's ratio, not by which element
> sits in the divider. Two different elements in the same topology give the same sign.
>
> **This does not contradict the Neve dossier, and the reconciliation is worth stating.** That file's
> claim is causal: its distortion is set by the voltage across its bridge and *not* by how much gain
> reduction is happening. Its own test measures that at fixed input, on the bridge alone, and passes.
> Test 17 turned a causal claim into a prediction about a sweep, and a sweep to deeper gain reduction
> moves the drive and the reduction together. It cannot isolate either. That is the same two-variable
> error that put a false distortion trend into the API 2500 dossier, arriving here from the other
> direction.
>
> What the build asserts instead is the thing that is true of this element and is what a listener
> hears: this unit runs −73.5, −69.0, −66.0, −62.3, −57.7 dBc against the Neve's −90.4, −85.7, −85.5,
> −85.4, −84.5, a gap of 16.9 to 26.8 dB across the sweep. The hardware probe above is still worth
> having; it would now be measuring the magnitude rather than settling a sign.

**(f) Noise.** Nothing published, and nothing on the drawing determines it, because the noise floor is set by
the input stage's device grades and those are in the missing item list.
*What would unlock it:* a residual noise measurement at the output with the input terminated, taken in OUT and
again in COMPRESS, which would also show how much of the noise the gain element contributes. *Difficulty:*
easy with hardware, impossible without. Lowest value on this list: the lab has never modelled a noise floor
from a measurement and there is no reason to start here.

**(g) Frequency response beyond the coupling corners.** Test 6 covers the low end, which the coupling capacitors
determine and the drawing therefore fixes. The top end is not published.
*What would unlock it:* a swept response on a real module. **Partially unlockable from the drawing alone**,
without hardware, by tracing what C8 and C26 (100 pF), C21 (33 pF) and C22 (2n7) actually compensate and
computing the resulting corner. **I did not do that work** — I read those parts as stability compensation and
moved on (9.4) — and it is a bounded, purely desk-based job for anyone who wants it. *Difficulty:* a few hours
at the same crops I already have.

**(h) Anything about the TG12345 console channel's limiter**, as opposed to the module on TG12413-D101, because
I could not establish that they are the same circuit (1.3). Chandler's chronology separates "the 1969 TG12345
console channel" from "the 1974 TG12413" [9], which suggests ancestry rather than identity.
*What would unlock it:* **a TG12345 channel schematic.** The archive item does not contain one and I could not
find one anywhere. The restored Mk II console physically exists, was auctioned in October 2024 [13] [14], and
was restored over four years by people who must have had drawings; EMI's own records are the other place to
look. If such a sheet surfaced, comparing its gain element with 4.2 would settle the question in an afternoon,
and it would also tell us whether the "Zener" reading (4.3, 4.8) holds for the recording desk as well as the
mastering one. *Difficulty:* an archival search, not a technical one, and the most likely of these eight to be
resolved by somebody who is not me.

## 13. References

### 13.1 How this list compares with the Neve's, and what that means

The Neve dossier lists **sixty-seven** numbered sources. This one lists **twenty-eight**, and the gap is not
laziness — it is the finding of section 8.1 expressed as a bibliography. The Neve is a product that has been
manufactured continuously since 1969 by a company that publishes technical handbooks; the TG12413 is a module
a corporate research laboratory built for its employer's own studios and never sold [10]. There is one primary
document and there are three companies talking about it, and that is the whole of it.

**Everything below was fetched during this work unless the entry says otherwise.** Two entries say otherwise
and both are flagged.

A few notes on how things were found, since several were not findable by guessing:

- **Nothing in the archive item is addressable by name.** The item `JL11440` [1] has no per-sheet identifiers,
  so the pages were located by pulling the metadata endpoint [2], then the whole-item OCR text [3] to find the
  strings "TG 12413 B 205A" and "4xHS2051", then the **per-page** OCR [4] to work out which of the seventeen
  page indices each module lived on. Only then were the sheets rendered at full resolution.
- **The sibling sheets in the same item** — the TG12414 filter unit [26] and the TG12412 tone control unit
  boards [27] — were read only far enough to confirm the module numbering that identifies the parent console
  (1.3). The TG12414 sheet independently corroborates Waves' published filter frequencies, which is a small but
  real cross-check on how faithfully Waves modelled this family.
- **`waves.com` sits behind Incapsula**, and a plain `curl` of the user-guide PDF path returns HTTP 302 to
  itself indefinitely. It downloads correctly with a cookie jar and a referer [12].
- **Chandler publish no manual for the TG1 Limiter or the Zener Limiter.** Their manuals index [23] carries
  twenty-seven PDFs including manuals for the Curve Bender [28] and the TG12345 MKIV EQ, and **neither limiter
  is among them**. The TG1 page links to a "TG1 Limiter Manual" whose target is not a PDF URL I could resolve.
  So Chandler's specification table [8] is the whole of what they publish about it.
- **The photographs were found through the Wikimedia Commons search API** [24], because the two usable images
  of the console are filed under titles no reasonable guess would produce.
- **Sound On Sound is unreachable for this unit**, as it was for most units in the survey [22]: every review
  slug I constructed returns HTTP 410.

**What I fetched and could not use.** Chandler's rear-panel photograph of the TG1 [25] shows connectors only.
The Wikipedia article on Abbey Road Studios, fetched at the same time as [13], gave nothing the TG12345 article did not. The Waves product
page's structured data [11] carries latency and platform tables and no acoustic figures.

## 14. Summary, for anyone who reads one section

- **The primary document exists and it is good.** EMI drawing TG12413-D101, one sheet, legible, with every
  switch ladder drawn out and two legend tables printed on it [5]. It is the transfer-console limiter, the same
  module Waves modelled and Chandler recreated (1.3).
- **The gain element is four HS2051 diodes in two matched pairs, arranged as two branches of two in series,
  with their cathodes at the +20 V rail** (4.1, 4.2, 4.3). It is not a ring. As drawn it runs in reverse
  breakdown, and EMI's own "Zener Limiter" product line, Chandler's "rarely seen diode network" and Waves'
  three separate references to a "Zener diode" all agree with that reading (4.8).
- **The shared diode-bridge component crate does not serve this unit** (4.9). Under the most generous reading
  it would need the thermal scale doubled, because there are two junctions per arm instead of one; under the
  reading the drawing supports, the law is not a hyperbolic tangent at all. What should have been shared is
  equation (G1), `u = 2·r_b·i + 2·V_n·artanh(i/I)`, which contains both units as special cases and which test 8
  checks.
- **There are no transformers in this module** (3.3), where the Neve has four. Chandler's rack recreation adds
  them and says so in its specification [8]; a model of the EMI module must not.
- **No measurement of this unit has ever been published, by anybody** (8.1). The two calibration anchors are
  both derived from resistor values on EMI's own drawing: the twenty-one-step output ladder, which reproduces
  the printed one-decibel legend to 0.09 dB (3.4), and the six-position recovery ladder, which fixes the
  positions at 1 : 2.00 : 4.77 : 9.45 : 19.4 : 47.1 (7.2). Everything else in section 12 asserts a circuit
  identity, a quoted qualitative statement, or a difference from the Neve anchored to a Neve figure.
- **Against the Neve, a listener should hear**: distortion that rises rather than falls as the unit works
  harder, a limiter that lets transients through where the Neve holds them to a tenth of a decibel, a release
  range three times wider, one detector rather than two fighting over the element, and a bottom end with no
  transformer in it (9.2).
