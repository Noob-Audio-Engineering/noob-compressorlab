# The API 2500: research notes for the switchable-topology VCA side of `noob-compressorlab`

Research dossier for the API 2500 model of the `noob-compressorlab` example plug-in of
noob-vst-webgui-framework. The example is a humorous, affectionate spoof of the API 2500 stereo bus
compressor. It is not a product, it is not a parity replacement for anything, and it does not use
the API, Automated Processes, 2500, 2500+ or THRUST names as its own name: the faceplate will carry
NOOB names, and the trademarks below belong to their owners and are used only to identify the
devices discussed, because this is research and research has to name things. This model sits behind
the same per-instance `model` switch that already selects the 1176, LA-2A, LA-3A, CL 1B, 6176,
Distressor and Neve behaviours; see [[SSL-Gbus]], [[dbx-160]] and [[Distressor]], which are the
lab's other VCA units and the ones section 12 argues against sharing with.

Conventions, kept the same as the other dossiers so they all read alike:

- Citations are `[n]`; the numbered list in section 14 gives the URL for every source, and
  reference-style link definitions at the very end make the `[n]` markers clickable.
- Numbers that come from a manufacturer specification, a manual, a patent, a datasheet or a
  published measurement are attributed. Numbers that are my own arithmetic are labelled **derived**;
  numbers that are my own guess are labelled **estimate**. Nothing labelled a measurement was
  invented. Where sources disagree, both figures are given and the disagreement is stated rather
  than resolved.
- "GR" is gain reduction. "THD" is total harmonic distortion. "CV" is the sidechain control voltage.
  "VCA" is a voltage-controlled amplifier. dBu is 0.775 V RMS, dBV is 1 V RMS, dBFS is digital full
  scale. "FF" is feedforward, "FB" is feedback.
- Where I measured something off an image I say which image, and section 14 gives the URL it came
  from so any measurement here can be re-derived from its source. The images themselves are **not**
  in this repository: they are API's own photographs, manual scans and a patent sheet, this
  repository is public, and a measurement that cannot be re-checked from its cited source is a fault
  in this file rather than a reason to commit the image. Where the image is a vector drawing I say so,
  because a number read out of a PDF's own path coordinates is a different kind of number from one
  read off a photograph, and section 2 keeps them apart.
- API spell the unit "2500" in running text and "25OO" (with letter O) in the older manual's
  headings. I use 2500 throughout. The current product is the **2500+**; where a fact differs
  between the two I say which unit it belongs to.

**Why this unit is worth the trouble, in one paragraph.** Every compressor in the lab has its
detector wired one way and leaves it there. The 2500 puts the choice on the front panel. Its TYPE
switch moves the detector's input between a split of the unit input and the output of the make-up
amplifier, which is not a voicing option but a change of topology: it turns the compressor's static
curve, its effective ratio, its knee and its ballistics into different functions of the same knob
settings, and it puts the make-up gain control inside the control loop. Nothing else in the lab does
that, and nothing else in the lab can teach it. Everything else here — the tilt filter in front of
the detector, the true-RMS chip, the seven-step ratio ladder — is a second reason rather than the
reason.

**The headline results, stated up front so the rest of the file can be checked against them.**

1. **THRUST is 10 dB per decade, which is 3.01 dB per octave, and it is a tilt rather than a
   high-pass.** The survey recorded "2 dB/octave on Med and 4 dB/octave on Loud" [17]. That figure
   comes from *Sound On Sound* [14] and it is wrong in both numbers and in kind. API's own manuals
   say "a slope of 10dB per decade (-3dB/8va)" [3] and "down 15 dB at 20 Hz and up 15dB at 20K Hz"
   [2]; the circuit is **US Patent 5,170,437** [8], which claims "approximately +10 decibels per
   decade" and prints the resistor and capacitor values; and those values, evaluated, give
   **9.77 dB/decade**. Three independent statements and one arithmetic check, all agreeing, against
   one review sentence. Section 8.
2. **The OLD position taps the detector after the make-up amplifier, so make-up gain is inside the
   loop.** API's own block diagram shows the feedback tap on the 2520 output node [4], and Hugh
   Robjohns found the behaviour on the bench: "in the feed-backwards mode, the level sensing is
   derived from after the make-up gain control... increasing the make-up gain results in even
   further compression" [14]. That single fact makes make-up gain a threshold control in OLD, makes
   the auto make-up circuit a second loop around the first, and means **the seven printed ratios
   cannot be correct in both TYPE positions**. Section 6.
3. **The auto make-up gain formula is recoverable, and its extreme reproduces a published number
   exactly.** API say auto make-up "applies make-up gain based upon the settings of the RATIO and
   THRESHOLD controls" [3], that it drives the output toward **+4 dBu** [2], that THRESHOLD bottoms
   at **−20 dBu** [2] [3], and that the manual GAIN pot runs **0 to +24 dB** [3] [14]. The standard
   auto make-up law, `M = (1 − 1/R)·(4 − T)`, at its extreme gives `1 × (4 − (−20)) = 24 dB`, which
   is the manual pot's range to the decibel. Four published numbers, three of which predict the
   fourth. Section 3.6.

**And the thing I want to flag before section 2, because it is the failure this repository keeps
guarding against.** API's own specification says the box is `19" X 1.75" X 10.5" deep` [1] [4].
API's own recall chart draws a panel whose vector outline has an aspect ratio of **11.175 : 1**,
which for a 19-inch width implies a height of **1.700 inches** [5]. API's own product photograph
gives **10.919 : 1**, implying **1.740 inches** [6]. The photograph agrees with the specification to
0.6 % and the recall chart is 2.9 % short. So the recall chart is the authority for *where things
are* and the specification is the authority for *how tall the panel is*, and section 2.2 says which
number came from which and does not average them.

## 1. What this unit is, its variants, and what I could not reach

### 1.0 Where the documents are, and what I could not reach

The survey ranked this unit below the SSL bus compressor and gave two reasons: "API publishes no
schematic, and `apiaudio.com` refused a TLS handshake from this machine so I could not reach API's
own manual" [17]. **The second reason has evaporated and the first one matters less than it
sounds.**

**`apiaudio.com` answers now, without the `www`.** `https://apiaudio.com/` returns HTTP 200.
`https://www.apiaudio.com/` still fails with exactly the error the survey recorded, `schannel: next
InitializeSecurityContext failed: SEC_E_ILLEGAL_MESSAGE`. So the host that refuses the handshake is
the `www` alias, not the apex domain, and every document below came off the apex. That is worth
recording as a technique rather than as a fact about API: **when a hostname refuses a TLS handshake,
try it without the `www` before concluding the site is unreachable.** The survey's own conclusion
that "API's own 2500 manual is unread" [17] was true of the path it tried and false of the site.

**What that opened.** API publish, for three separate products, a user manual, a specification-and-
block-diagram sheet and a recall chart:

| Document | Unit | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| `2500_user_18-06-18.pdf` [2] | original 2500 | Eleven pages. The THRUST prose with the ±15 dB numbers, **plotted THRUST response curves**, **plotted knee curves**, two topology diagrams, the control-by-control description, the TILT trim, the patent number |
| `2500+_user_23-01-09.pdf` [3] | 2500+ | Thirty pages. Every control, the "10dB per decade (-3dB/8va)" sentence, the BLEND/MIX section, the quick-start defaults |
| `2500-50th_user_2019-07-18.pdf` [7] | 50th Anniversary | The intermediate model, which is where BLEND first appears |
| `2500+_Specs-n-Block.pdf` [4] | 2500+ | The full specification **and API's own block diagram**, which is the closest thing to a circuit document that exists for this box |
| `2500_setup.pdf` [5] | 2500+ | The recall chart: **a vector line drawing of the whole front panel** with every legend and every detent dot |
| `setup2500.pdf` [9] | original 2500 | The original's recall chart |

**And the patent is the real prize.** The original manual prints `US Patent 5,170,437` in the corner
of the THRUST plot page [2]. That is **"Audio signal energy level detection method and apparatus",
Chris N. Strahm, assigned to Audio Teknology Inc. of Portland, Oregon, filed 17 October 1990,
granted 8 December 1992** [8]. It describes the THRUST filter, prints two schematics of it with
every component value, and states its slope. API's own manuals credit the circuit to ATI's Paragon
consoles [2] [3] and *Sound On Sound* records that API had joined the ATI Group two years before the
review [14], so the provenance closes: the patent is the parent company's, the product is API's, and
the numbers in the patent are the numbers in API's manual. Section 8 does the arithmetic.

**The two active parts publish full datasheets.** *Sound On Sound* opened the unit and reported
"four 2180 VCAs and one 2252 level detector per channel" [14]. THAT Corporation publish the **2180
series datasheet** [10] and the **2252 datasheet** [11], both twelve pages with complete electrical
characteristics, and they publish **Design Note 115** [12], a full 2180-plus-2252 compressor
reference design with a schematic. That design note is not the API 2500's schematic and I do not
treat it as one, but it is the chip maker's own account of how the threshold, the ratio and the knee
get built out of these two parts, and sections 5, 6 and 7 lean on it as such.

**What I could not reach, said plainly.**

- **No API schematic, for any 2500.** API publish a block diagram [4] and nothing below it. Every
  component-level statement in this file comes from the patent [8], from THAT's datasheets and
  design note [10] [11] [12], or is labelled an inference. There is no equivalent here of the SSL's
  card drawings or the Neve's blueprint set.
- **`patents.google.com` returns HTTP 503**, exactly as the SSL dossier found [SSL-Gbus 1.0]. The
  patent came from `patentimages.storage.googleapis.com` instead, which answers normally. That is
  the second reusable technique this section has produced: **the Google Patents front end and the
  Google Patents PDF bucket are separate services and the bucket is the reliable one.**
- **`apiaudio.com/docs/manuals/` and `/docs/files/` return HTTP 403** on the directory itself, though
  every file inside them downloads. So the manual list cannot be enumerated; each product page has
  to be fetched to learn its own filenames.
- **WebSearch was unavailable for this entire session** — the budget was exhausted before I started,
  on the first call. Every document here was found by constructing a URL, by walking API's own
  sitemap and product-page links, or by following a citation out of a document already in hand.
- **No independent laboratory measurement of an API 2500**, of any revision. No THD figure, no
  ballistics measurement, no ratio calibration, no frequency-response plot from anyone but API. This
  is the category-wide gap the survey identified [17] and it is still there. It bites harder here
  than it did on the SSL, because API's own specification does not print a THD figure either (10.1).
- **No photograph or drawing of the 2500's circuit boards** beyond Robjohns' prose, so the part
  identifications in [14] are one person's reading and I cite them as that.
- **No API statement of the attack or release measurement procedure**, no tolerances on any timing
  figure, and no statement of what "0.03 ms attack" is the attack *to*. The Neve handbook gives all
  three [Neve-33609 8]; API give none. Section 9 and section 13 both say what that costs.

### 1.1 API, ATI, and where the 2500 comes from

Automated Processes Incorporated started in 1968 building broadcast and recording consoles, and its
technical credits include the first computerised console fader automation (1973) and the first
programmable console with automation of EQ, aux sends, pans and faders (1974), the system that
became Total Recall [14]. The company is in Jessup, Maryland [2] [3].

The part that matters for this dossier is that **API joined the ATI Group around 1999** [14]. ATI —
Audio Teknology Incorporated, of Portland, Oregon — made the Paragon live console and, in Robjohns'
words, "a range of audio processors based around proprietary high-voltage preamp designs and **a
patented compressor circuit**" [14]. That patented circuit is THRUST, patent 5,170,437 [8], and the
2500 is the first API box to carry it. API's own manuals say so: the THRUST feature "has been used
for many years in the famed ATI Paragon and Paragon II consoles, as well as the Pro-6 Input Strip"
[2] [3].

So the 2500 is a 2001 product that is half API and half ATI, and its most interesting circuit is the
half that is not API's. That is not a criticism; it is the reason the primary document for its most
interesting circuit exists at all.

### 1.2 The three variants, and which one to model

| | **2500** | **2500 50th Anniversary** | **2500+** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reviewed / dated | *Sound On Sound* October 2001 [14]; manual rev. 18-06-18 [2] | manual rev. 2019-07-18 [7] | manual rev. 23-01-09, ©2019 [3] |
| Threshold range | **+10 dBu to −20 dBu** [2] [14] | +20 dBu to −20 dBu [7] | **+20 dBu to −20 dBu** [3] |
| VCAs per channel | "each channel used **three** VCA's" [2]; "**four** 2180 VCAs" [14] | "multiple VCA's" [7] | "multiple VCA's" [3] |
| BLEND / MIX | **no** | **yes**, introduced here [7] | yes [3] |
| TILT trim | **yes**, ±2 dB screwdriver trim right of the meters [2] [14] | — | **not in the manual** [3] |
| Link SHAPE labels | HI-pass / LOW-pass / BAND-pass [2] | FAST / SLOW / BOTH [7] | FAST / SLOW / BOTH [3] |
| Rotary controls | seven [14] | eight | eight [5] |
| Everything else | identical | identical | identical |

Three things follow.

**The variants differ in exactly two respects that change the model**: the threshold range, and the
presence of the output blend. Everything in sections 3 through 9 — the topology, the gain element,
the detector, the THRUST filter, the knee, the seven ratios, the seven attack times, the six release
times plus variable, the link percentage ladder — is common to all three, and both manuals use word
for word the same paragraphs to describe them [2] [3] [7].

**The link SHAPE relabelling is a relabelling, not a redesign, and the two labels agree once you
notice what the filter filters.** The original calls the three settings a high-pass, a low-pass and
both-at-once making a band-pass [2]; the current one calls them FAST (peak), SLOW (RMS) and BOTH
[3]. These describe the same switch because the filter is in the **control-voltage** link, not in
the audio: a high-pass on an envelope passes its fast movement, which is its peak content, and a
low-pass passes its slow movement, which is its RMS content. Both manuals are right and neither is
clearer than the other, so section 11 uses API's own words for the panel and the original's words
for the circuit.

**Model the 2500+ panel with a `2500` variant switch**, for the same reason the SSL dossier draws
the 500-series module [SSL-Gbus 2.5]: it is the version API still sell, API publish a
high-resolution photograph and a dimensioned vector recall chart of it, and it carries the whole
control set. Then let the threshold range and the presence of BLEND follow the variant switch, and
say so in the plug-in's help text.

### 1.3 Why it is famous, and what it is actually used for

The 2500 is a mix-bus and mastering compressor rather than a channel compressor. Robjohns found it
"worked very well in a broad range of applications involving everything from brickwall protective
limiting to gentle mastering compression", called it "flexible, creative and capable with a very
distinctive musical quality", and made exactly one design criticism, which was the absence of a
programme-dependent release [14]. API describe it as their "flagship compressor" [3].

Its reputation rests on THRUST more than on anything else. Robjohns, who had every reason to be
sceptical of a marketing name, wrote that "the Thrust control came as something of a surprise. At
its Loud setting, the extra punch created was impressive and seemed to work very well with a wide
variety of material, lifting the attack and weight of rhythm instruments in a totally different way
to conventional EQ" [14]. That is a reviewer changing his mind in print, and it is the single most
useful sentence anyone has written about this box, because it says the effect is real and says it is
not an EQ.

## 2. Controls and the front panel, with measured geometry

### 2.1 The controls, and what each one really does

Eight rotary controls, nine push switches, sixteen indicator LEDs, two VU meters and a power switch,
on one rack unit. Everything below is API's own description [2] [3] unless marked otherwise.

| control | type | range / values | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THRESH** | continuous pot, 11 detent dots | **+20 to −20 dBu** (2500+, 50th) [3] [7]; **+10 to −20 dBu** (original) [2] [14] | Sets where compression begins. It also sets the auto make-up gain (3.6), so on this box the threshold knob is two controls in one. A red LED above it lights when the input crosses it. |
| **ATTACK** | 7-position rotary switch | **.03, .1, .3, 1, 3, 10, 30 ms** [2] [3] | Seven fixed times. API's original manual spells the fastest one out as "30 microseconds" [2]. |
| **RATIO** | 7-position rotary switch | **1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 10, ∞ : 1** [2] [3] | API gloss ∞ as "INF:1 **or above 20:1**" [2], which is the only hint anywhere that the infinity position is finite. |
| **RELEASE** | 7-position rotary switch | **.05, .1, .2, .5, 1, 2 s**, then **VARIABLE** [2] [3] | The seventh position hands control to the VARIABLE pot; it is not a seventh time. |
| **VARIABLE** | continuous pot, 11 detent dots | **50 ms to 3 s** [2] [3] | Live only when RELEASE is fully clockwise. |
| **KNEE** | 3-position push switch, 3 LEDs | HARD (red), MED (yellow), SOFT (green) [3] | Shapes the transition into compression. Section 7. |
| **THRUST** | 3-position push switch, 3 LEDs | LOUD (red), MED (yellow), NORM (green) [3] | Inserts a tilt filter ahead of the RMS detector. Section 8. |
| **TYPE** | 2-position push switch, 2 LEDs | NEW / F FWD (yellow), OLD / F BACK (green) [3] | Moves the detector's input. Section 6. **This is the unit's reason to exist.** |
| **L/R LINK** | 7-position rotary switch | **IND, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100 %** [2] [3] | Mixes the two channels' control voltages in a set proportion. Both detectors stay live at every setting [2]. |
| **SHAPE** | 3-position push switch, 2 LEDs | FAST (peak), SLOW (RMS), BOTH — original labels HI-pass, LOW-pass, BAND-pass [2] [3] | Filters the **link** control voltage only, not the audio and not the channel's own CV. |
| **MANUAL** | latching push switch, red cap | — | Out: auto make-up. In: the GAIN pot. |
| **GAIN** | continuous pot, red cap, 11 detent dots | **0 to +24 dB** [3] [14] | Manual make-up. Live only when MANUAL is engaged. |
| **BLEND** | 3-position push switch, 2 LEDs | off, **X** crossfade, **‖** parallel [3] | 2500+ and 50th only. |
| **MIX** | continuous pot, blue cap, 11 dots, **detent at 50 %** [3] | 0 to 100 % | 2500+ and 50th only. Live only when BLEND is engaged. |
| **IN** | latching push switch, green cap | — | "a soft IN/out button that defeats the compression action silently, but allows the signal to pass through" [2] [3]. |
| **BYP** | latching push switch, red cap | — | "a relay hard-wire bypass that routes the signal directly from the INPUT XLR to the OUTPUT XLR, **without going through any electronics**", and it engages by itself if the power fails [2] [3]. |
| **METER** | 3-position push switch, 3 LEDs | GR (red), OUT (yellow), IN (green) [3] | Meter source. "It is a silent function and is isolated so the VU selection does not affect or load the signal in any way" [2]. |
| **TILT** | screwdriver trim, ±2 dB | original 2500 only [2] [14] | "a trim of the compression control voltage, allowing tilting of the compression left or right... This control adjusts about 2 dB in either direction, and **DOES NOT affect the uncompressed signal**" [2]. Section 5.6 says what it is for. |
| **VU meters** | two moving-coil | VU scale −20…+3, plus a red **G/R** scale 20…0 | "0 VU is +4dBu" and the gain-reduction scale reads "from 0 (no gain reduction) to 20dB", with 0 at the far right [2] [3]. |

**Three of those deserve a sentence on their own.**

**BYP is a real bypass and IN is not.** This is the opposite of the SSL, where the IN switch removes
the sidechain but leaves the VCA and the make-up gain in circuit [SSL-Gbus 2.1]. Here API give the
user both: a soft IN that stops the compression and a relay BYP that takes the electronics out of
circuit altogether. That matters for section 11, because it means the plug-in's own honest bypass
and the hardware's BYP are the same object and do not need two parameters.

**The compressor can pull more gain reduction than the meter can show.** "The range of the meter is
from 0 (no gain reduction) to 20 dB of gain reduction. **The compressor can compress up to 30 dB of
reduction**" [2]. That is a published figure with no conditions attached, and it is the only
published statement of this box's gain-reduction range.

**The TILT trim carries a warning.** "WARNING: Adjusting this control will likely require your unit
to be returned to the API factory to be re-calibrated!!" [2]. A ±2 dB user trim that voids the
factory calibration is a strange thing to put on a front panel, and section 5.6 argues it is there
because THAT's own datasheet says it has to be.

### 2.2 The front panel, measured

### 2.3 Knob sweeps, detent angles, and the dial laws

Every angle below is measured from the vector drawing [5], taking twelve o'clock as zero and
clockwise as positive.

**Two ring geometries, and only two.**

- **Seven-position switches** (ATTACK, RATIO, RELEASE, L/R LINK): seven dots at **−90°, −60°, −30°,
  0°, +30°, +60°, +90°**. A **180° sweep, 30° per detent**. Dot ring radius 0.0184–0.0187 W.
- **Continuous pots** (THRESH, VARIABLE, GAIN, MIX): eleven dots at **−150°, −120°, −90°, −60°,
  −30°, 0°, +30°, +60°, +90°, +120°, +150°**. A **300° sweep, 30° per dot**. Dot ring radius
  0.0149 W.

That is the whole geometry. It is worth stating plainly because it means a faceplate needs exactly
two knob widgets, not eight.

**THRESH is exactly linear in decibels.** Six of its eleven dots carry printed values, and every one
falls on a straight line:

| dot angle | −150° | −60° | 0° | +30° | +90° | +150° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| printed | **+20** | **+8** | **0** | **−4** | **−12** | **−20** |
| linear law, 4 dB per 30° dot | +20 | +8 | 0 | −4 | −12 | −20 |

**Derived:** 40 dB over 300°, 0.1333 dB per degree, 4 dB per detent dot, and all six printed labels
land on it exactly. So `api_threshold` is a plain linear parameter in dBu with no taper, and the
five unlabelled dots are at +16, +12, +4, −8 and −16 dBu.

**VARIABLE release is a log taper, and a commercial one rather than an ideal one.**

| dot angle | −150° | −90° | −30° | 0° | +30° | +90° | +150° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| printed | **50 m** | **.15 s** | **.25 s** | (blank) | **.75 s** | **1.5 s** | **3 s** |
| ideal log law, 0.05 → 3 s over 300° | 0.050 | 0.113 | 0.257 | 0.387 | 0.583 | 1.32 | 3.00 |

**Derived:** the endpoints and the −30° mark sit on an exact logarithmic law; the other three are up
to 30 % above it, always in the same direction. That pattern — exact at the ends, exact at one
intermediate point, high in between — is what a two-segment commercial audio-taper potentiometer
gives, and it is not what a mislabelled panel gives. So model the VARIABLE pot as a log taper from
0.05 to 3 s and expect the printed intermediate marks to sit slightly above the curve.

**GAIN is a two-segment taper, and the segments are visible in the printing.**

| dot angle | −150° | −90° | −30° | 0° | +30° | +90° | +150° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| printed (dB) | **0** | **3** | **6** | **9** | **12** | **18** | **+24** |
| dB per 30° dot, interval by interval | — | 1.5 | 1.5 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |

**Derived:** 1.5 dB per dot over the first four dots and 3 dB per dot over the last six, 0 to 24 dB
in total. More resolution at low make-up, which is where you want it.

**MIX is linear**, 10 % per dot: 0 at −150°, 20 at −90°, 40 at −30°, 60 at +30°, 80 at +90°, 100 at
+150°, and the unlabelled dot at 0° is the **50 % detent** API describe [3].

**The four seven-position switches**, position by position, at −90° through +90° in 30° steps:

| position | −90° | −60° | −30° | 0° | +30° | +60° | +90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ATTACK (ms) | .03 | .1 | .3 | **1** | 3 | 10 | 30 |
| RATIO (:1) | 1.5 | 2 | 3 | **4** | 6 | 10 | ∞ |
| RELEASE (s) | .05 | .1 | .2 | **.5** | 1 | 2 | VARIABLE |
| L/R LINK (%) | IND | 50 | 60 | **70** | 80 | 90 | 100 |

**The original 2500's threshold dial is a different law, and I could not resolve it.** Its recall
chart [9] is a rotated, text-bearing PDF rather than an outlined one, so I have its legends as text
objects: the THRESH scale prints **eight** values, `+10 +8 +4 0 -4 -8 -12 -20`, over a 30 dB range,
against the 2500+'s six over 40 dB. A least-squares circle through the eight label centres fits well
(radius 24.97, centre (281.2, 69.7)) and spans **268.5°**, but the angular steps between consecutive
labels come out at 34.2° to 44.2° while the decibel steps are 2, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4 and 8. Those two
sequences do not correspond, so the dial is **not** linear in decibels, and I cannot say what it is.

The reason I can state the 2500+'s law exactly and not this one is worth naming, because it is a
method rather than an accident: on the 2500+ chart I measured the **detent dots**, which are circles
of fixed size whose centres are their positions, and on the original chart I have only **label
centres**, which shift with the width of the text. `+10` is three characters wide and `0` is one, and
that alone is enough to move a text centre by several degrees on a 25-point radius. So the original's
taper stays unresolved, and the `2500` variant should carry the printed values as labels without
claiming a law behind them.

**And this gives the plug-in its defaults for free.** API's own quick start says "Set all of the
knobs in the COMPRESSOR section to the 12:00 position", plus KNEE MED, THRUST NORM, TYPE NEW, L/R
LINK 50 %, SHAPE SLOW, auto make-up on and BLEND off [3]. Twelve o'clock is 0°, and the table above
turns that into ATTACK 1 ms, RATIO 4:1, RELEASE 0.5 s, THRESH 0 dBu and VARIABLE about 0.4 s.
**Those defaults are published, not invented**, which is a small thing that almost never happens.

### 2.6 The meter, and what it actually reads

Two moving-coil VU meters, cream-faced and backlit, with **two scales on one face**: a standard VU
arc from −20 to +3 and a red gain-reduction arc from 20 down to 0. The METER switch chooses which
signal drives the movement; the face carries both scales all the time.

Three published statements govern it [2] [3]:

- **"0 VU is +4dBu"**, and "this reference point can be adjusted internally to other reference
  levels".
- The gain-reduction scale has **"the 0 point being all the way to the right, allowing more
  resolution of the gain reduction scale"**, and runs 0 to 20 dB.
- The compressor itself reaches **30 dB** of reduction, so the meter pins at 20 [2].

The first and third of those are the useful ones for a model. The reversed gain-reduction scale is a
drawing problem rather than a modelling one: the pointer is the same pointer, and only the numbers
run the other way. The 20 dB ceiling against a 30 dB capability means **a faithful meter must be
able to pin**, and a model that rescales its meter to whatever gain reduction it happens to be
producing would be showing the user something the hardware never shows them.

I have found no published statement of the meter's ballistics, its drive current per decibel, or
whether the gain-reduction reading is linear in the control voltage. The SSL had a clone builder who
published "about 50 µA/dB" [SSL-Gbus 2.4]; there is no equivalent here. Section 13 refuses to test
what nobody published.

## 3. Signal path and circuit, from the documents that exist

### 3.0 What kind of document this section is built on

There is no API schematic. What API do publish is a **block diagram**, on the same sheet as the
specification, revision C dated 14 January 2022 [4], saved as
`ref/api-2500plus-specs-block-diagram-250dpi.png`. It is a real engineering block diagram rather
than marketing artwork: it distinguishes switches from potentiometers, it shows the relay bypass as
two changeover contacts, it puts the transformer where the transformer is, and — the thing that
makes it worth having — **it draws the TYPE switch's two contacts at two named nodes**, which is the
one fact this whole dossier turns on.

The original manual carries a second, cruder pair of diagrams, one for NEW and one for OLD, drawn as
two separate signal chains [2]. They agree with the 2500+ block diagram and add the "3 VCA's per
channel" annotation.

Below the block level, everything is either the patent [8], THAT's datasheets and design note [10]
[11] [12], or an inference clearly labelled as one.

### 3.1 The audio path

```
 BALANCED IN ──┬──► 2510 ──► VCA block ──► 2520 ──► output transformer ──┬─► BYPASS relay ─► BAL OUT +
               │             (uncompressed                               └─► BYPASS relay ─► BAL OUT −
               │              + gain reduction)          ▲
               ├──────────────────────────────────────► both relays' other contacts (hard bypass)
               └──────────────────────────────────────► METER switch, "IN" position
```

Read off [4]. Five things in it.

**The input amplifier is a 2510 and the output amplifier a 2520**, API's own discrete op-amp modules
[2] [3] [4], and Robjohns confirms them by inspection: "The discrete op-amp blocks are evident: 2510
modules handle the inputs and 2520s drive the outputs" [14]. The 2500+ feature list says the same
[3].

**The output is transformer coupled**, at 75 Ω, and the specification says so [1] [4]. The input is
60 kΩ balanced and is not described as transformer coupled anywhere I can find.

**The VCA is drawn as one block with two halves**, lettered `UNCOMPRESSED SIGNAL` above a dashed
line and `GAIN REDUCTION` below it, with the audio entering on the left, leaving on the right, the
control voltage entering from below and the BLEND/MIX control entering from above [4]. That is API
saying the output blend happens **inside the gain-cell block**, not in a mixer after it — which is
what you would do if you had spare Blackmer cells and wanted a click-free, level-matched crossfade,
and which is consistent with API's own claim that "each channel uses multiple VCA's to minimize
noise and distortion found in single VCA compressors" [3].

**The bypass really is a bypass.** Two relay changeovers, one per output leg, whose alternate
contacts come straight from the input terminals. Nothing in between [4]. And the relays are
fail-safe: "If the power fails to the 2500, the BYP automatically engages" [2].

**There is no filter anywhere in the audio path.** No high-pass, no de-emphasis, nothing between the
input amplifier and the output transformer except the gain cell. The specification's 20 Hz to 50 kHz
±0.5 dB [4] is consistent with that, and it is why section 11 puts no filters in the audio path
either.

### 3.2 The sidechain, block by block

```
                     ┌── NEW ◄── sidechain amp ◄─┬── split of the input
   TYPE switch ──────┤                           └── SIDE CHAIN jack (normalled to the input)
        │            └── OLD ◄─────────────────────── output of the 2520
        ▼
   THRUST filter ─► RMS DETECTOR ─► THRESHOLD, ATTACK, RELEASE, RATIO, KNEE ─► SHAPE, L/R LINK
                                                     │                                │
                                                     │ AUTO                           ▼
                                                     └──────────────► GAIN ◄── IN / UNITY GAIN switch
                                                                       │  ▲
                                                                       │  └── MANUAL pot
                                                                       ▼
                                                            VCA control port, and the METER
```

Read off [4] and cross-checked against the two topology diagrams in [2]. Six consequences, and every
one of them changes the model.

**One THRUST filter and one detector serve both TYPE positions.** The TYPE switch is upstream of
both. So the tilt filter and the RMS detector are not properties of the feedforward path; they are
properties of the sidechain, and switching to OLD does not switch them out.

**The side-chain jack is only live in NEW.** API say it in as many words: "NOTE: Only active when
TYPE is set to NEW" [3], and the block diagram agrees, because the jack feeds the sidechain
amplifier which feeds only the NEW contact. The original's rear panel legend adds that the jack
"Normals to XLR INPUT" [2], so with nothing plugged in the sidechain amplifier sees the input split.

**The OLD contact sits on the output of the 2520**, which is downstream of the gain cell *and*
downstream of every gain the gain cell is applying, make-up gain included. Section 6 is about what
that costs.

**The IN switch does not remove the gain cell; it substitutes a unity-gain control voltage.** The
block diagram draws it as a changeover between the sidechain's control voltage and a node lettered
`UNITY GAIN`, feeding the GAIN block [4]. So with IN out, the VCA is still in circuit and still
receiving whatever the GAIN block produces. API's prose agrees: it "defeats the compression action
silently, but allows the signal to pass through" [2] [3].

**Make-up gain and gain reduction arrive at the same port.** The GAIN block is between the IN switch
and the VCA, it takes MANUAL from a pot and AUTO from the threshold/ratio block, and its single
output goes to the cell's control input [4]. One control voltage carries both. That is the same
arrangement as the SSL [SSL-Gbus 3.4], and it is what makes the OLD-mode make-up interaction
possible.

**The meter is driven from the control voltage, not from a measurement of the gain.** The line
lettered `GAIN REDUCTION` runs from the GAIN block's node to the METER switch [4]. So the
gain-reduction reading is the control voltage, scaled. For a Blackmer cell that is the same thing as
decibels of gain reduction (section 4), so the naive meter and the circuit meter agree here as they
did on the SSL [SSL-Gbus 2.4] — but they agree for a reason, and the reason is worth knowing.

### 3.3 What the block diagram does not show

Everything that matters below the block level, which is most of what a model needs:

- **How THRESHOLD, RATIO and KNEE are wired.** They are inside one box on the drawing. THAT's Design
  Note 115 [12] shows how those three are built around a 2180 and a 2252 and sections 6, 7 and 11
  use it as the best available account, labelled as an account of THAT's reference design and not of
  API's product.
- **Where the attack and release time constants sit** relative to the detector's own averaging
  capacitor. Section 9 argues from the parts that there must be two time constants in series and
  says so as an inference.
- **How the L/R LINK percentage network is built**, and whether the SHAPE filters are before or
  after the percentage divider.
- **Whether the ratio network is switched by TYPE.** Section 6.4 shows it must be, or else the
  printed ratios are wrong in one of the two positions, and says which way I would model it.
- **The auto make-up circuit.** Section 3.6 recovers what it must compute, from published numbers,
  without knowing how.
- **Any component value at all.** Not one appears on any API document for this box.

### 3.4 How many gain cells, and the disagreement about it

| source | claim |
|---|---|
| API, original 2500 manual [2] | "each channel used **three** VCA's to minimize noise and distortion found in single and dual VCA compressors" |
| *Sound On Sound*, from opening the unit [14] | "there are **four** 2180 VCAs and one 2252 level detector per channel" |
| API, 50th and 2500+ manuals [3] [7] | "each channel uses **multiple** VCA's" |

I cannot resolve this and I am not going to pretend to. Robjohns had the lid off and counted chips;
API were describing what the audio path uses. Both can be true if one 2180 per channel is doing
something other than carrying audio — driving the meter, forming part of the link network, or
implementing the auto make-up — and API's own later hedge to "multiple" is consistent with the count
having changed when BLEND arrived. **What matters for the model is that it is more than one and that
they are paralleled for noise**, which does not change the transfer function, only the noise floor
and the distortion, and neither of those is published for this box.

### 3.5 The link, and why it is not power summing

API describe the link twice, in almost identical words, in both manuals:

> "When many compressors are linked to the 'STEREO LINK' mode, only one channel is controlling the
> compression. When the 2500 is linked, **the control voltages are summed together, but both
> channels are still detecting their own control voltages.** This also eliminates changing of the
> tone of the compressor when linking is used. Many popular units actually change when linked!" [2]
> [3]

And the link is a **percentage**, in seven steps from independent through 50 % to 100 % [2] [3].

That rules out the obvious implementation. THAT's own design note says how you link two of these
detectors: "To make a two-channel stereo compressor, one need only duplicate the circuitry shown and
provide a means to link the two RMS detectors **at their C_T pins**. Such coupling results in **true
power summing** and a more euphonic behavior" [12]. API do not do that. Tying the C_T pins together
makes one detector out of two, which is precisely the "only one channel is controlling the
compression" arrangement API say they avoided, and it cannot be done by percentages.

**So the API 2500's link mixes two decibel-domain control voltages in a set proportion, and is not
true RMS power summing.** That is a real difference from the dbx 160's linking [dbx-160 10.6] and it
is testable: power summing and dB averaging give different answers for any pair of unequal channel
levels, and they differ most when the levels differ most. Section 13 has the test.

**Derived, and labelled as such:** if the two channels' detector outputs are `L` and `R` in decibels
and the link fraction is `k`, the natural reading of "the control voltages are summed together" in
fixed proportions that leave unity gain at `L = R` is

```
CV_left  = (1 − k/2)·L + (k/2)·R
CV_right = (1 − k/2)·R + (k/2)·L
```

so `k = 0` is independent, `k = 1` is a full average, and each channel keeps its own detector at
every setting. That is an inference about the arithmetic, not a reading of a circuit, and section 11
carries it as such.

### 3.6 The auto make-up gain, recovered from four published numbers

API describe auto make-up three times and never give a formula:

- "The 'auto' make-up GAIN function applies make-up gain **based upon the settings of the RATIO and
  THRESHOLD controls**" [3].
- "If the input signal is below +4 dBu, rotating the THRESH control will increase the output level
  until the output level is around **+4 dBu**, and then as the signal gets compressed, it will
  maintain an even output level, allowing THRESH and RATIO adjustments without having to re-adjust
  the make-up gain" [2].
- "This function is similar to the API **525 ceiling control**" [2] — and API describe that control
  as one where "increasing works the same as turning the threshold down and make-up gain up at the
  same time" [13].

The standard law that does what those three sentences describe is

```
M = (1 − 1/R) · (L_ref − T),   clamped at M ≥ 0
```

with `L_ref` the level the box holds constant and `T` the threshold, both in dBu. It is the amount
of gain the compressor takes away from a signal sitting at `L_ref`, added back. API's second
sentence gives `L_ref = +4 dBu`. The threshold bottoms at **−20 dBu** [2] [3]. The ratio tops out at
**∞:1**, so `(1 − 1/R)` tops out at 1. Therefore the largest make-up the auto circuit can ever
produce is

```
M_max = 1 × (4 − (−20)) = 24 dB
```

**and the manual GAIN pot's published range is 0 to +24 dB** [3] [14].

I want to be exact about what that is and is not. It is **not** proof that API implement this
formula. It is four published numbers of which three predict the fourth to the decibel, in a design
where the manual and automatic paths feed the same node and would sensibly be given the same range.
I record it as **derived**, I use it in section 11, and section 13 turns it into a test that asserts
API's published +4 dBu reference rather than the model's own output.

**One consequence, in NEW mode.** With this law, the output level for an input at exactly the
reference is independent of both threshold and ratio, which is the whole point of the control. In
OLD mode it is not, because the make-up gain is inside the loop (section 6.3), and that is where
Robjohns' warning comes from.

## 4. The gain element: the THAT 2180

### 4.1 Which part, and how firmly

*Sound On Sound*, having opened the unit, reports "four **2180** VCAs and one 2252 level detector per
channel" [14]. API never name either part in any document I have.

So the part identification here is **one journalist's inspection of one review unit in 2001**, and
it is weaker than the SSL's, where the part number is lettered on SSL's own card drawing [SSL-Gbus
4.1], and weaker than the dbx 160's, where the module is named in the schematic's own legend
[dbx-160 5.1]. It is stronger than the Distressor's, which is an inference from behaviour alone
[Distressor 3.2]. I take it as good, I say whose it is, and section 12 weighs it at that value and
not higher.

**What it is.** The THAT 2180 series are "Blackmer pre-trimmed IC VCAs": current-in, current-out,
log-antilog gain cells with two opposing-polarity control ports, trimmed at the wafer for distortion
and control-voltage feedthrough [10]. They are the direct descendants of the dbx 202 that SSL used
[SSL-Gbus 4.1] and of the dbx 200 module in the dbx 160 [dbx-160 5.1]. Three grades, A, B and C,
differing only in trimmed distortion.

### 4.2 The gain law, as a specification rather than a guess

THAT print it as an equation [10]:

```
Gain(dB) = (E_C+ − E_C−) / (0.0061 · (1 + 0.0033·T))
```

where `T` is the difference between 25 °C and the actual temperature. At room temperature that
reduces to `Gain(dB) = (E_C+ − E_C−) / 0.0061`, and with one port grounded to
`Gain(dB) = E_C / 0.0061`.

The published tolerances on that law:

| parameter | condition | 2180A | 2180B | 2180C | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gain-control constant, E_C+ | 25 °C | 6.0 / **6.1** / 6.2 mV/dB | same | same | [10] |
| gain-control constant, E_C− | 25 °C | −6.2 / **−6.1** / −6.0 mV/dB | same | same | [10] |
| control tempco | ref. 27 °C | **+0.33 %/°C** | same | same | [10] |
| gain-control linearity | −60 to +40 dB | **0.5 %** typ, 2 % max | same | same | [10] |
| gain at 0 V control | E_C− = 0 mV | **0.0 dB ±0.1** | ±0.15 | ±0.2 | [10] |
| off isolation at 1 kHz | E_C+ = −360 mV, E_C− = +360 mV | **115 dB** typ, 110 min | same | same | [10] |
| output noise, 20 Hz–20 kHz, R_out = 20 k | 0 dB gain | **−98 dBV** typ | −98 | −98 | [10] |
| slew rate | R_in = R_out = 20 k | **12 V/µs** | same | same | [10] |
| gain range / dynamic range | — | >130 dB / >120 dB | same | same | [10] |
| gain-bandwidth | — | 20 MHz | same | same | [10] |

**Total harmonic distortion at 1 kHz, no external trim** [10]:

| condition | 2180A typ / max | 2180B typ / max | 2180C typ / max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 dBV in, 0 dB gain | **0.005 % / 0.010 %** | 0.010 / 0.020 | 0.030 / 0.050 |
| +10 dBV in, −15 dB gain | 0.020 / 0.030 | 0.030 / 0.040 | 0.040 / 0.070 |
| −5 dBV in, +15 dB gain | 0.020 / 0.030 | 0.030 / 0.040 | 0.040 / 0.070 |

Two things in that table are worth carrying into the model.

**Distortion rises with gain reduction, by a factor of four from 0 dB to −15 dB of gain.** That is
the opposite of the diode bridge, where "distortion falls as gain reduction rises" [components
crate, diode-bridge README], and it is a real behavioural difference between the lab's two gain
elements. It is also the same table the SSL dossier used [SSL-Gbus 4.2], which is the first sign
that the *cell* is genuinely shared even where the *machines* are not.

**Which grade API used is unknown**, and the range across grades is a factor of six at 0 dB. Any
model that asserts a distortion figure for this box is asserting a grade nobody published.

### 4.3 Why the gain law makes the meter honest

Because gain in decibels is linear in the control voltage, and the control voltage is what drives
the meter (3.2), the gain-reduction meter reads decibels of gain reduction directly, with no
conversion anywhere. A model that computes `20·log10` of its own gain and paints that on the meter
gets the same answer. That is a small result but it saves building machinery, and it is only true
because the cell is exponential.

### 4.4 What the datasheet says that the model should not use

- **The symmetry pin.** Pin 4 nulls even-order distortion, and THAT's advice for the positive
  control port is "In order to maintain the wafer level adjustment which minimizes THD, **leave pin
  4 open**" [10]. Whether API trim it is unknown. Modelling a trim whose setting is unknown is
  modelling a free parameter.
- **The input-resistor trade.** "For every dB these resistor values are decreased, the voltage noise
  at the output of the OP275 is reduced by one dB", and larger resistors reduce distortion at high
  levels [10]. API's values are unknown, so this is a design axis I cannot place the 2500 on.
- **DC-coupling thumps.** "Any dc input currents will cause dc in the output which will be modulated
  by gain; this may cause audible thumps" [10]. A real behaviour, unmeasured in this box, and a
  model of it would be a model of API's coupling capacitors, which nobody has published.

## 5. The detector: the THAT 2252

### 5.1 What it is, and why this is the lab's first one of these

The THAT 2252 is an IC RMS-level detector: current in, **a voltage proportional to the logarithm of
the true RMS value** out. THAT's own description says it is "Based on dbx technology", that it
"Matches 2180 and 2181 Series VCAs", and its headline figures are >80 dB dynamic range, bandwidth to
beyond 20 kHz, and a crest factor of 8 for 1 dB of error [11].

**The lab already has a true-RMS detector dossier**, the dbx 160 [dbx-160 5], and that unit gets
there first: it is a potted **dbx model 208** module, and that dossier uses the THAT 2252 datasheet
as the best available proxy for it because the 208 has no published datasheet [dbx-160 5.1]. The
distinction worth keeping is small but real: **the dbx 160 is documented to contain the ancestor and
the API 2500 is reported to contain the part itself.** So if the dbx lands first, this is the lab's
second true-RMS unit and its first literal 2252; if it lands second, it is the first of both. Either
way section 12 does not propose to share the detector, and section 12.3 says why in the same terms
the dbx dossier used [dbx-160 11.4].

### 5.2 How it computes an RMS, and why that matters here

The 2252 rectifies the input current, takes the log of the square, and applies a **log-domain
filter** [11]. THAT's own account:

> "Another way of looking at this situation is to consider the action of Q6 and C_T as a first-order
> filter in the log domain... The result is that the voltage at pin 6 represents the average (or
> mean) of the square of the input signal, averaged over the time constant τ." [11]

with the time constant

```
τ = C_T · V_T / I_T = 0.0259 · C_T / I_T   seconds, at 300 K
```

and the square root taken for free, because "in the log representation, taking the square root is
equivalent to division by two": pin 6 sits at about 3 mV/dB of the mean square, which is 6 mV/dB of
its root [11].

Two consequences that a linear envelope follower does not have, both stated by THAT:

- **Falling signals decay linearly in decibels**, at a constant dB/s, because "the natural release
  of the exponential time weighting is a decaying exponential in the linear world. This maps to a
  **straight line** in the log representation" [11].
- **Attack and release cannot be separated.** "How fast the 2252 acquires a signal (the 'attack'),
  and how fast it returns to rest following a signal (the 'release'), are **locked in relationship to
  each other**... **Separate attack and release adjustments are not possible within the constraint of
  rms response**" [11].

**And that second sentence is why the API 2500 must have two time constants in series, not one.**
The dbx 160 has no attack or release control at all, and the dbx dossier shows that this is not a
simplification but a property of RMS detection [dbx-160 5.5]. The API 2500 has seven attack times
and seven release positions. It cannot get them from the detector. So the panel's ballistics must
live in a **stage after the detector**, operating on the detector's decibel-domain output, and the
detector's own averaging time constant is a fixed, unpublished, additional smoothing in front of
them.

That is an inference, and I label it one, but it is forced: the parts and the panel cannot both be
true otherwise. Section 9 works out what follows.

### 5.3 The published characteristics

| parameter | condition | min | typ | max | units | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| output scale factor | 31.6 nA < I_in < 1 mA, 25 °C | 6.0 | **6.1** | 6.2 | mV/dB | [11] |
| output tempco | ref. 27 °C | — | **0.33** | — | %/°C | [11] |
| symmetry voltage | no signal | −2 | — | +18 | mV | [11] |
| output linearity | 1 kHz, 1 mA > I_in > 100 nA | — | 0.5 | — | dB | [11] |
| output linearity | 1 kHz, 31.6 nA < I_in < 1 mA | — | 1.0 | — | dB | [11] |
| crest factor, 1 ms pulse repetition | 0.2 dB error | — | **3.5** | — | — | [11] |
| crest factor | 0.5 dB error | — | **5** | — | — | [11] |
| crest factor | 1.0 dB error | — | **8** | — | — | [11] |
| max frequency for 1 dB extra error | I_in ≈ 100 µA | — | 80 | — | kHz | [11] |
| max frequency for 1 dB extra error | I_in ≈ 1 µA | — | 30 | — | kHz | [11] |
| filtering time constant | — | — | 0.026·C_T / I_T | — | s | [11] |
| dynamic range | — | — | >80 | — | dB | [11] |

**The 6.1 mV/dB is the number to notice.** The detector's output scale factor and the 2180's
gain-control constant are **the same 6.1 mV/dB**, to the same ±0.1 mV/dB tolerance, from the same
manufacturer, and THAT advertise the match on the datasheet's front page: "Matches 2180 and 2181
Series VCAs" [11]. So a wire from the detector's output to the VCA's control port gives exactly
1 dB of gain reduction per decibel above the reference: an **infinite** ratio, with no scaling
anywhere. Every finite ratio is then an attenuator between them, and section 6 uses that.

**And the tempcos match too**: +0.33 %/°C on the VCA's control constant [10] and 0.33 %/°C on the
detector's output [11]. Two parts on the same board at the same temperature drift the same way and
the drift cancels in the ratio. That is not an accident; it is why these two parts are sold as a
pair.

### 5.4 Crest factor, and what the detector does not hear

The crest-factor table above is the most useful thing in the datasheet for a model of this box,
because it says quantitatively how much a transient is *not* seen. A signal with a crest factor of 8
— 18 dB of peak above RMS — reads 1 dB low. At a crest factor of 3.5, about 11 dB, the error is
0.2 dB [11]. That is the RMS detector's characteristic behaviour: it under-reads peaky material and
it does so predictably.

It also explains something about the panel. Robjohns' one design criticism was the lack of a
programme-dependent release [14], but an RMS detector *is* programme dependent, in a way a peak
detector is not: the same knob setting gives different effective ballistics on different crest
factors. That is not a substitute for a two-stage release network and I am not claiming it is, but
it is why the box does not feel as static as its seven fixed release times suggest.

### 5.5 The bandwidth limit, which is real and is not modelled by an ideal RMS

The 2252's error grows with frequency and the growth depends on level: 1 dB of extra error at 80 kHz
for a 100 µA input, but at only 30 kHz for a 1 µA input [11]. So the detector's high-frequency
accuracy falls as the signal gets quieter. Within the audio band and at normal operating levels this
is small. At low levels near the threshold it is not negligible, and it is one of several reasons
the transition into compression is softer on real hardware than on an ideal model.

I have no measurement of the 2500 that isolates this, so section 11 offers it as an optional,
default-off refinement rather than as part of the model.

### 5.6 What the TILT trim is for, and why it voids the calibration

The original 2500 has a screwdriver trim right of the meters that "adjusts about 2 dB in either
direction", "trims the compression control voltage", "**DOES NOT affect the uncompressed signal**",
and carries a warning that adjusting it will likely require a factory recalibration [2]. Robjohns
describes it as "a screwdriver trimmer to optimise the level balance (±2dB) between channels during
compression" [14].

THAT's design note says why a box like this needs one. Having recommended linking two detectors at
their C_T pins, it adds: "**Note that, when linking detectors in this manner, means to adjust for the
+/−3 dB tolerance on each detectors' 0 dB reference level must be provided**" [12].

API do not link at the C_T pins (3.5), but the underlying problem is the same and is worse, not
better, for two independent detectors: each 2252's zero-output reference current depends on its bias
and timing currents through `I_in0 = I_BIAS·I_T / 2.9` [11], so two channels built from two chips
will not agree about where 0 dB is. **The TILT trim is the adjustment THAT say you have to provide,
and the factory-calibration warning is what you would expect if turning it walks the unit off a
bench-set match.** That is an inference and I mark it one, but it explains a control that is
otherwise inexplicable, and it explains why it disappeared from the 2500+ manual: once you are
matching parts at the factory rather than trimming in the field, you take the screwdriver hole off
the panel.

For the model, the consequence is small and worth stating anyway: **the two channels of a real 2500
do not have identical thresholds**, by up to a few decibels before trimming, and a model with two
bit-identical channels is idealising something the hardware put a warning label on.

## 6. The TYPE switch: what actually changes, and where the tap moves

### 6.1 What the switch physically does

The TYPE switch is a single changeover at the **input** of the sidechain. Its two contacts sit at
two nodes [4]:

- **NEW**, lettered `F FWD` on the panel: the output of the sidechain amplifier, which is fed from a
  split of the unit's input and from the SIDE CHAIN jack.
- **OLD**, lettered `F BACK`: **the output of the 2520**, which is the audio path's output amplifier,
  downstream of the gain cell.

Its common contact feeds the THRUST filter, and from there the RMS detector and everything after it.
Nothing else in the sidechain moves. The detector is the same detector, the tilt filter is the same
tilt filter, the timing network is the same timing network.

API describe both in the same words in both manuals:

> "**OLD**: Feed-Back topology: The RMS detector receives the signal from **after the VCA**"
> "**NEW**: Feed-Forward topology: The RMS detector receives the signal from **before the VCA**" [3]

and, in the original,

> "When the OLD position is selected, the RMS detector gets the signal from the output of the VCA,
> and then feeds the VCA a signal based on a set ratio of that signal. This type of compression is
> called FEEDBACK compression and is how the older API 525, 1176 type and 660 type compressors
> worked. **The NEW mode is much harder and the OLD mode is very smooth.**" [2]

That last clause is API's own claim about the audible result, and the rest of this section is about
why it is true rather than whether it is.

**But the tap is not "after the VCA". It is after the output amplifier.** The block diagram puts the
OLD contact on the junction downstream of the 2520 [4], and Robjohns confirms it behaviourally:

> "one trap to be aware of when using manual make-up gain is that, **in the feed-backwards mode, the
> level sensing is derived from after the make-up gain control.** With high ratio and threshold
> settings the output level, naturally, is reduced, but **increasing the make-up gain results in even
> further compression.**" [14]

Since make-up gain reaches the audio through the gain cell's own control port (3.2), "after the VCA"
and "after the make-up gain control" are the same node, and both descriptions are right. The
distinction matters because it decides whether make-up gain is inside the loop, and it is.

### 6.2 The two static laws, derived

Write everything in decibels. `x` is the input level, `y` the output level, `T` the threshold, `M`
the make-up gain, `G` the gain reduction, and **`α` the fraction of the detector's decibel output
that reaches the control port** — the sidechain divider that the RATIO switch sets (5.3 shows why a
straight wire is α = 1). The gain cell contributes `M − G` decibels.

**NEW.** The detector reads `x`, so above threshold `G = α(x − T)` and

```
y = x + M − α(x − T) = (1 − α)·x + M + αT
```

Slope `1 − α`, so the ratio is

```
R_new = 1 / (1 − α)          and      α = 1 − 1/R_new
```

which is the standard Blackmer-topology ratio law, the same one the dbx 160 dossier derives from its
own circuit [dbx-160 6.1].

**OLD.** The detector reads `y`, so above threshold `G = α(y − T)` and

```
y = x + M − α(y − T)   ⟹   y = (x + M + αT) / (1 + α)
```

Slope `1/(1 + α)`, so the ratio is

```
R_old = 1 + α
```

**Both are exact for an ideal divider, and they are completely different functions of the same α:**

| α | 0 | 1/3 | 1/2 | 2/3 | 3/4 | 5/6 | 9/10 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ratio in NEW | 1 | **1.5** | **2** | **3** | **4** | **6** | **10** | **∞** |
| ratio in OLD | 1 | 1.33 | 1.5 | 1.67 | 1.75 | 1.83 | 1.9 | **2** |

The seven α values that give the seven printed ratios in NEW give a ratio range of 1.33:1 to 2:1 in
OLD. **A feedback loop with unity sidechain gain is a 2:1 compressor and cannot be anything else.**

### 6.3 Make-up gain is inside the loop, and that moves the threshold

Below threshold the box is not compressing, so `y = x + M`. Compression therefore begins when
`x + M > T`, that is at an **input-referred threshold of `T − M`**. Every decibel of make-up gain
lowers the threshold by a decibel.

In NEW the detector reads `x`, so the threshold is `T` whatever the make-up is.

That is Robjohns' trap, derived: turn up the manual GAIN in OLD and the box compresses more, because
you have moved the threshold down under it. It is not a defect and it is not a subtlety of the
circuit; it is what "the detector is after the make-up gain" means.

**The auto make-up survives this, and that is worth showing.** Take the law recovered in 3.6,
`M = α(L_ref − T)`, and substitute it into the OLD result at `x = L_ref`:

```
y = (L_ref + α(L_ref − T) + αT) / (1 + α) = L_ref·(1 + α)/(1 + α) = L_ref
```

**Derived:** the same auto make-up formula holds the output at the reference level in *both*
topologies, for any α and any threshold. So API can use one auto make-up circuit for both TYPE
positions and it will be right in both, while the manual pot is right in only one. That is a
satisfying result, it explains why API's own manual warns about manual make-up and says nothing
about auto make-up, and it is a good test (13.2).

### 6.4 The ratio problem, stated plainly

The panel prints **one** ratio scale and the TYPE switch has **two** positions. By 6.2, one divider
network cannot serve both. So one of these must be true:

1. **API switch the ratio network with TYPE**, so that OLD uses α = R − 1 rather than α = 1 − 1/R.
2. **API do not**, and the printed ratios are correct in NEW and describe something else in OLD.

I have no schematic and cannot settle it. What I can do is say what each costs.

**If (1), the sidechain must supply gain rather than attenuation in OLD**, up to α = 9 for the 10:1
position, and **the ∞:1 position becomes unreachable**, because `R_old = 1 + α` needs infinite loop
gain to reach infinity. It also makes the loop 1 + α = R times faster (6.5), which at the 10:1
position is a tenfold speed-up that nobody has ever remarked on.

**If (2), OLD's real ratios run from 1.33:1 to 2:1** and every description of OLD as "very smooth"
[2] and "smoother, softer, more transparent" [3] is straightforwardly explained: it *is* smoother,
because it is barely compressing by comparison.

**One printed phrase leans towards (1), and it is the only evidence either way.** API's original
manual glosses the top ratio position as "**INF:1 or above 20:1**" [2]. On a switch whose top detent
is printed `∞`, "or above 20:1" is a strange thing to write — unless the position is not actually
infinite in one of the two modes. A feedback loop reaches 20:1 at α = 19 and 40:1 at α = 39, so
"above 20:1" is exactly the sort of number a feedback design produces where a feedforward one would
produce infinity. I offer that as a reading of an odd sentence, **not** as evidence of a circuit.

**What I would model, and why.** Model `α` as the physical quantity, take the printed ratios as the
NEW-mode calibration because API say NEW sends the VCA "a signal that is an **exact ratio** of the
desired compression set by the RATIO control" [3] and say no such thing about OLD, and then apply a
**switched compensation** in OLD so that the panel keeps its promise, with the ∞ position mapping to
a large finite α. Expose the resulting curve in the plug-in's transfer-curve display so the user can
see what the switch did. That is a choice, it is the more generous of the two readings, and section
11 records it as a choice rather than as a finding.

### 6.5 The ballistics consequence, derived

Put a first-order lag of time constant τ in the sidechain, after the detector, and linearise around
an operating point. In NEW:

```
τ · dG/dt = α(x − T) − G
```

so the closed loop is just the lag: time constant **τ**. In OLD the detector reads `y = x + M − G`,
so

```
τ · dG/dt = α(x + M − G − T) − G = α(x + M − T) − (1 + α)·G
```

which is a first-order system with time constant **τ / (1 + α)**, settling to
`G = α(x + M − T)/(1 + α)`.

**Derived: in OLD the box is `1 + α` times faster than its panel says, and the factor depends on the
ratio.** At the 4:1 position with α = 3/4 that is 1.75×; if the ratio network is switched so that
α = R − 1, it is R×. The Distressor dossier records the same effect in weaker form — "feedback halves
the effective time constant versus a feed-forward reading" [Distressor 7.3] — and halving is the
α = 1 case.

This is the mechanism behind every "feedback compressors are more forgiving of a slow attack"
observation in the literature, and it is a real difference the model must produce rather than
approximate, because it is not a voicing choice: it falls out of the loop.

### 6.6 The knee consequence

Let the sidechain compute `G = f(d − T)` where `d` is whatever the detector reads and `f` is the soft
function the KNEE switch selects, with `f'` rising from 0 to α across the knee. In NEW the
input-referred knee is exactly the sidechain's knee. In OLD, differentiating `u + f(u) = x + M − T`
with `u = y − T` gives

```
dy/dx = 1 / (1 + f'(u))
```

so the same sidechain knee, measured **at the input**, is stretched by a factor between 1 and
`1 + α`.

**Derived: OLD widens the knee as well as changing the ratio.** So the "smooth" character API
attribute to OLD [2] [3] is at least three separate effects — a lower ratio or a different network, a
faster loop, and a wider knee — and a model that reproduces the topology gets all three free, while
a model that reproduces the *sound* by tuning constants gets none of them right at more than one
setting.

### 6.7 What Universal Audio say about OLD, and why it is not a target

Universal Audio's product page for their API 2500 model says: "With **exclusive access to API's
schematics**, UA analyzed two classic 'golden unit' API 2500s", and describes the switch as "'Old'
gives you characterful **peak-detection** harkening back to API's highly-coveted 525, or 'New' gives
you a modern feed-forward sound" [15].

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions.

**The schematics exist and someone has seen them.** That is genuinely useful context: it means the
document I could not reach is not imaginary, and it puts an upper bound on what is knowable about
this box from outside API.

**But "peak-detection" contradicts API.** API's block diagram routes both TYPE positions through the
same RMS detector [4], and both manuals describe the RMS detector as serving both [2] [3]. Either
UA's copy is loose, or the OLD path really does have a different detector and API's own diagram
hides it. I cannot tell, I am not going to guess, and **UA's description is not evidence about the
hardware** — it is a description of UA's product. Nothing in this file treats another vendor's
emulation as a target, a specification or a source, and section 13 proposes no test against one.

## 7. The knee: what HARD, MED and SOFT actually do

### 7.1 What API say

Three descriptions, consistent across every manual [2] [3]:

| setting | LED | API's words |
|---|---|---|
| **HARD** | red | "Sharp response curve"; "Immediate onset of compression (sudden transition to set ratio)"; "More aggressive and noticeable" |
| **MED** | yellow | "Slightly rounded response curve"; "Moderate onset of compression (firm, but less sudden transition to set ratio)" |
| **SOFT** | green | "Rounded response curve"; "Gradual onset of compression (fade-in up to the set ratio)"; "Similar to an **'over-easy'** type knee"; "More transparent" |

"Over-easy" is dbx's trademark and API use it as a common noun, which tells you what family of curve
they think they are drawing [2] [3].

**No manual gives a knee width, in decibels or in anything else.**

### 7.2 What API's own plots show, and what they refuse to show

The original manual devotes a full page to three plotted transfer curves headed "Compression KNEE at
the Threshold Point" [2], saved as `ref/api-2500-knee-curves-300dpi.png`. Like the THRUST page it is
vector art, so I read the curve coordinates out of the PDF rather than off a picture.

**What the geometry says.** Each plot draws a 45° input line rising to a threshold point and then a
fan of output lines. The three differ only in what happens at the corner:

| plot | corner behaviour, from the path coordinates |
|---|---|
| HARD | a **single point**. Two straight segments meeting at x = 235.35 pt. Knee width zero. |
| MED | rounding from x = 223.9 to x = 246.7 pt — a span of **22.8 pt**, centred on 235.3, which is the HARD corner to within 0.05 pt |
| SOFT | rounding from x = 205.4 to x = 246.7 pt — a span of **41.3 pt**, centred on 226.1, which is **below** the HARD corner |

Three findings, all from API's own drawing:

- **SOFT's knee is 1.81 times as wide as MED's.**
- **MED's knee is symmetric about the hard corner**; SOFT's is not. SOFT begins bending earlier and
  finishes at the same place, so it is biased below the threshold. Both end at exactly the same
  x-coordinate, 246.7 pt, which is not a coincidence in a drawing where nothing else lines up.
- **HARD really is a corner**, not a very narrow knee.

**And what the plot will not give me: a width in decibels.** The plot's axes carry no numbers. I
tried to calibrate them from the fan of ratio lines, and that is where it fails: the drawn output
lines have slopes of 0.274, 0.198, 0.129 and 0.064 relative to the 45° input line, which correspond
to ratios of about 3.6, 4.9, 7.6 and 15.3 : 1. **None of those is one of the unit's seven ratios.**
The plot is illustrative, its ratio lines are decorative, and reading a decibel width off it would
be inventing a number and dressing it in a manufacturer's citation. So I do not.

What I take from it is a **shape and an ordering**, which is exactly what the components crate's
standard says to assert where no magnitude is published [16].

### 7.3 What the knee probably is, from the chip maker rather than from API

THAT's Design Note 115 builds a compressor from these two parts and implements the knee like this:

> "**D5**, when placed inside OA1's feedback loop by SW2A, creates a conventional **hard-knee**
> threshold response. When SW2A is in its other position, D5 is **outside** the op amp feedback loop,
> and an offset generated by D1 is injected into the summing node of OA1 that compensates for the
> D5's forward drop. **The resulting 'soft knee' is a consequence of the turn-on characteristic of
> the diode.**" [12]

So in THAT's own reference design the knee is not a width parameter and not a polynomial. It is a
diode, and the switch decides whether the op amp's loop gain linearises it or not. Inside the loop
the diode's turn-on is divided by the loop gain and becomes a corner; outside it, the diode's own
exponential `i = I_s(e^(v/nV_T) − 1)` is the knee, and the knee width is set by `nV_T` and by the
sidechain's volts-per-decibel.

This is the third time the lab has met this. The SSL's threshold "is a diode, not a comparator"
[SSL-Gbus 5.1]. The dbx 160's knee "is a diode, and dbx say so in a patent" [dbx-160 6.2]. Now
THAT's own application engineers say the same thing about the same pair of chips. **I therefore model
the API's knee as a diode turn-on rather than as a knee-width parameter**, and I record that as an
inference from the chip maker's reference design, not as a reading of API's circuit.

**Three positions from a two-position mechanism.** THAT's switch has two settings and API's has
three. The obvious extension — and it is an inference, labelled as one — is a partial compensation:
inject some fraction of the diode's forward drop rather than all or none of it, giving a family of
curves between the two extremes. That produces exactly what API's plot shows: a corner, a small
symmetric rounding, and a larger rounding that starts earlier. A second diode with a different
ideality would do it too. I cannot choose between them and neither choice changes the model, because
both produce the same one-parameter family.

### 7.4 What that means for the parameter

**There is no `knee width in dB` on this box.** There are three switch positions, and the underlying
quantity is how much of a junction's turn-off is compensated. Section 11 gives the knee as an enum
of three, backed by a soft-knee function whose one shape constant is set per position, with the
constants tuned so that the **ratio** of SOFT's width to MED's is 1.81, which is the only number
API's own drawing actually supplies. The absolute widths are **estimates** and are labelled as such
in the parameter table, at the point of use, and in the test plan, which refuses to test them.

## 8. THRUST: deriving the slope, and correcting the record

### 8.1 What the survey said, where it came from, and why it is wrong

The survey recorded "the Thrust sidechain filter with a **2 dB/octave** slope on Med and **4 dB/oct**
on Loud" [17]. That is a faithful summary of its source, *Sound On Sound*, which writes:

> "The three options are for no filtering at all (Norm), a **2dB/octave high-pass filter (Med)**, or
> the full **4dB/oct slope (Loud)**." [14]

Four independent statements contradict it, three of them API's or ATI's own.

1. **API's 2500+ and 50th manuals**: "This circuit places a filter in front of the RMS detector with
   a slope of **10dB per decade (-3dB/8va)**" [3] [7]. API give both units, and they are consistent
   with each other: 10 × log₁₀2 = 3.01.
2. **API's original manual**, describing LOUD: "there is a gradual, linear filter, **down 15 dB at
   20 Hz and up 15dB at 20K Hz**" [2]. Thirty decibels across three decades is 10 dB/decade.
3. **The patent's abstract and its single claim**: "Passive and active, half-order, five-stage
   high-pass filters are described that provide the desired **+10 db/decade** amplitude versus
   frequency slope over the audio frequency range", and the claim recites "a half-order high-pass
   filter... characterized by a slope of **approximately +10 decibels per decade**" [8].
4. **The patent's own component values, evaluated** (8.3): **9.77 dB/decade**.

**So THRUST LOUD is 10 dB per decade, which is 3.01 dB per octave.** *Sound On Sound* is 33 % high
on the LOUD slope and, more importantly, describes MED as a shallower version of the same filter,
which it is not (8.4). I have no idea where 2 and 4 came from; they appear in no API document I can
find and they are not in the patent.

None of this is a criticism of a twenty-five-year-old magazine review that got everything else about
this box right, including two facts I could not have obtained anywhere else. It is a demonstration of
the reason this repository's research standard exists: a figure that appears in exactly one secondary
source, and that a primary source contradicts, is not a figure.

### 8.2 What THRUST is for, in API's own words

Both manuals give the same explanation, and it is a good one [2] [3]:

> "This circuit places a filter in front of the RMS detector, with a slope of 10dB per decade, which
> is **the inverse of the pink noise energy curve**... there is still a substantial amount of low
> frequency information compared to high frequency information in the audio signal path. When that
> signal is fed into an RMS detector, the detector will process the signal into a DC control voltage
> based on those louder low frequencies, resulting in a control voltage that favors the low
> frequencies of the signal, **causing pumping and a loss of punch**... evening out the energy... so
> **each octave has the same energy instead of each octave having half the energy as the one lower**."

The patent puts it as an objective: to provide "a filter having a predefined, positive, amplitude
versus frequency slope characteristic over the audio frequency range" whose slope "is approximately
equal in magnitude to that of a characteristic audio input signal and of opposite sign", so that the
detector performs "**energy** level detection" rather than "**amplitude** level detection", and "the
tonal balance and timbre of the audio input signal are preserved" [8].

**The last clause is the part worth holding onto.** THRUST is not a sidechain high-pass in the sense
the lab already has one. A sidechain high-pass stops the compressor reacting to bass. THRUST makes
the compressor react to every octave equally. Those are different aims and they produce different
curves: a high-pass leaves the top flat and removes the bottom, and a tilt lifts the top as much as
it drops the bottom. API's own manual calls it a "hi-pass filter" once, in the original [2], and
every plot and every number in every document says it is a tilt. I follow the numbers.

### 8.3 The circuit, from the patent, with the arithmetic

**US 5,170,437**, figures 3 and 4 [8], saved as
`ref/us5170437-thrust-patent-drawings-300dpi.png`. The filter is a parallel bank of five branches
between the input and a common node:

| branch | R | C | τ = RC | corner 1/(2πRC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **100 kΩ** | — (resistor only) | — | — (DC path) |
| 2 | **32 kΩ** | **0.16 µF** | 5.120 ms | **31.08 Hz** |
| 3 | **10 kΩ** | **0.05 µF** | 500 µs | **318.31 Hz** |
| 4 | **3.2 kΩ** | **0.016 µF** | 51.2 µs | **3108.5 Hz** |
| 5 | **1 kΩ** | **0.005 µF** | 5.0 µs | **31 831 Hz** |

In the **passive** version (figure 3) the common node drives a **100 Ω** shunt to ground and the
output is taken across it. In the **active** version (figure 4) the same bank is the input impedance
of an inverting amplifier with a **10 kΩ** feedback resistor [8].

**Look at the ladder.** The resistors step 32 k, 10 k, 3.2 k, 1 k — each 3.2 times the next. The
capacitors step 0.16, 0.05, 0.016, 0.005 µF — each 3.2 times the next. So each corner is 3.2 × 3.2 =
**10.24 times** the one below it: the corners are spaced **one decade apart**, at 31 Hz, 318 Hz,
3.1 kHz and 31.8 kHz, straddling the audio band at both ends. The patent calls the result "a stepped
linear approximation of the desired +10 db/decade slope over the audio frequency range from
approximately 20-Hz to approximately 20,000-Hz" [8].

**Why a decade of stagger gives half of 20 dB/decade.** A first-order high-pass rises at 20 dB per
decade between its zero and its pole. Interleaving zeros and poles a decade apart makes the response
rise for half of each decade and flatten for the other half, so the average is 10 dB/decade — a
**half-order** filter, `s^½`, which is what the patent's claim calls it. That is the whole idea, and
it is a genuinely elegant one.

**The arithmetic, done.** For the active version, `H(s) = −R_f · Y(s)` where `Y` is the bank's
admittance. Evaluating with the patent's own values:

| f | 20 Hz | 100 Hz | 632.5 Hz | 2 kHz | 20 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gain, dB | −10.38 | −3.14 | +5.18 | +10.15 | +18.93 |

```
rise from 20 Hz to 20 kHz = 29.31 dB over 3 decades = 9.77 dB/decade = 2.94 dB/octave
```

**Derived, and it validates itself.** The patent claims "approximately +10 decibels per decade" and
its own component values give 9.77. That is a 2.3 % agreement between a claim and the parts that
implement it, from a document that had no reason to be consistent and is.

The local slope is not constant — that is what "stepped linear approximation" means — and wanders
between **7.3 and 12.9 dB/decade** across the band, with the ripple period set by the decade
stagger. A model that implements a perfect 3 dB/octave tilt is smoother than the circuit.

**And it reproduces API's published plot.** API's original manual plots the LOUD response on
labelled axes [2]; the curve is vector art, so I read its endpoints out of the PDF: **−14.19 dB at
20 Hz and +14.54 dB at 20 kHz**, a span of 28.73 dB, crossing 0 dB at **625 Hz**. The geometric
centre of 20 Hz to 20 kHz is 632.5 Hz. Normalise the patent's network to 0 dB at 632.5 Hz and it
gives −15.55 dB and +13.75 dB, a span of 29.31 dB.

| | 20 Hz | 20 kHz | span | 0 dB crossing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| API's plotted LOUD curve [2] | −14.19 dB | +14.54 dB | 28.73 dB | 625 Hz |
| API's prose [2] | −15 dB | +15 dB | 30 dB | — |
| patent network, normalised at 632.5 Hz [8] | −15.55 dB | +13.75 dB | 29.31 dB | 632.5 Hz |

**Three sources agreeing to within 1.4 dB at the band edges and 8 Hz on the pivot.** That is as close
to a confirmed circuit as this dossier gets, and it is the basis for section 11's THRUST filter.

### 8.4 MED is not a shallower LOUD, and I could not find its circuit

API describe MED consistently and it is not a slope at all:

> "**MED (Medium)**: A slight attenuation of the low frequencies and a slight boost in the high
> frequencies, **with a flat mid-range** are applied to the signal feeding the RMS detector." [3]
> [2]

and their plot shows exactly that. Read off the vector coordinates of the MED curve in [2]:

| segment | from | to | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| low ramp | −14.76 dB at 20 Hz | 0 dB at ≈167 Hz | **16.0 dB/decade** |
| mid plateau | ≈167 Hz | ≈2.34 kHz | **0 dB, flat** |
| high ramp | 0 dB at ≈2.34 kHz | +14.95 dB at 20 kHz | **16.0 dB/decade** |

The two ramps are the same slope to two decimal places and the two shelf depths match to 0.2 dB.
That symmetry is real, not an artefact: the drawing was constructed, not sketched.

Note the corner frequencies. The plot's own gridlines are at 200 Hz and 2 kHz, and the drawn corners
sit at 167 Hz and 2.34 kHz — 12 pt left of one gridline and 12 pt right of the other, in a 469 pt
plot. I report what I measured and flag that the intended values are probably the round ones.

**So MED is a shelf pair with a flat middle, and LOUD is a continuous tilt. They differ in kind, not
in degree.** And both reach ±15 dB at the band edges, so MED is not "less" than LOUD in amplitude —
it is less in *where*. Because most programme energy lives between 200 Hz and 2 kHz, where MED is
flat and LOUD is not, LOUD does far more to the control voltage in practice, and that is consistent
with API calling MED "slight" and with Robjohns finding LOUD the striking setting [14].

**I tested one hypothesis about MED's circuit and it failed.** The obvious guess is that MED is the
patent's network with the two middle branches removed, leaving the 31 Hz and 31.8 kHz corners and a
flat span between them. I computed it: normalised at 632.5 Hz it gives −5.71 dB at 20 Hz and
+21.49 dB at 20 kHz, badly lopsided and nothing like a symmetric shelf pair. **That hypothesis is
ruled out.** I have no other, no API document names one, and I would rather record a dead end than
present a guess.

So section 11 specifies MED **from API's published curve** rather than from a circuit, and says so.

### 8.5 What THRUST is not

- **Not a sidechain high-pass.** The lab's shared `sc_hpf` parameter is a different object doing a
  different job (11.2), and the API 2500 does not have one.
- **Not in the audio path.** Every document is explicit that the filter is "in front of the RMS
  detector" [2] [3] [8]. Nothing about it reaches the output except through the control voltage.
- **Not switched by TYPE.** It sits after the TYPE switch (3.2), so it filters whichever signal the
  detector is reading, input or output.
- **Not the same thing as a loudness contour**, though API reach for that analogy [2] [3]. A
  loudness contour boosts the extremes of the *audio*; THRUST tilts the *detector's* view so that the
  audio comes out untilted. The patent's whole argument is that preserving tonal balance requires
  correcting the detector, not the signal [8].

## 9. Timing: the attack and release steps, and what is behind them

### 9.1 The published ladders

| control | positions | values | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ATTACK | 7 | **0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30 ms** | [2] [3] [14] |
| RELEASE | 6 + 1 | **0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 s**, then VARIABLE | [2] [3] [14] |
| VARIABLE | continuous | **0.05 to 3 s**, log taper (2.3) | [2] [3] [14] |

API spell the attack ladder out in words in the original manual — "30 microseconds, 100 microseconds,
300 microseconds, 1 m/sec, 3 m/sec, 10 m/sec and 30 m/sec" [2] — which removes any doubt about
whether `.03` means 30 µs or 30 ms.

The attack ladder is a 1–3–10 sequence, half a decade per step, spanning exactly three decades. The
release ladder is roughly binary, 1–2–5 with one gap, spanning 1.6 decades. Both are the kind of
ladder you get from a switched resistor bank with E12 values, and neither tells you anything else.

### 9.2 What API do not publish, and what that costs

**No tolerance on any timing figure. No measurement procedure. No statement of what the attack time
is the attack *to*.** The Neve handbook gives all three — a per-ratio transfer table with
tolerances, attack times with tolerances *and* a stated measurement procedure, and six recovery
times [Neve-33609 8] — and that is why the Neve dossier's ballistics tests have real bounds. API give
a panel legend.

This matters more than it sounds, because "attack time" is ambiguous by about a factor of three.
A time constant, a 10-to-90 % rise, a time to reach 63 % of the final gain reduction, and a time to
reach within 1 dB of it are four different numbers for the same circuit. Without API's convention,
**no attack test on this model can assert a published figure**, and section 13.4 says so and asserts
orderings instead.

### 9.3 Two time constants in series, not one

Section 5.2 established that the 2252's own attack and release are locked together and cannot be
adjusted separately [11], and that a box with seven attack times therefore cannot be getting them
from the detector. So the sidechain has:

1. the detector's own log-domain averaging, `τ_det = 0.0259·C_T/I_T`, fixed and unpublished, which
   is what makes it a true RMS rather than a rectifier; then
2. the panel's attack and release network, operating on the detector's decibel-domain output.

**That is an inference, and it is forced by the parts.** It also has a consequence worth stating:
**the fastest attack the box can achieve is limited by the detector, not by the ATTACK switch.** At
the 0.03 ms position the panel is asking for something three or four orders of magnitude faster than
any sensible RMS averaging window, and it will not get it. A model that implements 30 µs literally
will be faster than the hardware, and will lose the RMS detector's characteristic under-reading of
transients (5.4) exactly where it matters most.

I cannot put a number on `τ_det` because API publish neither C_T nor I_T. THAT's own application
circuit uses C_T = 10 µF with R_T = 2.2 MΩ [11], and the dbx 160 dossier derives τ ≈ 34.5 ms for that
box from its schematic [dbx-160 5.5]. Both are other people's boxes. Section 11 carries `τ_det` as an
**estimate** with a stated range and a note that it is the model's single least-supported constant.

### 9.4 The shape of the release, and the one thing API say about it

Nothing in any API document says whether the release is exponential, linear in decibels, or
programme dependent. Robjohns' one design criticism was that he "would have preferred an automatic
release-time mode, instead of the continuously variable option" [14], which tells us there is no
programme-dependent release *in the panel sense* — no Auto position, no dual time constant that API
advertise.

But section 5.2 says the detector's own decay is **linear in decibels**, a constant dB/s [11]. So the
box does have a programme-dependent recovery, in the sense that the time to recover depends on how
much gain reduction there is to recover from; it just is not called that and is not adjustable. The
dbx 160 dossier makes the same point about its own detector and derives a published release table
from it [dbx-160 5.5].

**The model should therefore not put an exponential release on the detector stage and a second
exponential on the panel stage.** The detector's stage is a log-domain filter with the dbx dossier's
`dL/dt = (D/τ)(exp((L_inst − L)/D) − 1)` behaviour [dbx-160 10.2]; the panel stage is an ordinary
decibel-domain one-pole with two coefficients. Getting those the right way round is most of what
makes this box behave like an RMS compressor rather than a peak one.

### 10.2 What is conspicuously absent

**No total harmonic distortion figure, of any kind, under any condition.** Not on the specification
sheet, not in any manual, not in the review. API publish frequency response, noise, maximum level
and impedances for this box and no distortion number at all. That is unusual for a 2001 product from
a company that trades on its discrete op amps, and it means **every distortion figure this dossier
could assert belongs to a THAT chip and not to an API compressor** (4.2).

Also absent: any ratio calibration, any ballistics measurement or tolerance, any knee width, the VCA
grade, the detector's timing components, the meter's ballistics or drive constant, and any statement
of the sidechain's volts-per-decibel. Section 13 tests none of them.

### 10.3 Two conditions that are missing from figures that are present

**"Signal to Noise Ratio: 120 dB" has no reference level, no bandwidth and no weighting** [4]. With
a +32 dBu maximum output, 120 dB below that is −88 dBu, which is a plausible unweighted wideband
noise floor for a box like this and is also exactly what you would get by writing down the 2180's
own ">120 dB dynamic range" [10] and calling it the unit's. I do not know which it is and section
13 does not assert it.

**"±0.5 dB, 20 Hz to 50 kHz" has no stated gain setting** [4]. The 2180's frequency response varies
with gain [10, fig. 3], so a response specification for a VCA compressor without a gain condition is
incomplete. It is still a real bound on the audio path and section 13 uses it as one, with the
limitation stated.

### 10.4 The calibration points that do exist

Four numbers in the documents behave like calibration points rather than like specifications,
because each one ties a control to a level:

1. **0 VU = +4 dBu** [2] [3]. The meter's reference.
2. **Auto make-up drives the output toward +4 dBu** [2]. The auto circuit's reference, and the same
   number.
3. **The threshold scale is in dBu**, linear, 4 dB per detent, +20 to −20 [3] and 2.3.
4. **The manual make-up range is 0 to +24 dB** [3], which 3.6 shows is exactly the auto circuit's
   maximum.

Those four are the whole basis for the static tests in 13.1 and 13.2, and they are enough to anchor
threshold, make-up and metering without inventing anything. They are not enough to anchor ratio,
knee or ballistics, and nothing else in the documents is either.

### 10.5 What has never been measured

No independent laboratory measurement of an API 2500 exists that I could find: no frequency
response, no distortion, no noise, no ratio calibration, no attack or release measurement, no null
test against any emulation. The DAFx-2025 dataset that gives the SSL bus compressor a public
measured ground truth [SSL-Gbus 10.2] has no API equivalent.

Universal Audio state that they had "exclusive access to API's schematics" and analysed "two classic
'golden unit' API 2500s" [15]. That is a claim about what UA had, it is useful for knowing that the
documents exist, and **it is not a measurement and not a target.** No test in section 13 compares
anything to it.

## 11. Recommended DSP design

Target: 44.1 to 192 kHz, real time, one `Processor` hosting the model behind the lab's existing
`model` switch. Section 12 argues what may and may not be borrowed; this section assumes that
argument's conclusions.

### 11.1 The shape of the engine

```
                         ┌──────────── detector tap, OLD ────────────────────┐
                         │  (post gain-cell, post make-up: y = x + M − GR)   │
 in_L ─┬────────────────►│                                                   │
       │                 │                                                   │
       │   ┌─ NEW ◄──────┴── x  (or the external side-chain input)           │
       │   │                                                                 │
       │  TYPE ──► THRUST tilt ──► RMS detector ──► − T ──► knee f() ──► × α │
       │                              (log domain,           │               │
       │                               τ_det)                ▼               │
       │                                          attack / release one-pole  │
       │                                                     ▼               │
       │                                          link mix with other chan   │
       │                                                     ▼               │
       │                                                 GR (dB) ────────────┘
       │                                                     │
       │                                       M = manual, or α·(4 − T) auto
       │                                                     ▼
       └──────────────► × 10^((M − GR)/20) ──┬──► blend with dry ──► out_L
                                             └──► meter
```

Six properties of that diagram are the model.

- **The audio path is one multiply and one blend.** No filters, no saturator, no oversampled stage
  in the audio path itself (3.1). The output transformer and the 2510/2520 op amps are real and
  their behaviour is entirely unpublished (10.2), so they are not modelled; 11.9 offers a
  clearly-labelled spoof extra instead.
- **The detector tap is a parameter.** `api_type` selects whether the detector reads `x` or
  `x + M − GR[n−1]`. That is the whole of the OLD/NEW switch and everything in section 6 falls out of
  it, rather than being tuned in.
- **The THRUST filter and the detector are downstream of the tap**, so they filter whichever signal
  the tap chose (3.2).
- **The detector is a log-domain RMS filter, and the panel ballistics are a separate stage after
  it** (5.2, 9.3). Two time constants in series, not one, and only the second is on the panel.
- **The knee is a soft function applied to `d − T` before the ratio divider** (7.3), not a knee-width
  parameter and not a polynomial blend of two curves.
- **The link mixes decibels, not powers** (3.5), in a set proportion, with both detectors live.

Latency is zero. The audio path never consults the detector's future.

### 11.2 Parameter table

All model parameters are prefixed `api_`. Stepped controls are integer indices, because every
stepped control on the hardware is a rotary switch or a cycling push switch with detents.

**Which controls use the lab's shared parameters instead of their own id.** The lab defines four
shared ids once, in `src/dsp/mod.rs::param_specs`, in the group `extras`: `link`, `mix`, `sc_hpf`
and `bypass`. My rulings, one line each:

| shared id | ruling for this model |
|---|---|
| **`bypass`** | **Use it, and give the API no `api_bypass`.** The hardware's BYP is a relay hard-wire bypass that "routes the signal directly from the INPUT XLR to the OUTPUT XLR, without going through any electronics" [2] [3], which is what the shared parameter already means. ### 11.3 The static curve, in equations

Everything in decibels. `d` is the detector's output, `T` the threshold, `α` the ratio divider,
`M` the make-up.

```
α(R)      = 1 − 1/R                        // R = 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 10; α = 1 at ∞
knee f(u) = 0                                        u ≤ −W
            (u + W)² / (4W) · (α_scale)              −W < u < W      // quadratic bridge
            u                                        u ≥ W
G         = α · f(d − T)                   // gain reduction, dB, ≥ 0
M         = api_gain                       if api_makeup_manual
            α · max(0, 4 − T)              otherwise                 // 3.6, derived
d         = x                              if api_type == new        // x = input level, dB
            x + M − G[n−1]                 if api_type == old        // 6.1
y         = x + M − G
```

with the knee half-width `W` per position:

| position | `W` | status |
|---|---|---|
| HARD | 0 dB (exact corner) | API's own plot draws a point, not a narrow curve (7.2) |
| MED | **3 dB** | **estimate**; only the ratio to SOFT is published |
| SOFT | **5.4 dB** | **estimate**, fixed by MED × 1.81, which **is** derived from API's plot (7.2) |
| MED / SOFT offset | MED symmetric about `T`; SOFT centred **0.45·W below** `T` | derived from the plot's own corner positions (7.2) |

I want the labelling here to be exact, because it is the kind of thing an audit catches. **The 1.81
ratio and the asymmetry are derived from API's drawing. The 3 dB is a guess.** Anything that depends
only on the ratio is defensible; anything that depends on the absolute width is not, and section 13
tests only the former.

**In OLD, the whole set of equations above is implicit in `G`**, because `d` depends on `G`. Resolve
it the way the lab already resolves the SSL's loop [SSL-Gbus 11.1]: use `G[n−1]`. The one-sample
delay is the standard resolution of an instantaneous feedback loop, it costs nothing at any sample
rate this runs at, and it is what makes the closed-loop behaviour in 6.5 emerge rather than being
imposed.

**The OLD ratio compensation** (6.4). Given a printed ratio `R`, use

```
α_new = 1 − 1/R                                    // exact, from the circuit
α_old = R − 1        for R ≤ 10                    // so that 1 + α_old = R
α_old = 39           for the ∞ position            // 40:1, "above 20:1" [2]
```

and **say in the plug-in's help text that the OLD compensation is a choice, not a measurement.**
Without it, OLD tops out at 2:1 and the panel lies; with it, the ∞ detent is finite and the panel
still nearly lies. There is no arrangement in which the printed scale is honest in both positions
and it is better to say so than to hide it.

### 11.4 The gain cell

The Blackmer cell is exponential in decibels by construction (4.2), so the "model" of the cell is a
`powf`, and the only thing worth modelling about it is its imperfection.

```
g_db   = M − G                                         // make-up minus gain reduction
g_lin  = 10^(g_db / 20)
y      = x · g_lin · (1 − κ · api_drive · (x·g_lin / X0)²)
```

`κ` is a single odd-order term standing in for the cell's THD. Its **only** published anchor is
THAT's table (4.2): 0.005 % at 0 dBV and 0 dB gain, rising to 0.020 % at −15 dB of gain, for the A
grade. Two things follow and both are limitations:

- **Which grade API fitted is unpublished**, and the grades differ by a factor of six.
- **The rise with gain reduction is real and published**, and the model should reproduce the
  *direction*: distortion goes **up** as the cell attenuates. That is the opposite of the diode
  bridge, whose distortion falls as gain reduction rises [components crate, diode-bridge README],
  and getting it backwards would be the same class of error the bridge's README records.

So `api_drive` defaults to **0** and the cell is clean unless the user asks otherwise. A spoof
plug-in may have a drive control; a research dossier may not assert a distortion figure for a box
whose manufacturer publishes none.

### 11.5 The detector

Two stages, in this order.

**Stage one: the log-domain RMS filter.** Use the dbx dossier's formulation [dbx-160 10.2], which is
the same chip family's behaviour and is already derived there:

```
D          = V_T / K = 25.9 mV / 6.1 mV/dB = 4.246 dB      // the "thermal decibel"
L_inst     = 20·log10(|x_thrust|)                          // instantaneous level, dB
dL/dt      = (D / τ_det) · ( exp((L_inst − L)/D) − 1 )
```

with an exact discrete solution costing one `exp` and one `ln` per sample. That gives, for free and
without tuning: a decay that is **linear in decibels** at `D/τ_det` dB/s, an attack that accelerates
with the size of the step, and the crest-factor under-reading of 5.4. It is the single block that
makes this box behave like an RMS compressor.

`τ_det` is the model's weakest constant (9.3). It is exposed as `api_tau_det`, defaulted to **20 ms**
as an **estimate**, and the reason for that default is stated in 11.7 rather than hidden in a
constant.

**Stage two: the panel's ballistics.** An ordinary decibel-domain one-pole with two coefficients,
applied to the detector's output after the threshold, knee and ratio:

```
a = 1 − exp(−1 / (τ · fs))
G[n] = G[n−1] + (G_tgt > G[n−1] ? a_att : a_rel) · (G_tgt − G[n−1])
```

with `τ_att` and `τ_rel` straight off the panel. **Do not** make this stage log-domain as well; the
detector already is, and doubling it would produce a box that recovers twice as slowly as its panel
in a way that compounds with gain reduction.

### 11.6 THRUST, exactly

**LOUD: implement the patent's own network** (8.3). Its transfer function decomposes exactly into a
constant plus four first-order high-pass sections in parallel, because the branches are in parallel:

```
H(s) / R_f = 1/R0 + Σ_k  s·C_k / (1 + s·R_k·C_k)
```

so, with `R_f` = 10 kΩ,

| section | gain `a_k = R_f / R_k` | corner `f_k` |
|---|---|---|
| DC path | **0.1** | — |
| 1 | **0.3125** | **31.08 Hz** |
| 2 | **1.0** | **318.31 Hz** |
| 3 | **3.125** | **3108.5 Hz** |
| 4 | **10.0** | **31 831 Hz** |

then scale the sum by **0.5511** (−5.18 dB) to put unity at 632.5 Hz, which is where API's own plot
crosses 0 dB (8.3). Four one-poles and a constant: cheaper than a biquad cascade and exact to the
patent.

**And the fourth section is the one to get right.** Its corner is at 31.8 kHz, which is **above
Nyquist at 44.1 and 48 kHz**. Do not bilinear-transform it: prewarping cannot place a pole above
Nyquist and an unwarped bilinear will put it in the wrong place. Use **pole matching**, `p =
exp(−2π f_k / fs)` with the zero at `z = 1` and the gain normalised at a chosen reference, which is
well-conditioned for any `f_k`. Dropping the section instead costs **5.2 dB at 20 kHz** and 0.5 dB at
20 Hz, which is not acceptable in the filter whose whole purpose is the band edges.

**MED: implement API's published curve, and say that is what it is** (8.4). A low shelf of −15 dB
below ≈200 Hz and a high shelf of +15 dB above ≈2.3 kHz, flat between, normalised to unity in the
plateau. I have no circuit for it, my one hypothesis was ruled out by arithmetic, and the model
should carry a comment saying so rather than implying a derivation it does not have. The measured
ramp slope of 16 dB/decade is shallower than a first-order shelf; match the **endpoints and the
corners**, which are what API drew, and accept a steeper transition than the drawing shows, or spend
a second-order pair on it if the transition matters.

**NORM: nothing.** Not a bypassed filter, not a flat biquad. The signal goes straight to the
detector.

### 11.8 Oversampling, and where antiderivative antialiasing serves better

**The audio path needs no oversampling.** It contains one multiply and one blend. There is no static
waveshaper in it at all unless `api_drive` is turned up, and the modulation product of a
slowly-varying gain against audio does not alias in any way that oversampling the audio helps.

**The sidechain is where the aliasing is**, and it has three static nonlinearities in series:

1. **The detector's rectifier**, `|x|`. This is the sharpest one, it generates harmonics without
   limit, and it is the textbook case for **antiderivative antialiasing**: the first antiderivative
   of `|x|` is `x|x|/2`, in closed form, cheap and exact. **Use ADAA here, not oversampling.**
2. **The log conversion**, `20·log10(|x|)`. Also static, also unbounded near zero, and also
   ADAA-able, though the antiderivative is `x(ln|x| − 1)` and needs care at the origin. Its output
   is heavily smoothed by the log-domain filter immediately after it, so the aliasing that survives
   is at very low level; ADAA on the rectifier alone recovers most of the benefit.
3. **The knee diode**, `f(u)`. Static, smooth, and applied to an already-smoothed envelope. It does
   not need either treatment.

**And one place where oversampling is the right tool and ADAA is not: THRUST at 44.1 kHz.** The
filter's top section sits above Nyquist (11.6). Pole matching handles it correctly, but if a future
version of the model ever wants the filter's phase right as well as its magnitude, running the
sidechain at 2× is simpler than arguing about it. `api_oversample` defaults to 2× and applies **to
the sidechain only**, which is a real saving over the lab's other VCA models and is a direct
consequence of the audio path being empty.

### 11.9 Metering, stereo and hygiene

- **The meter reads the control voltage** (3.2), which for a Blackmer cell is decibels of gain
  reduction (4.3). Draw the gain-reduction scale reversed, 0 at the right, and **let it pin at 20 dB**
  while the engine keeps reducing to 30 (2.6). A meter that rescales itself is showing the user
  something the hardware never showed them.
- **Two independent detectors, always**, with the link mixing their outputs (3.5). Never one detector
  driving both channels; API say in as many words that they avoided that [2] [3].
- **The `2500` variant should offer `api_tilt`** as a ±2 dB asymmetry in the control voltage that
  affects the compressed signal only, because API say it "DOES NOT affect the uncompressed signal"
  [2]. On the `2500plus` variant, hide it.
- **Both channels should be allowed to differ.** A real 2500's two detectors disagree about 0 dB by
  up to a few decibels before trimming (5.6). Whether the model should reproduce that is a taste
  question; whether it should *pretend* the hardware is matched is not, and the help text should say
  what the TILT trim was for.
- **The blend happens inside the gain-cell block on the hardware** (3.1). In the model it is an
  ordinary crossfade or parallel sum, and the only thing that matters is that `parallel` adds the
  full dry signal to a scaled wet one while `crossfade` trades them, exactly as API describe [3].

The transfer curve, live, with the ratio and knee drawn as the model actually computes them, because
section 6 means the curve genuinely changes when TYPE changes and a user who cannot see that will
think the switch does nothing. Alongside it, the THRUST response of the currently selected position,
because it is the box's signature and it is a filter nobody can hear directly. And the gain
reduction, on API's reversed scale.

## 12. Reuse, assessed honestly

An audit of this codebase found **three tube stages that were three different circuits wearing one
word**, and the components crate's README records the same lesson from the other side: the CL 1B
earned the photocell crate its boundary by *refusing* the T4's timing while still sharing the
photoconductor's distortion law [16]. This section applies that test twice, to two different parts,
and gets two different answers.

### 12.0 A correction to the brief, offered because it changes the question

I was asked to treat "a VCA component crate" as a decision about to be made. **It has already been
made and the code is in the working tree.** `noob-electrical-components` contains an untracked crate
`noob-electrical-components-blackmer-cell`, wired into the facade, with `BlackmerCell::TYPICAL` and
`BlackmerCell::UNTRIMMED` presets and a `gain_db(v_pos, v_neg)` entry point that takes **millivolts**
rather than decibels. Its module documentation already says why:

> "This crate is the **cell**, not 'a VCA'... 'VCA' is a functional category covering Blackmer's
> log-antilog cell, the operational transconductance amplifier and a field-effect transistor used as
> a variable resistor, which share a word rather than an equation... a component called `Vca` would
> have been the fourth [tube stage]."

and it records two documented users, the dbx 160 and the SSL, with the Distressor as an inferred
one whose "asymmetry of evidence is recorded here deliberately."

The workspace README has not caught up: it still lists "VCA" as an unbuilt candidate. So the useful
thing I can do is not to argue for a decision already taken, but to say **where the API 2500 lands
against the boundary that crate already drew**, and whether it shapes it or merely consumes it.

### 12.1 The Blackmer cell: yes, and it is the same arithmetic, plainly

**The API 2500 needs exactly the arithmetic the crate already holds, and needs nothing added to it.**

That is the plain answer the brief asked for, and here is the evidence rather than the assertion.
The three units' gain-cell numbers are not similar; they are the **same numbers from the same
datasheet**:

| tolerance | ±0.1 mV/dB | ±0.1 mV/dB | **±0.1 mV/dB** | — |

There is no interpretation in that column. The API 2500's gain cell is a THAT 2180 and the crate

**What the API adds, and what it does not.** It adds a **third user** and it adds nothing to the

the three documented ones — a magazine reviewer counting chips in 2001 [14] against two manufacturer

because "reported from inspection" and "lettered on the manufacturer's own drawing" are not the same
standard and pretending otherwise is how a component crate rots.

**And the boundary holds without strain.** The crate holds the part: the law, the constant, its
tolerance, its temperature coefficient, its linearity, its distortion surface. It knows nothing about
the 60 kΩ input impedance, the 2510 and 2520 op amps, the transformer, the detector, the threshold,
or how the control voltage is derived. Every one of those differs between the API and the SSL, and
none of them wants to move.

### 12.2 The RMS detector: yes, and the dbx dossier's own trigger condition is now met

This is the finding I did not expect and it is the one worth acting on.

The dbx 160 dossier refused to extract its RMS detector, and gave a precise reason and a precise
condition for revisiting:

> "**Therefore: the RMS detector stays inside the dbx model.** One documented user, no second one in
> sight, and the crate's own rule refuses it. This is the same refusal the CL 1B made about the T4
> cell... **If a second true-RMS detector ever arrives in the lab (a dbx 165, a 163, or any of the
> 900-series modules would bring one), revisit it then, and extract the log-domain filter and the
> crest-factor behaviour, which are properties of the technique rather than of this box.**"
> [dbx-160 11.4]

**The API 2500 is that second user, and it is better documented than the first.**

The dbx 160 contains a potted **dbx model 208**, which has no published datasheet, and that dossier
uses the **THAT 2252** datasheet as its best available proxy, citing THAT's own statement that the
2252 is "Based on dbx technology" [dbx-160 5.1]. The API 2500 is reported to contain **an actual THAT
2252** [14]. So the lab's second true-RMS unit brings the very part the first one had to borrow
numbers from.

|---|---|---|
| detector | dbx model 208, potted module | **THAT 2252** |

**So: extract a log-domain RMS detector component**, holding the technique and nothing else — the
log-domain one-pole and its time constant, the thermal decibel `D = V_T/K`, the linear-in-decibels
decay, the level-dependent attack, the crest-factor error table, the bandwidth-versus-level table —
with the dbx 160 as a documented-by-schematic user and the API 2500 as a reported-by-inspection one,
and the asymmetry recorded.

**And the boundary is drawn by a refusal, exactly as the photocell's was.** The dbx 160 has **no**
attack or release control, because for that box the detector's locked pair *is* the ballistics
[dbx-160 5.5]. The API 2500 has seven attack times and seven release positions, in a **second stage
after the detector** (9.3). If the component tried to hold "the ballistics", the API would have to
either give up its panel or corrupt the dbx's central finding. It holds the *detector*, and each
machine puts its own timing after it. That is the CL 1B's refusal in a new place, and a boundary
tested by a real refusal is worth more than one argued from first principles [16].

**One caution.** The dbx dossier's whole headline is that four published behaviours "fall out of one
number, τ ≈ 34.5 ms", derived from two components on dbx's schematic [dbx-160 5.5]. The API's
equivalent number is unpublished and is this dossier's weakest constant (11.7). The component must
therefore take `τ` as a parameter and must not carry a default, because a default would let this
model inherit the dbx's measured number as if it were the API's.

### 12.3 What only looks shared, item by item

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ratio** | a switch that changes a detector gain and a DC offset; slope varies with gain reduction [SSL-Gbus 12.2] | `α = 1 − 1/R`, exactly, from the circuit [dbx-160 6.1] | eight hand-fitted curves in a `Curve` struct of tuned constants [Distressor 7.4] | **`α = 1 − 1/R` in NEW and `1 + α` in OLD — two laws on one knob** (6.2) | **not shared.** The dbx's law is right and the API needs it *plus a second one selected by a front-panel switch*. A shared "ratio" would have to grow a topology argument to serve this box, which is a machine concern. |
| **knee** | not a parameter at all; a diode's turn-on inside a loop [SSL-Gbus 6.4] | a diode, and dbx say so in a patent [dbx-160 6.2] | per-position widths, 30 dB down to a couple [Distressor 7.4] | **three switch positions from a diode's compensation** (7.3) | **not shared**, though three of the four are diodes. They are three *different* diode arrangements and only the API's is switched in three steps. What is shared is a modelling *idea*, and ideas do not go in crates. |
| **detector** | precision full-wave rectifier into a passive RC [SSL-Gbus 12.2] | **log-domain true RMS** | band emphasis plus a branching envelope [Distressor 3.3] | **log-domain true RMS** | **shared with the dbx and only the dbx** (12.2). Two of four, and the other two are different objects. |
| **attack / release** | two resistors and a capacitor forming a divider that costs gain reduction [SSL-Gbus 12.2] | **none**; the detector's locked pair is the whole ballistics | continuous, program- and ratio-dependent, branching [Distressor 7.3] | **a dB-domain one-pole *after* an RMS detector**, plus a topology-dependent speed-up (6.5) | **not shared.** Four boxes, four mechanisms. This is the trap in its clearest form. |
| **link** | a **maximum** over independently rectified channels [SSL-Gbus 5.5] | **true RMS power summing** at the detector [dbx-160 10.6] | phase link, image link, both [Distressor 7.9] | **a percentage mix of two decibel-domain CVs**, both detectors live (3.5) | **not shared.** Four boxes, four different operators: max, power sum, phase detect, dB average. The lab's shared `link` toggle is an on/off switch and nothing more, which is the right size for a shared parameter. |
| **sidechain filter** | a six-position high-pass, 30 to 185 Hz [SSL-Gbus 2.1] | none; dbx document an external EQ instead [dbx-160 10.9] | band emphasis [Distressor 3.3] | **a half-order tilt, and no high-pass at all** (8.5) | **not shared.** The lab's `sc_hpf` is a real shared extra and the API should offer it as one, clearly marked as not on the hardware. THRUST is a different object and must not be presented through that parameter. |
| **make-up gain** | summed into the audio VCA's CV, permanently in circuit [SSL-Gbus 2.1] | an output pot | an output control | **summed into the cell's CV and, in OLD, inside the control loop** (6.3) | **not shared.** The SSL's is the closest and even it is outside the loop. |
| **bypass** | the IN switch is *not* a bypass; needs its own parameter [SSL-Gbus 11.2] | 160A only | — | **a relay hard-wire bypass, which is what the shared `bypass` already means** | **shared.** The one place the API is simpler than the SSL. |

### 12.4 What the API 2500 shares with the lab as it stands

- **The Blackmer cell component**, as a third user that consumes it unchanged (12.1).
- **A log-domain RMS detector component**, as the second user that justifies extracting it (12.2).
- **`dsp::vu`**, the VU model, and the transfer-curve stream, and the stereo plumbing. Infrastructure,
  excluded from the components crate by its own README [16], shared without ceremony.
- **The oversamplers**, though it needs them less than any other model in the lab, because its audio
  path is empty (11.8).
- **The one-pole and biquad sections**, for the THRUST filter. Also infrastructure.
- **`bypass`, `mix` and `link`** from the shared parameter registry, with `link` gaining a companion
  `api_link_pct` rather than being duplicated (11.2).
- **Nothing** with the photocell or the diode bridge. Different families entirely, and the bridge's
  distortion behaviour is the *opposite* of the 2180's (11.4), which is a good reason to keep them
  apart even in the reader's head.

### 12.5 The blunt version

**New module, `dsp::api2500`.** Not a variant inside `dsp::vca` and not a variant inside whatever the
SSL becomes, because the TYPE switch changes the shape of the engine and a variant flag that
reroutes the detector is a different engine wearing a flag.

**Consume the Blackmer cell crate unchanged**, and record that this unit's part identification comes
from a reviewer rather than a drawing.

**Extract the log-domain RMS detector.** The dbx dossier asked for a second user before it would, and
this is it — carrying a published datasheet for the exact part, where the first user had to borrow
one. Hold the technique; hold no ballistics; take `τ` as a parameter with no default.

**Share nothing named ratio, knee, detector-with-ballistics, link, sidechain filter or make-up
gain.** Every one of those is a different circuit across the lab's four VCA units, and the audit that
found three tube stages under one word found it because somebody made exactly this trade for exactly
these reasons.

The honest summary: this unit is **cheap to build** because the gain cell and the detector both
transfer, and it is **worth building** because its front panel contains a topology switch and nothing
else in the lab has one.

## 13. Test plan

**Two standards this repository enforces, and one of them bites unusually hard here.** Every test
asserts a **published figure**, names it, and cites it. And an audit found nine tests across five
plug-ins that asserted the model's own output where a published figure existed, one of which compared
an estimate with itself [16]. So no test below has an expected value the model would produce.

**API publish less than any manufacturer this lab has researched except Empirical Labs.** No
distortion figure, no ratio calibration, no ballistics measurement, no tolerance on any timing
number, no knee width, no meter constant (10.2). So the tests below draw on five kinds of figure, and
each test says which kind it is using:

- **(P)** a figure **printed** by API — a panel legend, a specification line, a sentence of the
  manual.
- **(D)** a figure API **drew** — measured out of the path coordinates of API's own vector artwork,
  with the measurement stated here and the artwork's URL in section 14.
- **(T)** a figure from the **THRUST patent** [8], including arithmetic performed on its own printed
  component values.
- **(M)** a figure from a **component manufacturer's datasheet**, THAT Corporation [10] [11] [12].
- **(A)** an **analytic** result derived in this file from the topology. These test the
  implementation against the algebra, and they are **not** hardware measurements; each one says so.
  They exist because this box's central claim is architectural, and an architecture can be checked
  against its own mathematics even when nobody has measured the box.

Unless a test says otherwise: 1 kHz sine, `api_variant` = `2500plus`, `api_type` = `new`, `api_knee`
= `med`, `api_thrust` = `norm`, `link` off, `api_oversample` = 2×, run at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.

### 13.1 Structure and static behaviour

1. **Bypass is exact.** `bypass` on: output equals input to 1e-6.
   *Figure:* none needed. This is an identity the plug-in owes its user, and on this box it is also
   what the hardware does, since BYP is a relay that routes the input to the output "without going
   through any electronics" **(P)** [2] [3].

2. **Unity at zero control voltage.** `api_in` off, `api_makeup_manual` on, `api_gain` = 0: output
   equals input to **±0.1 dB**.
   *Figure:* THAT's "Gain at 0 V Control Voltage: 0.0 dB, **±0.1 dB**" for the A grade **(M)** [10].
   The tolerance is the manufacturer's, not mine.

3. **Manual make-up is exact across its published range.** With no gain reduction, sweeping
   `api_gain` from 0 to +24 dB changes the output by exactly the set amount, ±0.1 dB.
   *Figure:* "Continuously variable between **0dB and +24dB**" **(P)** [3], confirmed independently as
   "Up to 24dB of make-up gain is available" **(R)** [14]. Tolerance from test 2.

4. **The audio path has no filters.** With the compressor out, response is flat within **±0.5 dB from
   20 Hz to 50 kHz** at every sample rate that reaches 50 kHz.
   *Figure:* API's own "**±0.5dB 20Hz to 50kHz**" **(P)** [4]. **Stated limitation:** API give no gain
   setting for that figure (10.3), and the model's audio path contains no filters at all, so it
   should be flat to floating-point precision. The API figure is used as the loosest published bound
   rather than as a target.

5. **Nothing in the sidechain reaches the audio.** Sweeping `api_thrust` through all three positions
   with `api_in` off leaves the output bit-identical.
   *Figure:* "This circuit places a filter **in front of the RMS detector**" **(P)** [2] [3] [8]. A
   model that put THRUST in the audio path would fail this, and it is the cheapest guard against the
   commonest possible misreading of this box.

6. **The gain-reduction range exceeds the meter's range.** With `api_ratio` = ∞ and a signal 40 dB
   over threshold, the engine produces more than **20 dB** and up to **30 dB** of gain reduction while
   the meter reads 20.
   *Figure:* "The range of the meter is from 0 to 20 db of gain reduction. **The compressor can
   compress up to 30 dB of reduction**" **(P)** [2].

### 13.2 The threshold, the make-up, and the auto circuit

7. **The threshold dial is linear in decibels.** The six printed detent values land on the parameter
   at exactly the six measured angles.
   *Figure:* API's own printed scale **`+20 +8 0 -4 -12 -20`** at −150°, −60°, 0°, +30°, +90°, +150°
   **(D)** [5], and 2.3's arithmetic showing all six fall on 4 dB per 30° dot. This asserts API's
   drawing, not my law.

8. **Auto make-up holds a +4 dBu input at +4 dBu out.** With `api_makeup_manual` off, a steady
   +4 dBu input produces a +4 dBu output for **every** combination of threshold and ratio, to
   ±0.5 dB.
   *Figure:* "rotating the THRESH control will increase the output level until the output level is
   around **+4 dBu**, and then... it will maintain an even output level, **allowing THRESH and RATIO
   adjustments without having to re-adjust the make-up gain**" **(P)** [2]. This is the strongest
   published behavioural statement API make about this box and it constrains threshold, ratio and
   make-up simultaneously.

9. **Auto make-up's maximum is 24 dB.** `api_ratio` = ∞, `api_threshold` = −20 dBu, auto make-up: the
   applied make-up is **24 dB**, ±0.1.
   *Figure:* the manual pot's published range, "**0dB to +24dB**" **(P)** [3], which 3.6 shows the auto
   law reproduces at its extreme from three other published numbers. **This test asserts a published
   figure that the auto law was not fitted to**, which is the only reason it is worth writing.

10. **In NEW, make-up gain does not change gain reduction.** Fixed input, fixed threshold and ratio,
    `api_type` = `new`: sweeping `api_gain` 0 to 24 dB leaves gain reduction constant to ±0.05 dB.
    *Figure:* **(A)**, from `d = x` in 6.2. Not a hardware measurement; it is the control condition
    for test 11.

11. **In OLD, make-up gain does change gain reduction, and by a known amount.** Same setup,
    `api_type` = `old`: raising `api_gain` by 10 dB produces the same increase in gain reduction as
    lowering `api_threshold` by 10 dB, to ±0.5 dB.
    *Figure:* Robjohns' published observation, "in the feed-backwards mode, the level sensing is
    derived from **after the make-up gain control**... increasing the make-up gain results in **even
    further compression**" **(R)** [14], made quantitative by 6.3. **This is the test that catches the
    commonest wrong assumption about this box**, which is that OLD and NEW differ only in flavour.

### 13.3 The TYPE switch and the topology

12. **NEW gives the printed ratio.** With `api_knee` = `hard` and the signal well above threshold,
    the measured slope is `1/R` for each of the six finite printed ratios, to ±2 %.
    *Figure:* API's printed ratio scale **`1.5 2 3 4 6 10 ∞`** **(P)** [2] [3], plus API's statement
    that in NEW "the RMS detector sends a signal to the VCA that is an **exact ratio** of the desired
    compression set by the RATIO control" **(P)** [3]. Those two sentences together are what make this
    an assertion about API's panel rather than about my α.

13. **OLD with an uncompensated divider would give 2:1 at the ∞ setting.** Run the model with the OLD
    compensation of 11.3 disabled: the ∞ position measures **2.00 : 1**, ±2 %.
    *Figure:* **(A)**, from `R_old = 1 + α` with α = 1 (6.2). Not a hardware measurement. It exists
    because 11.3's compensation is a **choice**, and a test that pins the uncompensated behaviour
    makes the choice visible instead of burying it.

14. **OLD is faster than NEW by 1 + α.** At the 4:1 setting and a fixed attack time, the OLD-mode
    10-to-90 % gain-reduction rise time is **1/(1 + α)** of the NEW-mode one, ±10 %.
    *Figure:* **(A)**, derived in 6.5. Not a hardware measurement, and no measurement of this box's
    ballistics exists in either topology (9.2). It asserts that the loop was implemented as a loop
    rather than tuned to sound right.

15. **The external side chain is inert in OLD.** With `api_sc_ext` on and a signal on the sidechain
    input, `api_type` = `old` produces the same output as `api_sc_ext` off.
    *Figure:* "NOTE: **Only active when TYPE is set to NEW**" **(P)** [3].

16. **The side chain normals to the input.** `api_sc_ext` on with no external signal connected
    behaves identically to `api_sc_ext` off.
    *Figure:* the rear-panel legend, "**Normals to XLR INPUT**" **(P)** [2].

### 13.4 Ratio and knee

17. **The ratio in OLD cannot be tested against a published figure, and I will not invent one.** See
    6.4: the panel prints one scale for two topologies, API never say which position it calibrates,
    and the compensation in 11.3 is my choice. Test 13 pins the uncompensated behaviour; nothing
    tests the compensated behaviour, because nothing published constrains it.

18. **HARD is a corner.** With `api_knee` = `hard`, the second derivative of the transfer curve is
    zero everywhere except at a single sample-resolution point at the threshold.
    *Figure:* API's own knee plot, whose HARD curve is two straight segments meeting at one point
    with no rounding at all **(D)** [2], and API's prose "Sharp response curve... **sudden transition
    to set ratio**" **(P)** [3].

19. **SOFT's knee is 1.81 times MED's.** Measure each knee width as the input range over which the
    local slope lies between 5 % and 95 % of its final value. The ratio of SOFT's to MED's is
    **1.81**, ±5 %.
    *Figure:* API's own knee plot, whose MED rounding spans 22.8 pt and whose SOFT rounding spans
    41.3 pt of the same drawing **(D)** [2]. **This is the only quantitative statement about the knee
    that exists anywhere**, and it is a ratio, so it survives the fact that the plot's axes are
    uncalibrated (7.2).

20. **SOFT's knee is biased below the threshold and MED's is not.** MED's knee is centred on the
    threshold to within 5 % of its width; SOFT's centre is below it.
    *Figure:* API's plot again **(D)** [2]: MED's rounding is centred on the HARD corner to within
    0.05 pt, and SOFT's is centred 9.30 pt below it, while both end at the same point.

21. **The absolute knee widths in decibels are not tested.** No figure exists (7.2). The `3 dB` half
    width in 11.3 is an **estimate** and asserting it would be comparing an estimate with itself,
    which is the specific failure the audit found.

### 13.5 THRUST

22. **LOUD's slope is 10 dB per decade.** Measure the model's THRUST LOUD response at 20 Hz and
    20 kHz; the difference divided by three decades is **10 dB/decade**, and it is within **±0.5 dB**
    per decade.
    *Figure:* API's "a slope of **10dB per decade (-3dB/8va)**" **(P)** [3] [7], and the patent's claim
    of "**approximately +10 decibels per decade**" **(T)** [8]. The ±0.5 tolerance is mine and is
    justified in the test's comment by the patent's own network measuring 9.77 (8.3).

23. **LOUD's endpoints are ±15 dB.** Normalised to unity at its own pivot, the LOUD response is
    **−15 dB at 20 Hz and +15 dB at 20 kHz**, ±1.5 dB.
    *Figure:* API's prose, "**down 15 dB at 20 Hz and up 15dB at 20K Hz**" **(P)** [2]. The ±1.5 dB
    tolerance covers the spread between that prose, API's own plotted curve (−14.19 / +14.54) and the
    patent network (−15.55 / +13.75), all three of which are tabulated in 8.3.

24. **LOUD pivots near the geometric centre of the band.** Its 0 dB crossing is between **600 and
    660 Hz**.
    *Figure:* API's own plotted curve crosses 0 dB at **625 Hz**, measured from its path coordinates
    **(D)** [2]. The window is centred on that and is wide enough to include the geometric mean of
    20 Hz and 20 kHz, 632.5 Hz.

25. **LOUD ripples, and the ripple is bounded.** The local slope measured over sixth-decade windows
    stays between **7 and 13 dB/decade** across 20 Hz to 20 kHz.
    *Figure:* the patent's own component values give a local slope between 7.31 and 12.88 **(T)** [8],
    computed in 8.3. The patent calls the response "a **stepped linear approximation**" [8], so a
    model that ripples is right and one that does not has replaced the circuit with its average.

26. **The top section survives at 44.1 kHz.** At `fs` = 44.1 kHz the model's LOUD response at 20 kHz
    is within **0.5 dB** of the analogue prototype's.
    *Figure:* the analogue prototype is the patent's own network **(T)** [8]. Its 31.8 kHz section is
    above Nyquist at 44.1 kHz, and dropping it costs **5.2 dB at 20 kHz** (11.6), so this test fails
    loudly for the obvious wrong implementation.

27. **MED is flat in the mid band.** Between **250 Hz and 2 kHz** the MED response varies by less than
    **1 dB**.
    *Figure:* API's "A slight attenuation of the low frequencies and a slight boost in the high
    frequencies, **with a flat mid-range**" **(P)** [3], and API's own plotted MED curve, whose middle
    segment is drawn exactly horizontal between 167 Hz and 2.34 kHz **(D)** [2].

28. **MED and LOUD reach the same band edges.** Their 20 Hz values agree to within 1 dB and their
    20 kHz values agree to within 1 dB.
    *Figure:* API's own plot **(D)** [2]: MED is −14.76 / +14.95 and LOUD is −14.19 / +14.54. This is
    worth asserting because it is the one thing about MED that a reader of the prose would get wrong —
    "slight" refers to where, not to how much (8.4).

29. **NORM is exactly flat.** With `api_thrust` = `norm` the sidechain response is unity at every
    frequency, bit-exact.
    *Figure:* "the THRUST filter is not inserted in the path to the RMS detector" **(P)** [3]. Not a
    flat biquad. Nothing.

### 13.6 The detector really is true RMS

30. **A square wave and a sine of equal RMS give equal gain reduction.** Same RMS level, same
    settings: gain reduction agrees to **±0.2 dB**, where a peak detector would differ by 3.0 dB.
    *Figure:* the detector is specified as "**True RMS Response**" **(M)** [11]. The 3.0 dB is the
    crest-factor difference between the two waveforms and is what the test is discriminating against.

31. **The crest-factor error follows THAT's published table.** With 1 ms pulse repetition, the
    detector under-reads by **0.2 dB at crest factor 3.5, 0.5 dB at 5, and 1.0 dB at 8**, each ±0.3 dB.
    *Figure:* THAT's own crest-factor table, at their own stated condition of a 1 ms pulse repetition
    rate **(M)** [11]. **This is the single best test in this file**: a three-point published curve,
    with a stated measurement condition, for a behaviour no other model in the lab has.

32. **The decay is linear in decibels.** After a step down, the detector's output falls at a constant
    dB/s, straight to within **±0.3 dB** over 20 dB of fall.
    *Figure:* "the natural release of the exponential time weighting is a decaying exponential in the
    linear world. This maps to a **straight line** in the log representation" **(M)** [11].

33. **The attack accelerates with step size.** A 30 dB step reaches 90 % of its final detector output
    in less time than a 10 dB step does.
    *Figure:* THAT's account of the log-domain filter, in which "for sudden increases in the input
    current, the current available to charge the capacitor C_T is proportional to the **square of the
    short-term increase**" **(M)** [11]. An **ordering**, not a magnitude, because THAT publish the
    mechanism and not a table for it.

### 13.7 Ballistics

34. **The attack ladder is ordered and spans three decades.** The seven positions give monotonically
    increasing attack times, and the ratio of the slowest to the fastest is **1000**, ±10 %.
    *Figure:* API's printed ladder **`.03 .1 .3 1 3 10 30`** ms **(P)** [2] [3], spelled out in words
    as "30 microseconds... 30 m/sec" [2]. **Stated limitation:** this asserts the *ratio*, not the
    absolute times, because **API publish no measurement procedure and no tolerance** (9.2) and
    "attack time" is ambiguous by about a factor of three between the four common conventions.

35. **The release ladder is ordered and spans 40:1.** Same form as test 34.
    *Figure:* API's printed ladder **`.05 .1 .2 .5 1 2`** s **(P)** [2] [3], same limitation.

36. **The VARIABLE pot's endpoints are 0.05 and 3 seconds.** At its extremes the effective release
    time is 0.05 s and 3 s, to ±10 %.
    *Figure:* "Variable release times continuously variable between **50m/sec and 3 seconds**" **(P)**
    [3]. The endpoints are published even though the taper is not, so the endpoints are what is
    asserted; the intermediate marks are a **derived** log taper (2.3) and are not asserted.

37. **The absolute attack and release times are not tested against a figure.** No API document states
    what its numbers measure (9.2). Any bound I wrote would be my convention asserted against my own
    implementation.

38. **The detector's own time constant is not tested.** It is an **estimate** (11.7). Testing it would
    be comparing a guess with itself.

### 13.8 Link, stereo and metering

39. **IND is genuinely independent.** With `link` off, a signal in one channel produces exactly zero
    gain reduction in the other.
    *Figure:* "IND (independent): No linkage between channels; channels operate independently of each
    other with **no influence** from the opposite channel" **(P)** [3].

40. **100 % gives equal gain reduction.** With `link` on and `api_link_pct` = 100, two channels at
    different levels receive gain reduction equal to ±0.1 dB.
    *Figure:* "**100% will yield equal gain on both channels**" **(P)** [3].

41. **Both detectors stay live at every link setting.** At 50 % link, muting one channel's *audio*
    still changes the other channel's gain reduction, and the change is half what a full link would
    give, ±10 %.
    *Figure:* "the control voltages are summed together, but **both channels are still detecting their
    own control voltages**", and "each channel is still controlled by its own detector, preventing
    loading and slaving from one side" **(P)** [2] [3]. This test distinguishes API's arrangement from
    the ordinary one-detector stereo link and it is the reason API wrote those sentences.

42. **The link averages decibels rather than summing power.** With one channel 20 dB above the other
    and 100 % link, the resulting gain reduction is that of the **arithmetic mean in decibels**, which
    differs from the true-RMS power sum by a computable amount.
    *Figure:* **(A)**, from 3.5's reading of API's prose. Not a hardware measurement. It is here
    because the dbx 160 does the other thing [dbx-160 10.6] and a shared implementation would silently
    pick one; this test makes the difference explicit.

43. **The meter pins.** With 30 dB of gain reduction the meter reads exactly 20 dB and does not
    rescale.
    *Figure:* test 6's published pair **(P)** [2].

44. **0 VU is +4 dBu.** With `api_meter` = `out` and a +4 dBu output, the meter reads 0 VU.
    *Figure:* "**0 VU is +4dBu**" **(P)** [2] [3].

### 13.9 The panel

45. **The faceplate's aspect ratio is 10.857 : 1.**
    *Figure:* API's own "**19" X 1.75"**" **(P)** [4]. Not the recall chart's 11.175, which is 2.9 %
    short (2.2). This is a test because I got a panel height wrong once by eyeballing a photograph
    and this repository now writes that mistake down.

46. **Every control sits where API drew it.** Each of the eight knob centres, nine button centres and
    sixteen LED centres matches the table in 2.2 to **0.002 W**.
    *Figure:* API's own recall chart, read as path coordinates **(D)** [5], independently corroborated
    by the photograph to 0.0012 W (2.2).

47. **The knob sweeps are 300° and 180°.** The four pots sweep 300° with eleven dots at 30°; the four
    seven-position switches sweep 180° with detents at 30°.
    *Figure:* measured dot angles from the recall chart **(D)** [5], listed in 2.3.

48. **The defaults are API's own.** On instantiation the model reports THRESH 0 dBu, ATTACK 1 ms,
    RATIO 4:1, RELEASE 0.5 s, KNEE MED, THRUST NORM, TYPE NEW, LINK 50 %, SHAPE SLOW, auto make-up on
    and BLEND off.
    *Figure:* API's own quick-start list **(P)** [3], plus the detent-angle table that turns "12:00"
    into values **(D)** [5]. Very few units in this lab have published defaults; this one does, and
    the test says so.

### 13.10 What I will not test, and why

- **The ratio calibration in OLD** (test 17). One printed scale, two topologies, no statement of
  which it describes.
- **Absolute knee widths in decibels** (test 21). API's plot is uncalibrated and its own ratio lines
  match none of the unit's ratios (7.2).
- **Absolute attack and release times** (test 37). No measurement procedure, no tolerance.
- **The detector's averaging time constant** (test 38). Unpublished; the model's value is a guess.
- **Distortion, of the box or of the gain cell.** API publish **no THD figure at all** (10.2), the
  2180 grade API fitted is unknown, and the three grades differ by a factor of six (4.2). Asserting
  THAT's A-grade number against a model of an API compressor would be asserting a part choice nobody
  published. `api_drive` defaults to zero and is untested.
- **Noise.** "Signal to Noise Ratio: 120 dB" has no reference level, no bandwidth and no weighting
  (10.3), and a floating-point model's noise floor is a design decision. Asserting one against the
  other would be theatre.
- **The meter's ballistics or its drive constant.** Nothing is published. The SSL had a clone builder
  who gave "about 50 µA/dB" [SSL-Gbus 2.4]; there is no equivalent here.
- **The SHAPE filters' corner frequencies.** API say only high-pass, low-pass and both, on the
  control voltage [2]. No frequency appears in any document.
- **The two channels' matching.** Real 2500s disagree by up to a few decibels before trimming (5.6),
  and API put a warning label on the trim. A model can be perfectly matched or deliberately
  mismatched; neither is a published figure.
- **Anything against another vendor's emulation.** Universal Audio's schematic access and two golden
  units [15] tell us the documents exist. They are not a number to match, no null test has been
  published, and no test here compares this model to any commercial plug-in.
